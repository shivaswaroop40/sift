Cloudflare's HTTP traffic doubled from 63 million to 115 million requests per second between late 2024 and mid-2026. Daily requests from AI agents grew over 1,700 percent in one year. For the first time, more than half of internet traffic was non-human.

The shift breaks the historical business model where search engines sent visitors that clicked ads or bought subscriptions. AI answer engines now summarise content and send readers elsewhere, costing websites bandwidth without revenue. Human traffic to retail, software, and financial services sites fell as much as 40 percent in one year.

Publishers cannot manage what they cannot see or charge for. Cloudflare introduced Web Bot Auth for cryptographic verification, separate controls for search and training crawlers, and a Disallow AI Training mechanism. Licensing deals with AI companies remain bespoke and bilateral, reaching only large publishers.

The core problem is revenue per request falling while costs rise. Traditional blocking tools are too blunt for an economy where agents act on human requests and genuinely deliver value. Sites must learn to serve both audiences or lose control of how their content is used.
