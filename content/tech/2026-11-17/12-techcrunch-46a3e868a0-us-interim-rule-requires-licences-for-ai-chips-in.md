The Commerce Department is issuing an interim rule that requires licences to export, reexport or transfer AI chips and servers to data centres anywhere if they may supply compute to companies in China or other D:5 embargoed countries. The rule covers facilities or networks above 12 million total processing performance, roughly 750 Nvidia H100 or H200 chips, and also covers indirect access through brokers.

The rule adds licence requirements for advanced chip shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil, which Commerce says carry a higher-than-normal risk of diversion. Chips destined for US-based companies in those countries are exempt. Existing operators whose services fall under the rule must obtain licences within 90 days of it taking effect.

For anyone running or renting GPU capacity abroad, the compliance burden moves onto operators. Exporters and data centre operators must monitor customers' ownership structures and activities to make sure D:5 entities are not behind them. Commercial recommendation systems, such as those in e-commerce or social media, are excluded.

The rule also rescinds the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule of January 2025, which set country-level caps. The article links the timing to a scandal involving Nvidia customer Megaspeed, which the US reportedly suspects of supplying compute to Chinese firms. That link is reported, not established in the rule text.
