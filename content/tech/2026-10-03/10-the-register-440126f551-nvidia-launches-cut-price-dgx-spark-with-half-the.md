Nvidia released a 64 GB version of its DGX Spark system for $4,999, while raising the 128 GB model to $6,950—a 75 percent jump from launch. The lower-spec variant cuts memory in half but maintains the same 273 GB/s bandwidth by using lower-capacity LPDDR5x modules rather than fewer chips.

For engineers running inference on smaller models, the 64 GB tier becomes viable. Models in the 26-35 billion parameter range can handle local AI agent workloads at a significantly lower entry point. However, the cut undermines use cases like fine-tuning that demand larger capacity.

Memory supply constraints are driving both the price increases and the product fragmentation. The platform originally promised a $3,000 price point; the 64 GB model now costs 67 percent more than that initial expectation.
