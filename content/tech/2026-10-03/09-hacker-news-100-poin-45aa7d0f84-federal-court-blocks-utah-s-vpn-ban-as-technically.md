Utah's SB 73 required adult websites to block VPN users or geolocate every visitor with perfection to verify age, threatening nationwide compliance burden. A federal judge issued a preliminary injunction, finding the law violates the Commerce Clause by imposing obligations on out-of-state businesses.

The ruling matters because geolocation technology cannot reliably distinguish a VPN user in Salt Lake City from one in Seattle or Shanghai. Sites would need to verify the age of all 28 million US visitors to ensure no Utahans accessed their content unverified, making the law unenforceable as written.

Utah's proposed administrative rules demanded detection of VPN obfuscation through latency monitoring and timezone checks, methods the court acknowledged are unreliable and subject to false positives under normal network conditions.
