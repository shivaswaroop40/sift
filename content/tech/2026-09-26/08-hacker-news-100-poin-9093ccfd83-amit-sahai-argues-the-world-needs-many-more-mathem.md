Cryptographer Amit Sahai, in a guest post on Terence Tao's blog, argues that AI systems are already generating ideas that go beyond fast calculations, and the mathematics community must prepare for a future where researchers struggle to keep up with machine-produced work.

Sahai warns that some current and aspiring mathematicians may choose to leave the field, mirroring students he knew as an undergraduate who gave up because the pace felt unattainable. He frames human understanding as a collective responsibility rather than an individual career choice.

The post uses a hypothetical one-terawatt fusion reactor designed by AI to make the case that consequential decisions cannot rest on reasoning no human community understands. Sahai proposes a 'deployable intellectual reserve' of mathematically trained people who can audit and engage with AI-generated designs and proofs.

Sahai concedes that AI assistance may make individual researchers more effective, but argues biological limits on attention and depth mean society will still need far more trained mathematicians than it currently has.
