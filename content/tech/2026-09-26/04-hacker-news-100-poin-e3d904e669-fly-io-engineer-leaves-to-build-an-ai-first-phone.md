Thomas Ptacek has left Fly.io to join Kurt in building a phone designed from scratch for a world where AI generates software on-device for individual users. The argument: today's phones are scaled-down PDP-11s, architected around fixed-function applications shipped by professional developers, and that model is about to collapse. The new device is intended to build apps for the user, in English, on demand, rather than download them from app stores.

The piece is a long essay, not a product reveal. Ptacek spends most of it arguing that AI is dissolving the boundary between programmers and users, that single-audience software of one or two people will dwarf packaged applications, and that the core job of a modern OS, partitioning strangers' code from each other, loses its purpose when most software is conjured locally by the user themselves.

The concrete claim worth flagging is the prediction about software distribution. Ptacek expects developers to shift from shipping finished apps to shipping building blocks, with the rest assembled by users through natural language. He also concedes he is talking his book, as he is joining the company building the device. No technical specs, OS details, or timelines are given in this excerpt.
