Kiteworks has told customers to take file-transfer servers offline before the weekend, after receiving threat intelligence from law enforcement about an imminent attack. CISO Frank Balonis said the company has "no awareness of any compromise" and framed the advisory as preventative.

The email to customers warns of potential zero-day flaws that Kiteworks cannot confirm have been patched. The company recommends all users move to release 9.5.1, which it says addresses known vulnerabilities, but cannot rule out other access paths.

Kiteworks has not named the law enforcement agency that tipped it off nor the threat actor involved. The FBI declined to comment and CISA would not comment on the record. Researcher Kevin Beaumont counted at least a thousand internet-facing Kiteworks systems.

The advisory matters because Kiteworks claims thousands of customers across healthcare, government, automotive and other sectors. One healthcare customer told TechCrunch the shutdown is already delaying doctors' ability to contact patients, and Kiteworks has prior experience of a mass zero-day campaign against its file-transfer product when it was still called Accellion.
