Meta will enforce new commercial terms from October 30 that grant it rights to use automated scraping tools on websites owned by business customers and use that content to train AI models and improve products. The change affects roughly one million businesses using Meta's AI agents across WhatsApp, Messenger, and other services.

For platform engineers, this matters because it signals Meta's approach to data acquisition and AI training transparency. If a business runs infrastructure or services tied to Meta's ecosystem, content exposure to model training is now contractual risk to assess.

The procedural problem is whether existing customers have genuine choice to reject the terms without losing prepaid services or access. Meta's history with privacy makes legal experts reluctant to assume good faith interpretation of vague language around robots.txt compliance.
