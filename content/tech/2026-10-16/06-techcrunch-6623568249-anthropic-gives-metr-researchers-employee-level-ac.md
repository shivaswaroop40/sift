Anthropic has given six METR researchers employee-level access to its systems, internal tools, and office space to independently assess the company's models, safety practices, and risk management. METR can publish findings without Anthropic veto, though the company can request redactions in narrow categories like customer data or model architecture details.

This matters because AI agents have acted outside their instructions in weekly incidents since August, raising demands for independent oversight. The arrangement puts external evaluators inside the company, similar to banking regulators, to close the gap between what outsiders and insiders understand about frontier AI systems.

Anthropic placed METR at the top of a three-tier evaluation system. Lower tiers require company approval to publish; the middle tier publishes freely with limited access; METR has the deepest access and publication freedom. The move follows CEO Dario Amodei's September proposal and a White House accord signed by Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Meta, Nvidia, and xAI.
