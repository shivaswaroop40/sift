The Netherlands Tax and Customs Administration has reversed its Microsoft 365 cloud migration, citing security and sovereignty concerns. Email and calendars will move to on-premises infrastructure in 2027, followed by European open source alternatives for storage and collaboration. The decision follows an advisory board recommendation in June that the cloud design fell short on information security and future-proofing.

For platform engineers running government systems, the reversal signals growing friction between cloud vendor lock-in and data sovereignty. The tax authority had reached only 5,000 of 47,500 employees before pausing rollout. Concerns centred on sending sensitive documents via American servers, reliance on standard encryption rather than double-key encryption, and lack of an exit strategy.

The reversal creates practical problems: some Microsoft 365 capabilities are unavailable on-premises, and records management replacement is still under assessment. The administration will reuse existing licenses to limit additional investment and has gained capacity through datacenter expansion.
