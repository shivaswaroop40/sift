Debian's DFSG, Licensing & New Packages Team has started using LLMs to send out reviews of packages entering the project's new queue. The team is itself relatively new. The practice follows a general-resolution vote in August, in which Debian members chose the fifth of eight options, 'Responsible Use of Generative AI', after years of debate over LLM usage. The practice has sparked debate within the project.

The new queue is the entry point for new packages, and the DFSG team's reviews are part of that gate. Its remit covers licensing and new packages. Submitters may now be reading model output rather than a human reviewer's assessment. For engineers who package software for Debian, or build on it, that changes whose judgement stands behind a review. Accuracy and accountability now sit with the model and the team supervising it.

The excerpt cuts off before naming the concerns, so the specific objections to the LLM reviews are not stated here. The open question is what 'Responsible Use of Generative AI' requires of a review team in practice. The DFSG team's reviews are an early application of the policy Debian voted for.
