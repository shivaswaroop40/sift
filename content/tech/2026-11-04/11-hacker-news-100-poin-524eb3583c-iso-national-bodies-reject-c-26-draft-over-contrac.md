On 23 October 2026, after a twelve-week ballot, the ISO National Bodies disapproved the C++26 Draft International Standard. A two-thirds majority was required and was not reached. The comments from the National Bodies give one clear reason. A number of them judge contracts not ready for release. The author, a WG21 member who helps represent Germany's vote, says this is the first failed DIS ballot in C++ history as far as he can find.

The practical effect is delay, and whether the standard keeps the C++26 name is open. ISO offers four options: a revised draft for a new DIS vote, a return to the committee draft stage, publication as a Technical Specification, or cancelling the project. Each needs a majority in WG21, which already voted C++26 forward with contracts. Cancelling would revert to C++23 and move to C++29 unchanged, so features already accepted would need fresh papers and could lapse if their champions lack time.

Contracts have a long record. They entered the C++20 draft in Cologne in 2019 and were removed at the same meeting. A study group worked from 2020 to about 2024, and the Evolution Working Group then carried the feature into C++26 by majority vote, followed by WG21's March 2026 vote on the full draft. The author notes that NBs previously voted against a standard only on new information, and he does not count a new paper with old arguments as new.

The only other unapproved DIS he found in ISO records is ISO/IEC DIS 26926, the C++/CLI Language Specification dated 11 August 2006, and that project was halted. WG21 meets on 16 November to try to break the deadlock. The author sees no reason to continue work on C++29 for now and calls the outcome a massive disaster for the community.
