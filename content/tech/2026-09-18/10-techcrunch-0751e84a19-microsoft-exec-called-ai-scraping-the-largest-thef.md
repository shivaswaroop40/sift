Unredacted material from the New York Times' copyright lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft surfaces internal statements in which a senior Microsoft Applied Science director, Brent Hecht, described AI scraping as "the largest theft of labor in human history" and an "astonishing theft of unprecedented proportions." OpenAI's head of ChatGPT, Nick Turley, wrote that publishers face an "existential threat" from "largely substitutive" chatbot products. Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella testified that paywalled content should be licensed and that he would have forced OpenAI to retrain had he known about paywall scraping.

The filing also puts concrete numbers on the copying. It claims OpenAI's mid-training datasets contain more than 91,692 copies of works from the Times, Daily News and Center for Investigative Reporting, and that a Common Crawl-derived dataset includes over 2 million nytimes.com documents. A separate Project Mango dataset allegedly holds at least 160,903 unique works from the news publishers.

The claims cut against the companies' fair use defences. Microsoft internal data reportedly shows Copilot cutting click-through rates to nytimes.com by as much as 93% versus traditional Bing search. Hecht's January 2024 presentation called this a "doom loop" that would "hurt the performance of our models and the entire web at the same time." The filing alleges OpenAI staff devised hacks to bypass paywalls and deliberately stripped copyright notices so models would not reproduce them.

Much of the new information comes from the Times' own brief rather than the underlying exhibits, which remain sealed, and the quotes are presented without original context. OpenAI and Microsoft declined to comment. Earlier rulings have mostly favoured AI companies' fair use arguments, and the Trump administration recently filed a brief supporting OpenAI's position.
