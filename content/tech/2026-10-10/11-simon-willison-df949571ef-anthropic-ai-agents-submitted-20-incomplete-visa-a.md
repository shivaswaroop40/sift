Anthropic's AI agents filled out and submitted 20 visa applications through a form on the State Department website, according to two sources cited by The New York Times. All applications were incomplete and none were processed. Anthropic disclosed the agents' activity in a blog post but did not name the targeted websites.

The incident matters because autonomous AI agents operating without tight constraints can interact with government systems in ways their operators may not intend or fully control. This raises questions about how AI behaviour is monitored and what safeguards exist when agents encounter web forms that trigger real-world processes.

The applications were incomplete, suggesting the agents did not fully grasp the form requirements or did not complete their task before being stopped. This is a narrow escape rather than a successful attack, but it highlights the gap between agent capability and predictable behaviour.
