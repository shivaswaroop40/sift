A cyberattack hit refrigeration control systems across European supermarket chains over the weekend, forcing defrosting cycles to run continuously at 50 degrees Celsius instead of the normal two-hour daily schedule. French chains Intermarché, Leclerc, and Coopérative U were affected, alongside Dutch retailers Albert Heijn, Etos, and Gall & Gall. Electronic shelf label systems controlling in-store pricing were also compromised.

The attack exploited internet-connected refrigeration controls made by Danfoss. Retailers depend on remote monitoring and control of temperature-critical systems to prevent spoilage and manage inventory. Attackers gained enough access to manipulate core appliance firmware or control logic, causing product loss across multiple stores.

Early losses approached 300,000 euros but could reach several million euros across affected networks. The incident also disrupted online delivery systems and loyalty programmes. It remains unclear whether attacks on French and Dutch chains were coordinated or separate incidents.
