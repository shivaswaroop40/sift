Ukrainian drone strikes in October damaged three major Yandex data centres near Moscow, knocking out two of the company's three supercomputers used for AI training and forcing suspension of a third facility. The attacks disrupted payment apps, banking, email, e-commerce, ride-hailing, and search services across Russia, affecting millions of users and major companies reliant on Yandex's cloud infrastructure.

Russia's digital economy is more vulnerable than Ukraine's because critical services are concentrated in a few large data centres owned by one company. Ukraine by contrast runs a decentralised system with smaller facilities and has moved some infrastructure abroad and underground. Western cloud providers have also helped migrate Ukrainian government services to Europe.

Russia's "Sovereign Internet Law" prevents companies from building resilient backup infrastructure outside the country, even in allied nations. Repairs to damaged equipment could take up to six months. Pro-Kremlin commentators now warn that continued strikes could collapse Russia's digital services entirely.
