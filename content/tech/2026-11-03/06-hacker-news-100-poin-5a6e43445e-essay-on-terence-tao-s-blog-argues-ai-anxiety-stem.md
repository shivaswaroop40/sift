A guest essay on Terence Tao's blog, written by Karthik C. S., Amit Sahai and Jie Xue, argues that anxiety about AI replacing mathematicians reflects a problem that predates the technology. The field already measures intellectual life through publications, citations and awards. The authors write that mathematicians had 'already come to understand ourselves in terms suited to machines', and that AI merely exposes the consequences of that view.

The same structure applies to software engineering. If the worth of engineering work is defined by output, a system that produces more of it appears to threaten the engineer rather than the definition. The authors also reject the common response of claiming distinctively human abilities such as creativity, intuition or taste. They argue that valuing those abilities only as an advantage over machines keeps the same conception of worth in place.

The argument rests on a Platonic view of mathematics. Truths exist independently of whoever proves them, so the significance of a result lies in its relation to that order rather than in the distinction of the person expressing it. The authors separate correct derivation from understanding. An axiomatic system permits indefinitely many deductions, and the ability to produce them does not by itself supply a worthy direction of inquiry.

The proposed alternative, 'egoless mathematics', is sketched rather than fully argued in the available text. It concerns the position from which knowledge is pursued, allowing what is discovered to change the purposes of the inquiry rather than merely serve them. The piece is philosophical throughout, with no empirical claims about AI capability.
