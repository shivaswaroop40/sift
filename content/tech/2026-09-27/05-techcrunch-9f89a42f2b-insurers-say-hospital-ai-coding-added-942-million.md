An analysis by the Blue Cross Blue Shield Association found that hospitals’ use of AI to draft insurance claims led to an extra $942 million in US healthcare spending over a two-year period. The study pointed to a sharp rise in patients being documented as having complex conditions, with no matching change in the treatment delivered.

The finding matters because it suggests AI is being used to inflate coding rather than improve care, shifting cost from providers to payers and ultimately to premiums. BCBSA framed the trend as a one-sided loss for insurers, while hospitals counter that AI simply captures clinical detail humans miss.

Abridge founder Shiv Rao acknowledged the risk of a feedback loop where automated systems on each side escalate against each other, though he argued the same tools could in principle reduce friction. The report does not name specific hospitals or AI vendors, so the mechanism is inferred from aggregate claims data rather than audited clinical records.
