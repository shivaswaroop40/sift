The Investigatory Powers Tribunal has confirmed that the UK government served Apple with a Technical Capability Notice. It rejected the Home Office argument that acknowledging the notice would harm national security. The Home Office had relied on its usual "neither confirm nor deny" policy. The precise demands in the notice remain confidential.

The three judges cited extensive public reporting, statements by US officials, and media briefings they concluded the UK government had authorised. The story first broke in February 2025, when The Washington Post reported that the Home Office had demanded an iCloud backdoor the month before. The order has been widely reported as seeking access to end-to-end encrypted iCloud data.

A Technical Capability Notice is issued under the Investigatory Powers Act 2016. It compels companies to make technical changes that support lawful surveillance. For Apple, the choice was to defy a UK legal order or weaken the encryption its customers expected. The ruling covers the notice's existence only, so the substance is still untested in public.
