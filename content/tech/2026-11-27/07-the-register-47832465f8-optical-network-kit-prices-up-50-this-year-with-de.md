Network operator RETN says AI datacenter demand is pushing up optical networking equipment prices by around 50 percent since the start of 2026. Kit that once arrived in three months now takes 12 to 15, and some orders take up to 18 months. Telcos are competing directly with datacenters for chips and components.

RETN says developers building outside established hubs may need to involve carriers two to five years before opening. Fibre to remote sites is the weak point. Near cities, developers often fund fibre themselves at a few percent of project cost. Over long distances, carriers are reluctant to build a route for one site.

The report also says customers now expect four or more redundant routes to long-haul destinations, up from two. A genuinely diverse circuit might cost €1,800 a month rather than €600, according to RETN. Redundancy only helps if routes avoid shared ducts or cable paths.

The source is a report from a carrier, so the figures reflect its own view of the market. RETN also concedes that many carriers delayed upgrades to save money, which made the shortage harder to absorb.
