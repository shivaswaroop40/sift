Birmingham City Council faces a bill of £128.1 million for moving from SAP to Oracle. That combines the original 2022 implementation and this year's reimplementation. The 2019 business case forecast £19.96 million. A report to the Audit Committee said funding allocation had previously reached £157 million. The new Oracle Fusion system went live in August with no priority-one issues.

The first go-live in 2022 was a year late. The council had planned to run Oracle out-of-the-box but built several customisations, including a bank reconciliation system that did not work. It could not understand its cash position or produce auditable accounts. It has spent over £5 million on manual workarounds. The failure contributed to the council's effective bankruptcy.

Officials reportedly ignored a red warning before go-live that said late payments, failed debt collection and incorrect accounting were likely. The true cost is probably higher than £128.1 million. School implementation was in the original budget but excluded from the rerun. The 2019 case also booked £27 million in savings to 2031. A 2024 Audit Reform Lab analysis put total impact at £216.5 million by April 2026.
