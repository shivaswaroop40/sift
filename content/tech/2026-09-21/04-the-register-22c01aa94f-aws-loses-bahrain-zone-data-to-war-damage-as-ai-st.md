AWS said war damage to its Middle East infrastructure permanently destroyed resources and data hosted only in its Bahrain Availability Zones. Customers with no copies elsewhere lost their data. The damage overwhelmed the resilience built into the region.

The episode matters because cloud redundancy inside a region is not the same as an independent backup elsewhere. Treat resilience as someone else's responsibility and you carry the full loss when physical infrastructure fails.

The UK air traffic control outage in September added another data point. NATS reportedly told airports no backup was available because it was undergoing overhaul, and that the live data was not copied because of the volume involved. Backups are expensive, hard to test, and easy for management to deprioritise when nothing is visibly broken.

The AI buildout is making the storage problem worse. Western Digital had allocated its entire 2026 hard drive production by mid February. Provisioning three copies on two media with one off site, the 3-2-1 rule, sounds neat until you have to buy the drives.
