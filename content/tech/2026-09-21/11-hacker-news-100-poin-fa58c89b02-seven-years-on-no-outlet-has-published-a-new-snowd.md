The last document from the Snowden archive was published on 29 May 2019 by The Intercept, which closed its archive earlier that March. The Guardian stopped in February 2014, Der Spiegel in January 2015, and The New York Times and ProPublica in August 2015. Since 2019, no news outlet, journalist or institution has released a single new document from the archive.

Laura Poitras and Glenn Greenwald each say they still hold full copies and that the material still matters. Greenwald said in 2023 that the archive runs to hundreds of thousands of documents. Poitras has described a vast amount of unreported information of contemporary and historical significance. Neither has published anything in seven years, and neither has explained the silence.

The piece sets out how the archive was originally distributed. Snowden shared an encrypted archive called Pandora with about 50,000 documents with Poitras and Gellman in May 2013. He warned Poitras about a single point of failure, and copies were handed to Trevor Timm at the Freedom of the Press Foundation, to two unnamed people, and to Ewen MacAskill at the Guardian.

It also documents the pressure that shaped British coverage. A D notice was issued to UK outlets the day after the first Guardian story, and academic coding found most British press coverage between 2013 and 2015 was pro-surveillance. The article is cut off before reaching its account of what happened to The Intercept's copy.
