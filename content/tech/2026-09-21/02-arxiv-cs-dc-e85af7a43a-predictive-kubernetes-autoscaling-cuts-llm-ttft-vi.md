The paper decomposes predictive autoscaling for LLM serving on Kubernetes into four factors: token-aware demand tracking, startup-delay lookahead, a bounded uncertainty margin, and plant-state observation. It evaluates each in isolation on production-derived heavy-tailed workloads generated by ServeGen.

A simple EWMA predictor with delay-aware lookahead and an upper confidence bound margin reduces TTFT SLO violations from 53% under reactive QPS-based scaling to 0.5% across five random seeds. Lookahead alone contributes a 14x reduction and is the single largest factor. Kalman filter variants did not improve the cost-SLO tradeoff.

A real Kubernetes test with Qwen2.5-7B on A100 GPUs using vLLM confirmed the mechanism: a delay-aware lookahead controller cut TTFT violations from 63.5% to 3.7% relative to reactive KEDA scaling. The authors also show that token granularity matters because context length, via KV cache pressure, degrades TTFT far more than request rate at matched throughput.

The paper distinguishes findings specific to Kubernetes actuation from those general to LLM serving, which is useful for teams choosing where to invest effort. Claims rest on one model, one hardware type, and a synthetic generator plus a single cluster validation, so the absolute numbers are indicative rather than universal.
