A Mandiant analyst had been embedded in hacker group TeamPCP since March, watching the group's supply-chain attacks from inside a 12-person channel called CanisterWorm, Google Threat Intelligence Group researcher Austin Larsen said at LABScon. TeamPCP compromised open-source tools including Trivy, LiteLLM, TanStack and Mistral AI, deployed a worm called Mini Shai-Hulud, and ultimately breached over a thousand organisations including GitHub, OpenAI and the European Commission.

Google used the inside view to contact providers such as AWS and Microsoft and have stolen credentials revoked, then notified victims directly. The intelligence also surfaced an AI-developed zero-day in login software that bypassed two-factor authentication. Google tested the exploit, confirmed it worked, and passed details to the vendor for patching.

Two alleged Australian members, Ruben Ian Thomson and Louis Michael Gaeb Gaebler, were arrested last month with FBI assistance. Google also received information from cybercriminal group ShinyHunters, which had partnered with TeamPCP and later turned on them. The story of the mole itself is thin on technical detail beyond access to chats and a credential server.
