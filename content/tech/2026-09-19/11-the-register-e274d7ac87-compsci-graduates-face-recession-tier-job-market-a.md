A working paper from three US Census Bureau economists finds that graduates from the most AI-exposed college majors have seen employment and earnings drop sharply since ChatGPT launched in late 2022. The top decile of AI-exposed majors saw a five percentage point drop in initial employment and a 13 percent decline in full-quarter initial earnings, on par with graduating into a large recession.

For readers hiring or planning careers, the mechanism matters. About half of the earnings decline comes from graduates shifting into lower-paying sectors such as retail and food service rather than staying in their field. Other contributors include more self-employment and grad school, which together can explain up to half of the employment drop.

The effects are concentrated in CS, maths, statistics, some engineering, networking and journalism. Less AI-exposed fields like nursing and many education disciplines have not seen the same deterioration. Job-finding rates for affected graduates have fallen faster than graduate numbers, and enrolment in the most exposed programmes is starting to decline, which argues against a simple oversupply story.

The economists stress the finding is short-term. They note firms may be hiring fewer new graduates for reasons tied to AI uncertainty rather than direct AI use, and they caution against extrapolating to a long-term doomsday forecast. The damage does tend to fade with time since graduation, though prior recession cohorts show earnings can stay depressed for years.
