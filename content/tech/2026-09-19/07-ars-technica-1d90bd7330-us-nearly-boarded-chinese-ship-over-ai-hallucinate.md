A US Special Operations Command analyst used a chatbot to assess the manifest of a Chinese ship transiting the Middle East, and the model produced an intelligence report claiming the vessel was carrying nuclear arms programme components. The US military prepared to intercept and board the ship with air support before officials found the chatbot had misidentified the cargo. One source told CNN the incident "almost started a war."

For practitioners, the episode is a stark illustration of what happens when generative AI is used to fuse open-source material with classified signals intelligence without sufficient guardrails. It also exposes a cultural gap: the Department of Defense has shipped AI tools to 1.5 million active personnel and built GenAI.mil on top of Gemini and Grok, while still relying on analysts to verify outputs that LLMs cannot guarantee.

The State Department's 2023 declaration called for a human in the loop and accountable chains of command for military AI use. The reported near miss suggests those principles did not prevent a hallucinated output from reaching the planning stage of a kinetic operation against a nuclear-armed rival.
