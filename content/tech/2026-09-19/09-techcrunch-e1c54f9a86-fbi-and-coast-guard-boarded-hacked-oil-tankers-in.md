US Coast Guard and FBI personnel boarded two US-bound oil tankers in the Gulf of Mexico between 21 and 24 August after detecting network compromises on both vessels. The agencies moved to verify the integrity of operational and information technology systems, including navigation, propulsion and cargo controls. The captain, crew and onshore owner staff cooperated with responders, and the joint statement reported no operational disruptions, vessel instability, physical danger or environmental impact.

One of the vessels is the 333-metre VL Prosperity, capable of carrying over 2 million barrels of oil. Citing Iranian media, CBS reported the ship was compromised on 7 August while travelling from Egypt to the United States, with attackers interfering with speed and fuel systems and communications being lost for more than a day. The tanker remained in the Gulf of Mexico at the time of reporting.

The attackers have not been publicly named. The US is examining that possibility of Iranian involvement, in the context of recent destructive hacks attributed to Iran-linked groups against US targets including medical device maker Stryker, the Los Angeles transit system and over a hundred water utilities. CISA has previously characterised such activity as opportunistic.

Tanker networks sprawl across navigation, cargo management and vessel operations, giving attackers a wide attack surface. The incident shows that compromise of bridge and engine systems is now treated as a boarding-worthy event, not just an IT problem.
