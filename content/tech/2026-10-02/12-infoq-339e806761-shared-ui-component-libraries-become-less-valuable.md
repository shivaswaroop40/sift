Generative AI can now produce styled, accessible components on demand, undermining the economic case for maintaining a canonical shared library. The real maintenance burden kicks in after initial release, when supporting multiple consuming teams becomes complex.

Consistency and design tokens still need centralizing, but the shipped component code no longer justifies its upkeep cost. Versioning, dependency management, and coordinating changes across teams creates friction that regeneration avoids.

Accessibility, genuinely complex widgets, and cross-team functional consistency remain valid reasons to curate shared code. But for standard components, paying a dedicated team to maintain them is harder to justify than generating them per-product.
