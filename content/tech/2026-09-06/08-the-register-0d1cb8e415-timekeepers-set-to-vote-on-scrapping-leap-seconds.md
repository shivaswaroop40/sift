The General Conference on Weights and Measures is set to vote in October on a draft resolution that would end leap second adjustments and make UTC continuous from 20 May 2027. Under the proposal, the gap between UTC and UT1, a measure of Earth rotation time, would be permitted to grow to as much as one hour. Since leap seconds were introduced in 1972, 27 have been added.

The push comes from a recent increase in Earth's rotational speed, which has experts estimating a 30 percent chance of a negative leap second being required by 2035. Setnam Shemar of the UK's National Physical Laboratory warned that most digital systems and time servers are hardcoded on the assumption that leap seconds are strictly additive. A negative second could disrupt telecoms, electricity networks and satellite navigation.

Under the proposal, UTC would not diverge from rotational time by a full hour for at least a thousand years. Shemar said the resolution is likely to pass, given widespread concern among national delegates about the risks to critical infrastructure.
