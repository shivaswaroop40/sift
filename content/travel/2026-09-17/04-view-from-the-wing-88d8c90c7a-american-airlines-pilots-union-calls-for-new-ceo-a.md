The Allied Pilots Association (APA) president Captain Nick Silva has publicly criticised American Airlines CEO Robert Isom, questioning whether someone else could turn the carrier around faster even as management shifts towards revenue-focused investment that pilots had asked for.

American lags United and Delta on financial performance and is a high-cost carrier that needs a revenue premium to be profitable. Investment in product upgrades only began in earnest 18 to 21 months ago, so benefits will take years to show in margins, a timeline sensitive to oil prices, interest rates and the wider economy.

The criticism coincides with the early start of contract negotiations. The union's opening demand includes a board seat for pilots, mirroring arrangements at Delta and United, and it is positioning itself as the solution to American's financial underperformance rather than a beneficiary of it.

The APA is also dealing with internal politics. A proposed merger with the larger Air Line Pilots Association was paused after the committee said in February there was no path forward, though the board directed it to re-engage in May, and a June rule change lowers the threshold for approving any deal to a majority of votes cast.
