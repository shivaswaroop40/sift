The European Commission approved €430 million ($494 million) in free ETS carbon allowances to 130 airlines for sustainable aviation fuel use in 2025, up from around €100 million in 2024. The permits covered over 530,000 tonnes of SAF and avoided roughly 1.7 million tonnes of CO2, according to Commission figures released Wednesday.

The support comes from a €1.5 billion pot designed to push SAF adoption and meet mandated blending targets. SAF still costs between three and ten times more than fossil kerosene, so the allowances help close the price gap while the supply scales up. The Commission has proposed expanding the scheme as part of wider ETS reform.

For travellers, the scheme matters because airlines are being paid to use greener fuel on European flights, but the underlying mandate is what forces uptake regardless of airline preference.
