American Airlines CEO Robert Isom told the Morgan Stanley Laguna Conference that the airline has been selling too much Basic Economy, with 30% of seats producing half of its revenue. He said buy-ups have improved by five percentage points since the airline started making Basic Economy less rewarding for frequent flyers.

Basic Economy tickets bought from 17 December 2025 no longer earn AAdvantage miles or Loyalty Points, and from 18 May 2026 they lose elite seating benefits and upgrades. American is making its cheapest fare genuinely worse so customers move up the cabin rather than picking the bottom fare by default.

American plans to grow premium seating on narrowbody departures from about 25% today to roughly 40%, adding first class rows on A319s, A320s, 737 MAX 10s and A321neos. Most narrowbody aircraft are also being retrofitted with more Main Cabin Extra, giving passengers a cheaper upgrade than first class.

The source notes risks remain: seat-back entertainment and Starlink wifi will take years to roll out, and an economic downturn could cut short the investment. American sees a clear payoff though, including an incremental $1.5 billion in pretax profit from its Citi co-brand card alone.
