Ryanair collected €4.99 billion ($5.8 billion) in ancillary revenue in FY2026, equivalent to about €24 ($27) per passenger. The figure is up 6% year-on-year, while passenger traffic grew 4% to 208.4 million. For many travellers, this extra revenue is close to the basic fare they paid for the flight.

The result shows that Ryanair's low-cost model depends on selling services after the initial booking. A Basic fare includes only a small personal bag. Baggage, seat selection, priority boarding, food and other products are charged separately. Because the airline carried 208.4 million passengers, every extra €1 per passenger would add about €208 million ($242 million) in annual revenue.

The €24 figure is an average, not a standard charge. Some passengers pay nothing beyond the fare; others might spend about €50 on a checked bag, seat, priority boarding and onboard food. A 20 kg checked bag bought online costs between €21.49 and €59.99 per flight, depending on route and date. Airport check-in can cost €55.
