United Airlines has accumulated hundreds of custom lie-flat business class seats intended for its 167 Boeing 737 MAX 10s on order. The aircraft's repeated certification delays have left the seats with nowhere to go. Chief executive Scott Kirby said: 'We've got a bunch of lie-flat seats that we don't know what to do with. They don't fit on other airplanes.'

Seats are not generic furniture. They are designed around a particular fuselage, cabin width, installation arrangement and certification basis, so the MAX 10 seats cannot be installed on an Airbus A321neo. United has already built a premium-heavy A321neo subfleet, the Coastliner, with 20 Polaris lie-flat suites, 12 Premium Plus seats and 129 economy seats, for transcontinental routes. That means the commercial problem the MAX 10 was meant to solve has been solved with a different aircraft, leaving the earlier hardware stranded.

It is unclear whether all the seats have been delivered and stored or whether some are completed designs and orders still in the supplier chain. If stored, they may need inspection, maintenance, recertification or updates to electronic systems. If not yet manufactured, they could require redesign. United has not disclosed how many seats are built, what it paid, or how much of the order remains outstanding.
