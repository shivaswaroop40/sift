Azriel Kaplan redeemed 32,000 Flying Blue miles and paid $248.76 on KLM's website for a Delta flight from Athens to New York JFK. The booking screen said payment was successful and the booking was confirmed, and KLM emailed a confirmation. But the ticket was never issued because of a technical problem. At the airport, Delta had no record of a ticket, and Kaplan paid $1,567.30 for a replacement.

KLM refunded the miles and taxes and offered a $500 voucher, but refuses to cover the replacement fare. It argues that no contract is formed before ticket issuance, citing its own contract language. The quoted provision actually says a ticket evidences the contract, subject to proof to the contrary. Taking payment and confirming a booking is strong proof. The Montreal Convention also says documentation failures do not invalidate a contract of carriage.

Kaplan has filed a complaint with the US Department of Transportation. KLM had earlier offered to reimburse the replacement ticket as goodwill, asked for proof of purchase, then stopped responding when he requested interest and legal fees. The airline now says it owes nothing. The claim for the actual replacement cost looks strong, though the DOT may not sympathise with the request for fees.
