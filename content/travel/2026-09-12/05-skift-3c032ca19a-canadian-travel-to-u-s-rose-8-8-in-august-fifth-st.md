Canadian residents made 8.8% more return trips from the U.S. in August than a year earlier, according to Statistics Canada. It was the fifth consecutive month of year-over-year growth. Automobile trips rose 9.9% and air trips rose 3.6%. The figures measure return trips by Canadian residents, not visitor spending or border crossings by other nationalities.

The growth matters because U.S. travel executives had feared the trade war would cool a budding rebound in Canadian arrivals. The August data suggests the national recovery is continuing, but it remains far below pre-trade-war levels. Automobile return trips were 27.4% lower than in August 2024, and air return trips were 22.7% lower.

Statistics Canada has cautioned that recent gains partly reflect a base-year effect. Because visits dropped so sharply in 2025, current increases are bouncing off a low base. Skift notes that not every state is seeing the same rebound, and raises questions about why state-level figures for Florida and New York show declines while the national data shows growth.
