Europe's new ETIAS travel authorisation system will not launch until at least 2027. Scammers have set up more than 150 websites offering ETIAS waivers, mimicking official EU sites and claiming urgency. No applications are being collected and no waivers are available now.

Independent travellers planning trips to continental Europe should not apply for ETIAS yet. The system applies to 30 Schengen countries and will eventually cost around $22 for adults, valid for three years. Fake sites often use misleading domain names and charge excessive fees or harvest personal data.

When ETIAS does launch, apply only through the official EU website. Commercial brokers can legally process applications but may charge inflated fees and pose identity theft risks. Check the EU's official page to confirm the system is operational before taking any action.
