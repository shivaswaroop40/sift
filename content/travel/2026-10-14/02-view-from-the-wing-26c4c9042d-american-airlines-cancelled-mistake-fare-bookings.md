American Airlines sold business class awards globally at severely discounted rates for about a month via a multi-city search glitch. Tickets cost as little as 7,000 miles to Delhi and 10,000 miles from Australia in business class. The airline discovered the error around September 20 but did not cancel bookings until late October, three times longer than its stated policy allows.

For independent travellers booking premium cabin awards, this matters because American's own contract commits to a 72-hour window from discovering a mistake to cancellation. The airline exceeded this deadline by weeks, leaving passengers who had already planned trips and incurred costs with little recourse. American offered only current prevailing rates or cancellation, sometimes unable to rebuild itineraries with partner segments that were no longer available.

Other airlines have honoured similar pricing errors. United flew business class Los Angeles to Auckland in 2007 at mistake-fare prices, and American itself honoured $450 business class tickets to China in 2015. The difference here is timing: American's own rules set a clear deadline, which the airline ignored.
