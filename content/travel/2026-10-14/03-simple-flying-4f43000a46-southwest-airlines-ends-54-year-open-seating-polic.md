On 27 January 2026, Southwest Airlines replaced its distinctive open-seating system with assigned seats and eight boarding groups. The shift followed extensive research showing 80% of existing customers and 86% of potential customers preferred assigned seating, and that open seating was the top reason customers chose competitors.

The change lets Southwest sell premium extra-legroom seats at higher fares, tying cabin hierarchy to pricing like other US carriers. The airline expects the seating model to contribute significantly to $1.8 billion in additional earnings forecast for 2025-2026, driven by investor pressure to improve financial performance.

Early implementation proved awkward. Crowded boarding areas and overhead bin congestion emerged in opening weeks, partly linked to the simultaneous end of free checked bags. Southwest later adjusted the process, keeping groups one and two in pre-boarding lines, undermining one promised benefit of the new system.
