Breeze Airways launched 19 new and returning routes between 1 and 3 July, lifting its July schedule to about 266 daily departures, some 44% more than a year earlier. Thirteen of the routes face no nonstop competitor, and roughly 68% are monopolies. Several restore links lost when Spirit Airlines ceased operations earlier in 2026. Chief executive David Neeleman believes the carrier can profit in markets generating as few as 10 passengers per day each way.

For travellers in smaller cities, this means new nonstop pairs the big carriers skip, such as Akron-Canton to Portland in Maine and Fort Lauderdale to Salisbury, near Ocean City. Fort Lauderdale alone gained several new destinations. The trade-off is frequency. A nonstop a few times a week replaces a hub connection, not daily service.

The 10-passenger figure is market demand, not load per flight. Ten travellers a day each way is about 140 one-way passengers a week. On three weekly round trips, that demand concentrates on a handful of departures. The Airbus A220-300, with 137 seats, about 3,450 nautical miles of range and lower fuel burn than older jets of similar size, keeps trip costs low enough for a small schedule. Ancillary revenue, including the Ascent premium cabin and bag fees, lifts yields.

The 10-passenger threshold is Neeleman's claim, reported by AeroXplorer. The article offers no route-level financial evidence for it. Breeze has flown only since 2021. Its expansion coincides with major carriers retreating from regional airports and retiring 50-seat jets, leaving gaps it now fills.
