Qatar Airways began flying from Doha to Bogotá and Caracas on November 4, three and a half months later than planned after delays blamed on the war in Iran. The service runs twice weekly, on Wednesdays and Sundays, on a triangular routing with a Boeing 777-200LR. It is the airline's first passenger service to Colombia and Venezuela, taking its South American network to three countries and three cities alongside São Paulo.

The route is now Qatar Airways' longest, with a maximum block time of 20 hours 35 minutes including the ground stop in Colombia. It edges out Doha to Auckland, which stops in Australia in both directions because of the same conflict. The 777-200LR carries 272 seats, QSuites in business and Starlink Wi-Fi, giving travellers a new one-stop option to both cities via Doha.

Planners should note the limits. The inaugural flew Doha, Caracas, Bogotá, Doha, and the routing reverses from the second service to avoid a nonstop departure from high-elevation Bogotá, where takeoff performance restricts payload. The Bogotá to Caracas leg has no traffic rights, so it cannot be booked on its own. The market is thin. OAG data shows about 137,000 round-trip passengers a year between the two cities and the regions served via Doha, and a source said the Qatari government ordered the route.
