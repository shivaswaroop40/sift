The Red Tower of Death, Rudá věž smrti, stands in Karlovy Vary, Czechia. It was the central dry-sorting and crushing plant for uranium ore, surrounded by forced labour camp "L" (Vykmanov II), widely considered one of the most dangerous camps in the Jáchymov system. Between 1948 and 1961, more than 60,000 prisoners passed through the Czechoslovak uranium labour camps. Hundreds died on site, and many more succumbed to radiation exposure after release.

The tower now serves as a memorial to the victims. Travellers who want places with a story can visit a surviving structure from the Jáchymov camp system in the Karlovy Vary area, and see the plant where uranium ore was sorted and crushed.

The main cause of death was not external radiation. Prisoners inhaled concentrated radioactive uranium dust and radon gas without protective equipment, which led to fatal silicosis and lung cancer. The name refers to the exposed red brick, and symbolically to the blood of the political prisoners who perished there under the communist regime.
