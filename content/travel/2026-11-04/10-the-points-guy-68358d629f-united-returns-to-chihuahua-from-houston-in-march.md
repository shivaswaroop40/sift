United Airlines will fly from Houston's George Bush Intercontinental to Chihuahua Airport in northern Mexico from March 28, 2027. The carrier last served the route in 2020, according to Cirium schedule data. It will use a Bombardier CRJ-550, a conversion of the 70-seat CRJ-700 fitted with 10 first-class, 20 extra-legroom economy and 20 economy seats. United will join American Airlines among U.S. carriers at the airport.

The route gives travellers a Star Alliance option into northern Mexico via United's Houston hub. Chihuahua's tourism secretary says it reflects growing interest among North American travellers in the state's attractions. The CRJ-550 carries only 50 passengers, so seats will be limited, though 10 of them are first class.

The timing carries risk. United chief executive Scott Kirby has warned the airline has "no hesitation to cut capacity of flights that are losing money" in 2027 if fuel prices stay elevated. Jet fuel averaged $4.56 a gallon globally in the week ending Oct. 30, up 3.8% on the previous month, according to IATA.

United is otherwise growing in 2027. It plans at least five new routes from San Francisco, including Charleston and Richmond, plus Chicago O'Hare to Bermuda. A 10-route summer long-haul expansion adds Luxembourg and Ljubljana. Tulum, Mexico, loses its United service in January.
