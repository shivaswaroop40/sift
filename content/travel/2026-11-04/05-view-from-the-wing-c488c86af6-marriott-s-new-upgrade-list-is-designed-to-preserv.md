Marriott made $794 million in the third quarter, up from $728 million a year ago. Revenue per available room rose 6.5%. Adjusted earnings per share rose from $2.47 to $3.02, beating the $2.84 analysts expected. On the earnings call, CEO Anthony Capuano said the new elite upgrade list, now in beta, would show members their place in line while still preserving premium room upsell opportunities for owners.

The list shows a member's position but does not commit a room. A hotel can keep selling premium rooms while guests wait for complimentary upgrades, and the later it commits a room, the longer it has to find a paying customer. Members lower on the list, seeing they are unlikely to get a free upgrade, become more likely to buy the upsell. Marriott has not explained the beta's inventory or clearance rules.

Credit cards drove much of the fee growth. The quarterly filing shows $68 million in additional card fees against the same quarter last year, and 52% of the $131 million increase in franchise fees came from cards. Fifty-one owners representing about 1,000 hotels have demanded better compensation for award stays. Marriott is appeasing them with lower Bonvoy fees, higher reimbursement on high-demand award nights, more flexible renovation rules and a guest recommendation incentive.

Capuano also cited 309 million Bonvoy members. That figure counts guests who signed up for a lower member rate, and Marriott does not share active member numbers. Hotel incentives for enrollment run as high as $10,000 a month. View from the Wing also reports layoffs in the customer service teams guests use when hotels refuse to honour benefits.
