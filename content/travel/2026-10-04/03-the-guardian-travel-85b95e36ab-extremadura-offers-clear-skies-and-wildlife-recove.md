Extremadura and Castile and León, the sparsely populated interior regions of Spain, are developing astrotourism under an initiative called Buenas Noches. The programme maps routes between stargazing sites with minimal light pollution, including Corral de los Lobos, an ancient stone enclosure built to trap wolves.

For independent travellers, the region offers unpolluted night skies, lower prices than coastal resorts, and access to places with genuine history. The landscape has recovered wildlife populations, including Iberian lynx and wolves, which represent successful conservation efforts.

Recent wildfires burned nearly 20,000 hectares of cherry orchards in 2025, threatening future harvests and the few events that draw visitors. The region has lost thousands of residents in recent years, leaving many towns quiet but with character intact.
