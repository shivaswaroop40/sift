The TSA plans to install new screening lanes at seven airports within six weeks that use facial recognition gates, body scanners, and automated bag screening. The agency expects to double passenger throughput and may allow PreCheck members to bring additional items like water bottles through checkpoints with suitable scanners.

For frequent flyers, the change means potentially faster identity verification through automated gates, though error handling for passengers who fail facial recognition could create delays. The move adds body scanners to PreCheck screening, which has traditionally used metal detectors.

The equipment costs $4 million per airport with a projected three-year payback. The TSA administrator wants to recruit 20 million additional PreCheck members over three years, with staffing cuts through attrition rather than layoffs.
