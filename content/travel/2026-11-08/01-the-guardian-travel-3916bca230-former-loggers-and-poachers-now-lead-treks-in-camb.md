The Chi Phat community ecotourism project in Cambodia's Koh Kong region runs overnight treks into the Cardamom Mountains, reached by a six-hour bus ride west of Phnom Penh. The Wildlife Alliance founded the community in 2007. Many of its guides and staff are former loggers, poachers and hunters who now earn their living protecting the forest they once exploited. One cook, Mr Crab, 60, once drove trucks hauling out the very trees he now helps guard.

For independent travellers, the project offers well-priced excursions into one of the largest unfragmented rainforest corridors in mainland south-east Asia. The range recently made global news as the home of new fauna species and a sanctuary for rare and endangered wildlife, including forest elephants and dholes. The area also holds ancient burial grounds and was the Khmer Rouge's last stronghold into the late 1980s.

Protection works on two levels. Armed government rangers enforce national park law on the ground, while animist traditions around neak ta spirits keep large areas of forest untouchable and safe for wildlife. Trek etiquette follows the beliefs. Guides say nothing negative in the forest, take nothing, and ask the gods before urinating. The writer travels at the start of the wet season, with intense humidity, sudden heavy rain and mosquitoes that can carry dengue.
