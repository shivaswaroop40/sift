American Airlines has reopened Flagship Dining at Los Angeles International Airport after a six-year closure and a refurbishment. The venue is a sit-down, a la carte restaurant inside the Flagship Lounge, serving coursed meals with table service at no charge. It operates at three hubs: LAX, Dallas Fort Worth and Miami. Access has also widened, with business-class passengers eligible since September.

Eligible business passengers include those booked in Flagship Business Plus on the Boeing 787-9 or 777-300ER, and anyone in business class on a qualifying long-haul, transcontinental or Hawaii flight marketed as Flagship. At LAX this covers certain transcontinental services, but a domestic first-class ticket will not get you in. Space is limited, so you join a waitlist for a table at the Flagship Lounge.

The wider access comes as American retires Flagship First. November 19 is the last date for long-haul first class as a stand-alone product on its 777s, which are being refitted without first class and with the highest business-class seat count the airline has offered. Transcontinental Flagship First ends in March. JFK no longer has Flagship Dining. American runs three premium lounges there with British Airways instead.
