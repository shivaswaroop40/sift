Air Canada will pull the Boeing 787 from 11 routes in the coming months, including Montreal-Paris, Montreal-Berlin, Toronto-Zurich and Toronto-Venice. Six of those services will switch to the Airbus A321XLR on some or all frequencies, while others get the 777 or A330. The XLR carries 182 seats with 14 in business and no premium economy, against 255 to 298 seats and a 21-seat premium economy cabin on the 787 variants.

On XLR flights, premium economy disappears entirely and business class seats drop from 20 or 30 to 14. That leaves fewer premium seats to buy or upgrade into on those routes. The XLR is also a narrowbody, so the transatlantic experience differs from the widebody it replaces. Travellers who value cabin space may want to check the aircraft before booking.

The A321XLR is now the cheapest long-range aircraft in the fleet to operate, and Air Canada is targeting the 787's weakest Europe routes, where loads are light or fares weak. Cutting supply can lift yields, and a smaller aircraft could allow added frequency if demand supports it. Air Canada operates five XLRs, with 25 more due, and plans new XLR destinations including Basel, Oslo, Shannon and Seville.

The freed 787s move to longer routes where their range and fuel efficiency count. New services include Vancouver to Sapporo, Vancouver to Guangzhou and Montreal to Dubrovnik, with 787s also placed on Toronto to Barcelona, Prague and Stockholm. All except Barcelona are too long for the A321XLR.
