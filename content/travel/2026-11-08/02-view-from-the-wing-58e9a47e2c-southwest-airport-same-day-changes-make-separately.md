Southwest's free same-day flight change carries a trap for travellers who paid for seats separately. A change made at the airport makes the original seat purchase nonrefundable. Changes made on the website, in the app or by phone keep the payment if the replacement seat costs the same or less. If it costs more, you pay the full new price and, with limited exceptions, the old purchase is refunded to the original payment method.

The same replacement flight can be changed at the counter or in the app, with different effects on your seat payment. A traveller standing in the terminal forfeits that money by asking an agent. Self-service is the cheaper route whenever a separately purchased seat is involved.

Free confirmed same-day changes need an available seat, the same origin and destination airports, and the same travel date, requested at least 10 minutes before the original departure. Choice, Choice Preferred and Choice Extra fares qualify, as do Getaways by Southwest bookings. Basic economy does not, even for A-List members, and partner itineraries are excluded. Standby differs. Paid seats become nonrefundable when standby clears, even through the app. Elite members on Basic economy can list for standby, with 30 minutes' notice through the app or 10 minutes in person.

Seat credits have a separate limit. A traveller who paid about $230 for seats, then cancelled and rebooked after a medical emergency, found the credit usable only for airfare. On voluntary cancellation, the seat payment becomes airfare-only credit, for travel completed within 12 months of the original seat purchase. Exchanging two paid connecting seats for a costlier nonstop triggers only one automatic refund. The second must be requested.
