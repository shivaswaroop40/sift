A guest booked two nights with points at a Hyatt in Palm Springs, arrived in the early hours after the scheduled arrival date, and stayed both nights. The hotel charged an extra $50 no-show fee and kept the points for both nights. A month of calls to the hotel and Hyatt has produced holds and disconnections but no answer. The guest is not disputing the points, only the fee.

Hyatt's agent said the first night's points were forfeited for a no-show and a cash fee still applied. That mixes up two rules. A no-show should mean a cash penalty with the points returned. Forfeiting points is the rule for guests who check in and leave early. The $50 fits neither rule and may be an internal award reimbursement amount charged to the guest by mistake.

The wider risk is the penalty structure. A late cancellation or no-show on an award stay returns the points and leaves a cash bill at the hotel's standard rate when the charge is made. The amount is not disclosed at booking, and the author argues guests cannot meaningfully agree to liability they were never quoted. Marriott, Hilton and IHG have similar terms. Documented cases include an $11,212.50 charge in Vail, later refunded, $5,500 after a bereavement and $3,690 at Thompson Central Park.

The mechanism is that the loyalty programme pays the hotel cash for award nights and will not reimburse stays that never happened, partly because hotels could redeem no-shows on busy nights to claim higher payments. Guests arriving in the middle of the night should tell the hotel they are still coming, so the overnight audit does not mark them as a no-show.
