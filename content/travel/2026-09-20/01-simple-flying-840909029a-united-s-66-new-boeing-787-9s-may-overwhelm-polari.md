United has 66 Boeing 787-9s on order featuring a Polaris 2.0 layout with 64 business-class seats, including 8 Polaris Studio suites, compared with 48 on existing 787-9s. The new configuration entered service in the second quarter of 2026 on routes from San Francisco to Singapore and London.

Each new aircraft adds 16 premium seats, a 33% increase per frame, so lounge demand will rise faster than terminal space can be added. United has confirmed it is evaluating Polaris lounge expansion in two markets, with Los Angeles and Chicago identified by industry reporting as the leading candidates, though neither has been publicly confirmed.

Los Angeles faces the sharpest pressure. Four United 787 flights to Sydney, Melbourne, Hong Kong and Beijing can depart within roughly an hour. With 64 business-class seats per aircraft, that window could see up to 256 premium passengers, 64 more than the previous configuration.

Chicago already received a 50% Polaris lounge expansion in April 2025, reaching nearly 25,000 square feet and 350-plus seats. The article notes peak-period crowding depends on how many eligible passengers arrive during a single departure bank, not on total seat count.
