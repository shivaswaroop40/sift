Spotnana executive chairman and CEO Steve Singh will argue at Skift Global Forum in New York on September 22 to 24, 2026 that the travel industry undervalues what happens after a booking. He says AI agents at Spotnana already handle cancelled segments, unticketed flights and refunds, and believes servicing costs can fall by 50% or more while improving personalisation.

For independent travellers the change matters because it shapes who picks up the phone when something goes wrong. If agents move from queues to higher-value service, response quality on rebooking or refunds may rise, though the risk is that headcount quietly shrinks and service degrades for anything outside the routine.

Singh also targets a 95% curation standard for conversational AI booking, aiming to surface the options a traveller would have chosen from a full list. The unresolved question is who audits that number and what happens to travellers in the remaining 5%, which is relevant when an algorithm decides what to show.

The 50% figure comes from Spotnana's own data on managed-travel clients. It is a vendor claim, not an industry benchmark, so the real test is whether the savings hold across different platforms and traveller types.
