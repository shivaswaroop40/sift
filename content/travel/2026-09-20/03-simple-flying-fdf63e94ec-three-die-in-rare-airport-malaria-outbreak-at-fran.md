Three people have died in a rare outbreak of airport malaria linked to Frankfurt Airport (FRA), Germany's largest airport and a hub for Lufthansa, Condor and AeroLogic. Six airport employees have contracted the disease since July, two of whom died, along with a resident of the nearby Schwanheim neighbourhood who had not been to a malaria-endemic region.

All cases were caused by Plasmodium falciparum, the parasite behind the most dangerous form of the disease, malaria tropica. It is not contagious between people and instead spreads when an infected mosquito hitches a ride on an aircraft or in luggage and escapes into the local area, biting people who never left Germany.

Mosquitoes caught in traps at the terminals have been sent to a laboratory in Belgium for testing, and Fraport has placed additional traps to contain the outbreak. Authorities have not yet said where the flights carrying the mosquito originated, and it remains unclear how the insect reached the airport in the first place.

Travellers concerned about insects in their luggage after a flight are advised to isolate bags outdoors, wash all contents on high heat for at least 30 minutes and vacuum seams thoroughly, according to pest control guidance.
