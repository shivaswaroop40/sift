A Tennessee family says Delta left their father’s body unrefrigerated overnight in Memphis after a missed connection. Marvin “Ed” Morgan died in Florida and was being shipped home through Eagle Wings Air. His body arrived after 8 p.m. but cargo pickup at the Memphis facility closed at 7 p.m., so the receiving funeral home could not collect him until the next morning. By then, the family says, he had decomposed too much for a final viewing.

The connection was missed by two minutes, with the first flight from West Palm Beach arriving 31 minutes short of Delta’s two-hour transfer window for human remains. Delta’s Memphis station page itself says it does not accept inbound human remains after 7 p.m. or without overnight holding. The family says Delta knew the body was unembalmed, which Delta’s policy permits, but had no plan for what to do once the cargo office closed.

The station’s published notice also states a cooler is available, which the family’s account contradicts. Eagle Wings has refunded the family and apologised. Delta has offered condolences and said it was investigating. Morgan was buried beside his wife, and his daughter arranged one final trip down the dragstrip at Gleason Raceway, where he had been the announcer.

The piece also recalls two earlier Delta mishandling cases, a 2017 misrouting of remains from Raleigh to Salt Lake City during a storm meltdown, and a 2013 mix-up in which a Virginia family received the wrong casket from LaGuardia.
