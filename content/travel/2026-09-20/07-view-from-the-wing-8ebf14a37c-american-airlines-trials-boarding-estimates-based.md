American Airlines is piloting a change at Charlotte Douglas that estimates boarding time using the inbound aircraft's expected arrival, rather than leaving the flight showing on time when the plane has not yet landed. The change is aimed at the so-called rolling delays, where flights stay marked on time while the inbound aircraft is still en route or has only just departed.

This matters to travellers who have spent hours waiting at a gate for a plane that was never going to make the scheduled departure. Under the current system, American has posted incremental short delays rather than reflecting the true arrival time, forcing passengers to consult FlightAware or FlightRadar24 themselves to judge when to head to the gate.

The pilot is limited to Charlotte for now, with no announced timeline for other hubs. Aviation analyst JonNYC reported the change on September 19, citing the airline directly.

Delays still update for genuine reasons such as repairs, crew changes or air traffic restrictions, so real-time estimates will shift. The change only addresses cases where the inbound aircraft is clearly not going to arrive on schedule, not all delay causes.
