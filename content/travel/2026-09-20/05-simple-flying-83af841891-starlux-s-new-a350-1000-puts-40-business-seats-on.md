Taiwan's Starlux is configuring its incoming Airbus A350-1000s with four first, 40 business, 36 premium economy and 270 economy seats, giving roughly 23% premium seating, one of the highest ratios among Asian carriers. The airline took its first A350-1000 in December 2025 and had four in service by August 2026, with 18 planned in total.

The large business cabin matters because Starlux is positioning itself as a luxury alternative to EVA Air and China Airlines on long-haul routes from Taipei to North America and Europe. The 8,400 nautical mile range opens nonstop flights to East Coast US cities, a market EVA Air and China Airlines cannot yet match with the A350-1000.

The four first-class suites are kept for branding rather than volume, making Starlux the only Taiwanese carrier offering a first-class fare. By 2033 Starlux plans a 76-aircraft fleet, close to the 89-strong EVA Air and 85-strong China Airlines.

The source is a feature explaining the seat choice; there are no fare or schedule details for specific US routes, as Starlux has not yet announced them.
