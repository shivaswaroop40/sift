Boeing now holds roughly 30 completed 777-9 airframes built years before the aircraft's certified configuration was finalised. First delivery has slipped from 2020 to 2027, and each stored jet must be brought up to a common standard through what Boeing calls change incorporation, covering software, wiring, and structural work.

For travellers, the delay matters because the 777-9 is a key replacement for ageing 777s and A380s on long-haul routes. Emirates, which has 270 on order, has said it will not accept the first ten aircraft, pushing those jets back into Boeing's already strained inventory and likely delaying new business-class cabins and seat products tied to the type.

The rework problem is illustrated by WH007, the seventh 777-9 built in 2019, which Boeing dismantled in late 2025 after deciding modification was no longer economic. Boeing has set up a dedicated team inside Commercial Airplanes to handle the stored fleet, but CEO Kelly Ortberg says the process will take several years.
