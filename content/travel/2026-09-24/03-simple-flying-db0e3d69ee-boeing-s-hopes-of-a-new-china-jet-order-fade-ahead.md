Boeing is now unlikely to secure a new mega-order from China at this week's Trump-Xi summit, with sources saying the prospect is fading. Earlier expectations of up to 500, or even 750, aircraft have given way to hopes of simply finalising the 200-jet commitment announced in May.

That earlier deal is itself in doubt due to a dispute over engines, spare parts and maintenance support. US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said around 140 of the orders are in a good state, with a further ten being finalised, and analysts suggest the deal could shrink to 150 aircraft or fewer.

Boeing has struggled in China for nearly a decade while Airbus has gained share, and its last major Chinese order was the 300-aircraft deal worth over $37 billion in 2017. China is forecast to need around 9,000 new jets by 2045, making the market critical to Boeing's long-term position.
