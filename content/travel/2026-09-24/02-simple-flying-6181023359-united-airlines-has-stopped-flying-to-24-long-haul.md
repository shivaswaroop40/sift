United Airlines has stopped flying to 24 long-haul airports since 2006, according to Cirium Diio data. The list includes 15 European airports, five in Africa and Asia, and four in the Middle East. Most dropped routes were served from Newark Liberty or Washington Dulles, with Asian routes from San Francisco. The carrier still has more long-haul departures than any other airline, averaging 157 daily from US hubs.

The cuts matter because United's own network no longer serves these cities, but many remain reachable through its alliance, joint venture and codeshare partners. That is a key reason for such agreements. Passengers planning trips to places like Copenhagen, Oslo, Stockholm, Manchester or Mumbai may need to book on partner airlines rather than United metal.

The list is retrospective and depends on the timeframe. It excludes Dubai, where United is scheduled to return in March 2027 because of the war in Iran, and Nagoya, which still has nonstop service from Guam. Some dropped airports, such as Berlin Tegel and Istanbul Atatürk, have since closed. United's experience in Dakar lasted only from May 2025 to January 2026.
