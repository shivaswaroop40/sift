Helsinki's 200-mile tunnel network includes swimming pools, a go-karting track, the Amos Rex art museum and Temppeliaukio church, all designed to convert into bomb shelters holding up to 900,000 people. The Itäkeskus swimming hall alone can shelter nearly 4,000 and be drained and fitted with decontamination showers in under 48 hours.

Approved as the world's first masterplanned underground city in 2010, the system grew from heating and telecoms infrastructure originally carved out during the second world war. Visitors can swim at Itäkeskus for €6, explore Amos Rex from €20, and enter Temppeliaukio church for €8.

The dual-purpose design is unusual. Most cities keep underground space segregated for defence, but Helsinki built leisure facilities first and shelter capability second. The result is civil infrastructure that happens to be a bunker, not a bunker retrofitted for civilians.
