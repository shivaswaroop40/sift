Belitung, an island off Sumatra long nicknamed the "Maldives of Indonesia", was confirmed as a UNESCO Global Geopark last year, joining 17 protected geosites including Triassic granite formations and rare Satam tektite stones formed by a meteorite strike 700,000 years ago.

For independent travellers, the designation matters because a new direct flight from Singapore and the opening of two low-impact hotels, the Sheraton Belitung and the Billiton Ekobeach Retreat, are making the island newly accessible while community-managed trails now require guides.

Visitors can expect a quiet island with snorkelling tours through the 350-hectare Tanjung Kelayang Reserve, boat trips past Dutch colonial lighthouses and reefs, and the chance to see the endangered Belitung tarsier under strict ranger supervision. The piece frames Belitung as a working model of regenerative tourism, where locals balance growing visitor numbers against protecting pangolins, hornbills and the tarsier, so anyone planning a trip should expect managed access rather than free-roaming exploration.
