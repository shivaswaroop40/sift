Spirit Airlines' closure has removed a major low-cost competitor from the US market. With fuel and labour costs already climbing, airlines now face less competitive pressure on prices, particularly on domestic routes where Spirit once undercut larger carriers.

For independent travellers who hunt fares, this shift matters. The disappearance of Spirit's rock-bottom pricing removes a pricing anchor that forced legacy carriers to compete on cost. Routes that once saw three-way battles between Spirit and majors now see softer discounting.

Ways exist to offset higher fares, though the source does not detail them. Loyalty programmes, advance booking, and shoulder-season travel remain relevant strategies, but the structural advantage that Spirit's presence created is now gone.
