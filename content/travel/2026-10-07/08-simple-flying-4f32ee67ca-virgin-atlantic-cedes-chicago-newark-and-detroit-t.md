Virgin Atlantic stopped operating its own flights to Chicago, Newark, and Detroit, handing those routes to Delta Air Lines under their transatlantic joint venture. Both carriers pool costs and revenue across UK-North America flights regardless of who operates them, so Virgin Atlantic profits from Delta's services on these routes as effectively as if it flew them itself.

For Virgin Atlantic, this shift makes sense operationally. Delta's vast domestic network can feed connecting passengers into these hub airports, filling 250 to 300 seats daily with transfers. Virgin Atlantic's premium-heavy fleet configuration works better on point-to-point routes where high-yield corporate passengers pay top fares without needing domestic connections.

The Austin collapse illustrates why. Virgin Atlantic launched LHR-Austin service in 2022 targeting tech sector corporate travel, but when those budgets contracted to 70% of historical levels post-pandemic, the route became unviable. Without Delta hub feed to backfill empty seats, the airline lost money on its premium-configured widebodies and pulled out by January 2024.
