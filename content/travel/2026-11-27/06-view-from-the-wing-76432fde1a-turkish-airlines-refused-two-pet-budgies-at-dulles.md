A Georgian journalist, David Nikuradze, and his partner were turned away at Washington Dulles on 14 August. They were flying via Istanbul to Georgia, the country, with two parakeets. Turkish check-in staff asked for a Swine and Bird Import Permit, which is a US state of Georgia form. The birds had a USDA-endorsed health certificate.

Nikuradze returned the next day with stamped letters from Georgian authorities saying no such permit was needed. He still could not fly for a week. Turkish told him earlier flights already had cats booked, and the airline does not carry parakeets and cats on the same plane. They flew on 21 August using the original paperwork.

He puts the cost at more than $5,000. That covers rebooking fees, a pet-friendly hotel in Manassas, a vehicle big enough for six suitcases and a cage for the birds. The author notes Turkish uses dnata for check-in at Dulles and suggests outsourcing may have played a part. That is speculation, not a confirmed cause.
