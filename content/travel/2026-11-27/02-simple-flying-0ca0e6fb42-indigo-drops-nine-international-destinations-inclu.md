IndiGo has no scheduled flights to nine international destinations through 2027, based on a comparison of its 2026 network with its schedule to October 2027. Three are long-haul: Copenhagen, Heathrow and Manchester, all flown on 787-9s leased from Norse Atlantic. The others are Almaty, Baku, Ho Chi Minh City, Réunion, Tashkent and Tbilisi.

The Norse Atlantic lease has ended and Norse has suspended all its flying. Heathrow had daily 787-9 flights from Delhi and Mumbai until October 2026. IndiGo's Heathrow slots have gone to British Airways. Manchester flights from both cities ended in August 2026, and Mumbai to Copenhagen ended in February 2026.

Amsterdam survives, flown by A321XLR with block times of up to 11 hours five minutes and a payload restriction. The source says this keeps IndiGo's Schiphol slots. Amsterdam is likely to be an early A350-900 destination, as those aircraft are due to arrive in 2027.

The Caucasus and Central Asia routes ended between January and February 2026. Pakistan's airspace remains closed to Indian carriers, and Iran's closure also affected Baku and Tbilisi. Chennai to Réunion lasted one season, with 54 departures and a load factor of about 90%, though yields may have been low.
