Bomb threats disrupted at least 27 flights across Europe on Thursday, the second wave this week. No explosives have been found. French fighter jets intercepted Aer Lingus flight EI402 from Dublin to Rome, carrying more than 150 passengers, and escorted it to the Évreux air base in Normandy. Bulgaria's air force escorted three airliners to the ground. In Madrid, Air Europa and Iberia aircraft were halted, emptied and searched after three emailed threats arrived in under 20 minutes.

None of the threats has proved real, but each one still costs a diversion, a search and a rebuilt schedule. The hoaxes add to a list of disruptions, from conflict-driven fuel costs to closed airspace, that is making routine flying in Europe harder. Travellers on European routes should expect diversions, searches and delays even when threats turn out to be false.

One detail remains unexplained. The French prime minister's office said the pilot of the Aer Lingus flight had not cooperated as expected, without giving more detail. No explosives have been found on any affected flight, and whoever sent the threats remains unknown.
