More than 500 petroglyphs are etched into the smooth slate bedrock along the shores of Kejimkujik Lake in Nova Scotia. The site sits inside Kejimkujik National Park and National Historic Site. The Mi'kmaq have lived, hunted and fished in the area since time immemorial, a presence documented in oral histories and in archaeological finds such as ancient campsites, fish weirs and burial grounds.

For independent travellers who prefer places with a story, the carvings are that story. They serve as a visual record of Mi'kmaq history, cut directly into the lake's bedrock rather than kept somewhere separate. The surrounding park holds the wider evidence of that presence, from campsites to weirs and burial grounds, so one visit takes in both the carvings and their context.
