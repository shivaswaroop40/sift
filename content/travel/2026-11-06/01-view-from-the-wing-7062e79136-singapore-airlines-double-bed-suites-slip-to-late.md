Singapore Airlines has unveiled new first class suites and business class seats for its long-haul fleet. The first class centre pair can open into an 80-by-60-inch double bed, which the airline calls the largest in commercial aviation. Business class gains sliding doors and a seat that adjusts continuously from upright to flat, removing the flip-over bed and diagonal sleeping. The first aircraft will not appear until the second half of 2027, after two delays.

The programme covers 41 existing A350s and 31 ordered Boeing 777-9s. The A380, existing 777-300ERs, medium-haul A350s, 787-10s and 737-8s keep their current cabins, and the long-haul A350 and 777 seats date to 2013. The A350 business seat also narrows from 28 inches to 22 inches, trading width for a longer, straighter bed. Only the 777-9 gets all four new cabin designs.

Boeing's 777-9 delays explain why seats designed for that aircraft are going onto A350s first. The 777-9 seats are certified but the aircraft is not, and the A350 seats still need certification. The ultra-long-range A350 has no firm debut date or launch route for the new first class. Singapore still targets the end of 2030 to complete the A350 retrofits.

On the 777-9, premium economy gains two inches of pitch, reaching 40, and nine inches of recline. Economy on the wider 777-9 moves to a 3-4-3 layout at 31 to 32 inches of pitch, against 3-3-3 on current 777-300ERs. A350 retrofits will not bring these seats, only refreshed upholstery and an updated entertainment interface.
