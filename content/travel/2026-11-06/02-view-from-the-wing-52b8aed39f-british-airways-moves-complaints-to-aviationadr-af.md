British Airways is not renewing its contract with the Centre for Effective Dispute Resolution and will use AviationADR for customer disputes. The airline calls it a contract renewal decision. Civil Aviation Authority figures for April 2025 to March 2026 show BA settled 4,414 complaints before a decision, CEDR ruled for passengers 1,619 times and for BA 2,234 times, and passengers received £5,001,728 in all, a 73% success rate including settlements.

EasyJet made the same switch, and the numbers moved. CEDR upheld easyJet passenger complaints 79% of the time over its final six quarters with the airline. In the next six quarters under AviationADR the rate was 29%. AviationADR attributes this to airlines paying valid claims earlier, using law firms to filter cases before escalation. Passengers reach an adjudicator only after the airline's own process rejects them, so the adjudicator is the last free step before court.

A February 2026 review commissioned by the CAA found AviationADR gives airlines decisions in passengers' favour one week before passengers receive them, ostensibly to prepare payment, during which airlines can raise legal challenges. The reviewer said this may create an impression of bias and recommended ending both the advance access and airline challenges to binding decisions. CEDR generally uses legally qualified adjudicators. AviationADR does not require legal qualifications.

AviationADR says its decisions are independent and based on evidence and law, and passengers who reject a ruling can still go to court. Ryanair left AviationADR in 2018 after adverse decisions over staff strikes and rejoined in 2021 after rule changes. The airline pays the body that rules on its complaints. CEDR stops taking new aviation cases on 31 December, and the CAA is working with AviationADR on capacity.
