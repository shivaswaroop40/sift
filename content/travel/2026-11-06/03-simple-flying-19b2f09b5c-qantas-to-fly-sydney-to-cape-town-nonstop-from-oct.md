Qantas will fly nonstop from Sydney to Cape Town from October 19, 2027. The service runs three times weekly, on Tuesdays, Fridays and Sundays, using a Boeing 787-9. The great-circle distance is 5,945 nautical miles (11,010 km) each way, with a block time of 14 hours 40 minutes outbound and 12 hours 55 minutes returning. The route will have no payload restriction, so flights can carry a full load of passengers and belly freight.

No airline has flown between Australia and Cape Town before. The route will also be Cape Town's longest within the Southern Hemisphere, and Qantas' third to South Africa alongside Sydney-Johannesburg and Perth-Johannesburg. The 787-9 has 236 seats, with 42 in business and 28 in premium economy. The average fare, excluding taxes, is US$1,335 one way, about 12% higher than Sydney to Johannesburg, even though the Cape Town route is marginally shorter.

Booking data shows 87,000 round-trip passengers flew between Australia and Cape Town in 2025, up nearly a fifth on 2024. Only about 23,000 were local Sydney-Cape Town traffic, which the nonstop may grow by around 40%. Qantas will also feed passengers from Auckland, Brisbane and Melbourne, places with around 42,000 passengers. Tour operators can now sell open-jaw trips into Johannesburg and out of Cape Town on one airline.
