Tourist taxes are expanding across major cities. Italy expects €1.2 billion from visitor taxes across 1,411 municipalities this year. Amsterdam plans to raise its accommodation tax from 12.5% to 20%, while Kyoto has increased its rates. These levies are shifting from funding tourism marketing towards housing, schools, and general city services.

The appeal for officials is political rather than practical. Tourists cannot vote against spending decisions, making them ideal taxpayers from a government perspective. Visitors arrive, spend, and leave; if costs rise, their only recourse is to visit elsewhere or choose a different destination.

This represents a quiet reinvention of how visitor taxes function. Rather than creating economic incentives to attract more tourists, cities are using them to fund local infrastructure and services that residents would otherwise need to support through traditional taxation.
