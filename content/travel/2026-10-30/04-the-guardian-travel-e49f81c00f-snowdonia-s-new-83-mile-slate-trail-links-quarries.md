The Snowdonia Slate Trail, or Llwybr Llechi Eryri, is a new 83-mile loop around the national park, starting in Bangor and ending in Bethesda. It links abandoned slate quarries with some of the UK's finest riverside and mountain walking. A Guardian writer walked a three-day section from Llanberis to Betws-y-Coed, via Beddgelert and Llan Ffestiniog, taking in the Dorothea and Cwmorthin quarries.

The route suits walkers who want scenery with a story. Dorothea quarry's industrial buildings were hand-fitted without mortar, and the last Cornish beam engine drew 10 gallons a second from pits 500ft down. Beddgelert has pubs, cafes and gear shops, and the community-owned Y Pengwern at Llan Ffestiniog offers basic rooms. On the remote upland the writer saw nobody all day, with Yr Wyddfa's crowds less than 10 miles away.

The trail also records the industry's human cost. In the late 19th century Cwmorthin quarry was known locally as 'the slaughterhouse' for its fatal accidents, and it took 10 tons of stone to make one ton of marketable slate. After violent clashes the landowner defeated the quarrymen, and many emigrated to the United States. The route also crosses the 298-mile Cambrian Way, allowing a detour up Cnicht.
