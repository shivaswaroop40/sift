American Airlines cancelled award tickets nearly three weeks after issuing them. A pricing error had made some international multi-city awards price at the cost of the first domestic segment, long-haul flights included, among them business class to Australia at 24,500 miles outbound. The writer filed a DOT complaint, because American's contract of carriage requires it to void a mistaken fare within 72 hours of learning of it. American's response admits more than 72 hours passed, argues it can cancel an award whenever it wants, and asks for dismissal.

The case matters to anyone who chases mistake fares or award deals. DOT policy lets an airline cancel an error fare rather than honour it, provided it demonstrates the mistake, refunds the ticket and reimburses verifiable expenses. American then published a stricter deadline for itself. If DOT enforces that deadline, a ticket issued at an error price becomes harder to cancel once 72 hours pass from discovery, even when the passenger knew the deal was too good.

American's answer rests on AAdvantage terms that let it revoke a benefit issued in error. But those terms also make award travel subject to the operating airline's contract of carriage, and say the specific agreement governs when terms conflict. There is precedent. DOT fined Alitalia for publishing passenger protections in its conditions of carriage while relying on conflicting tariff terms.

The complaint is unresolved. American denies its legal conclusions without explaining why the 72-hour language does not govern these tickets. Its filing also reuses the opening of an answer submitted six days earlier in another passenger's case, complete with a reference to that passenger's eleven awards.
