Phu Loc Champa Temple stands alone on a hilltop above the flat farmland of An Nhon Bac in Vietnam. It was built in the 12th century and rises about 15 metres from a square base. The brickwork carries the marks of Champa builders, including vertical pilasters, false doorways and spearhead arch motifs.

The civilisation that built it has vanished, and this weathered sanctuary is one of its surviving traces. For independent travellers, that makes it a site with a story, set in working farmland rather than a resort area. The architectural detail alone distinguishes it from Vietnam's more familiar monuments.
