United Airlines has introduced a basic fare product for its domestic first class cabin. The move follows the basic product the airline launched for Polaris business class this spring. The model mirrors basic economy, where the cheapest tickets in a market carry restrictions intended to push travellers toward more expensive fares. The report describes the new domestic first class version as restrictive.

This matters because the cheapest first class fare may no longer buy the full first class product. Premium cabins are increasingly subject to the same unbundling that reshaped economy pricing. Anyone comparing fares on United should check the fare rules before booking, since the discount may come at the cost of benefits they expect.

The excerpt ends before the details, so the exact restrictions are not confirmed here. The wider pattern is documented, though. The report notes that restrictions once limited to the cheapest economy tickets are increasingly spreading to premium cabins across the industry, not just at United. Travellers should expect the cheapest premium fare to come with conditions attached.
