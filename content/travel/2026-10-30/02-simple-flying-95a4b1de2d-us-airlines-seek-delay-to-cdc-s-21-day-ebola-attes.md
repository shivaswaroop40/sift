From 23:59 EST on 3 November 2026, every passenger boarding an international flight to the US must attest that they have not been in the Democratic Republic of the Congo in the previous 21 days. The CDC rule applies regardless of where the flight originates, and covers US citizens, green card holders and crew. Anyone who has been in the DRC within that window cannot enter until the 21 days have elapsed.

Airlines For America, the US carrier advocacy group, supports the CDC's aim but says changes to check-in systems, digital signatures, station procedures and training cannot be completed before the 3 November start date, and is pressing for a delay. Airlines must also retain the forms for at least six weeks after arrival. Travellers should expect an extra step at check-in on every US-bound international flight, even with no DRC travel history.

The industry also argues the rules are too broad. IATA says it is unreasonable to require all airlines serving the US to modify their procedures to collect attestations from every individual. According to Reuters, of the 130 million people screened by US Customs and Border Protection in 2025, just 25,000 had direct links to the DRC, about one in 5,200. A4A wants a response focused on travellers who have actually visited the worst-affected areas, and at minimum an exemption for crew and military personnel.

Whether the CDC delays or narrows the rules before the start date is unresolved. The attestation can be provided digitally or on paper. As the rules stand, anyone flying to the US on an international service from 3 November will need to sign one, whatever their travel history.
