Antarctic tourism has grown from about 8,000 visitors in the early 1990s to about 122,000 in the 2023-24 season, according to IAATO figures. One forecast projects more than 450,000 by the 2033-34 season. Fleets have grown with demand. Lindblad has put two more polar ships into service since 2019, and Viking runs two polar-capable expedition ships with two more on order. Veteran guides say the pool of experienced staff has not kept pace.

For travellers booking the continent, this matters directly. Guides say the staffing gap is changing what guests can safely do. Antarctica has no single authority able to cap growth, because the continent is governed by consensus. The safety and quality of a trip therefore rest largely on which operator you choose.

The claim comes with caveats. The industry disputes that standards have slipped, and the guides' case rests on anecdote rather than published figures. Capacity also arrived on a lag, since many of the ships were ordered before the Covid-19 pandemic, according to Jonathan Zaccaria, who is quoted in the report. Demand has since surged as remote trips became easier to book and wealthy travellers chase rarer experiences.
