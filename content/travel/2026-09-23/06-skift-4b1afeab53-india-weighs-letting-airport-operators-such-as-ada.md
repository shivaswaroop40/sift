India's civil aviation ministry is reviewing whether companies that run airports should be allowed to hold stakes in scheduled carriers. Minister K. Rammohan Naidu said the government is weighing the "pros and cons" but has reached no decision.

The debate was reopened by Adani Airport Holdings, which has asked the Airports Authority of India for a waiver from rules capping an airport operator's stake in an airline at 10%. Adani runs Mumbai Airport.

For travellers, the outcome could reshape competition on domestic routes. India wants to break its airline duopoly, and the policy will determine whether airport groups can launch their own carriers or take meaningful ownership of existing ones.

The restriction dates back nearly 20 years, when Delhi and Mumbai airports were privatised. Any change would need a formal regulatory shift before any new airline linked to an airport operator could take off.
