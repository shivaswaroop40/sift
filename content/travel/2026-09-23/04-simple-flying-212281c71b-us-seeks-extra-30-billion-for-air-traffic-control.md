One day after a telecom failure halted flights at Newark, JFK, LaGuardia and Philadelphia on 21 September 2026, US Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy asked Congress for an additional $30 billion for aviation infrastructure, on top of $12.5 billion already approved in 2025 for the FAA's Brand New Air Traffic Control System. The outage delayed or cancelled around 9,500 US flights and forced more than 100 diversions.

The new request would split funding roughly three ways: $10 billion for ageing ATC towers, $10 billion for telecommunications and other ATC technology, and $10 billion for wider airport improvements. FAA's published hardware targets include 5,170 high-speed network connections, 27,625 radios, 462 digital voice switches and 612 radars. Equipment-related delay minutes in 2025 ran about 300% above the 2010-2024 average.

The meltdown began when a primary circuit failed at Philadelphia TRACON at around 09:30, and a backup failed because a New Jersey fibre-optic cable had been severed during construction. Duffy says the replacement architecture is designed to remove such single points of failure by giving FAA facilities multiple telecom routes. By 22 September, operations had largely normalised.

Progress is already under way: the FAA has replaced over 64% of its copper telecom infrastructure and converted 403 radio sites. It also began limited operation of SMART, an AI-supported platform that combines around 200 data streams to forecast congestion, on the same day as the outage. Obsolete copper wiring is targeted for elimination by September 2027.
