A telecommunications failure at the FAA's Philadelphia air traffic control facility grounded arrivals at Newark, JFK, LaGuardia, Philadelphia and Boston airports for several hours on Monday September 21, triggering around 7,000 delays and cancellations across the United States according to FlightAware. Newark was the worst hit, with commercial arrivals halted for nearly 8 hours and more than 600 flights cancelled.

The cause was an unlucky combination of failures: a primary telecom circuit went down, and the backup fibre-optic cable had been cut during railroad construction in New Jersey. With controllers unable to use some radar and communications systems, the FAA sharply restricted traffic through the airspace. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said telecom lines were repaired by Monday evening and operations were resuming.

The repair is only the start of the recovery. Aircraft and crews are now displaced around the country, and rebuilding tightly timed schedules at busy hubs can take a day or two. A plane meant to fly from Chicago to Denver can still be late if it was supposed to overnight there from Newark and never arrived. The FAA has not confirmed multi-day disruption, but airlines warn residual delays are likely through Tuesday.

United issued a travel waiver covering Newark after the disruption, and other carriers allowed penalty-free rebookings. Travellers flying on Tuesday should check their inbound aircraft in the airline app before heading to the airport. If a flight is cancelled and a passenger declines alternative transport, federal rules entitle them to a refund, but airlines are not obliged to cover hotels or meals because the disruption originated with air traffic control rather than the carrier.
