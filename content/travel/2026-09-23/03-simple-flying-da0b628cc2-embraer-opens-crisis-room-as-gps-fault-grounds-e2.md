A GPS failure affecting Embraer E190-E2 and E195-E2 jets fitted with Honeywell Primus Epic 2.0 navigation systems has prompted the planemaker to open a crisis room with Honeywell. The fault was first detected at Brazilian carrier Azul on Tuesday evening, which grounded all 43 of its E2 aircraft. More than 200 E2 jets across 24 airlines are affected, with Porter Airlines, KLM Cityhopper, Binter Canarias and Luxair also reporting cancellations.

A temporary workaround involves pulling circuit breakers to force a cold restart of the navigation modules, but Embraer warns the failure can recur after a reset. A permanent fix will need a software patch from Honeywell. Flightradar24 data suggests a handful of E2s remain in service. It is unclear whether Royal Jordanian and Hunnu Air have grounded their fleets.

The bigger operators are Porter with 52 E2s and KLM Cityhopper with 25. LATAM and GOL are unaffected for now as their E2 orders have not yet entered service. Mexicana de Aviación is the only other Central or South American carrier hit, with a much smaller fleet.

The cause of the GPS signal reception error has not been publicly identified. Embraer has promised updates as its joint investigation with Honeywell progresses, but no timeline for a permanent software patch has been given.
