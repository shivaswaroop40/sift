United and Air Canada are launching six routes between the U.S. and Canada for summer 2027. United will return to St. John's, Newfoundland for the first time since 2016, plus add Denver service to Ottawa and Quebec City. Air Canada is adding routes from Louisville, Indianapolis and Newark to Canadian cities.

For independent travellers, this opens a new easternmost entry point to Canada on the U.S. east coast. St. John's becomes accessible from Newark, where WestJet currently holds a monopoly on U.S. service to the city. The expansion follows a 6% decline in cross-border capacity over two years.

Air Canada is cutting five U.S. routes, including services from Boston, Charleston, Nashville, Raleigh-Durham and Seattle to Canadian destinations. The cuts reflect weak demand on those specific pairs, though overall capacity is being redirected to stronger markets.
