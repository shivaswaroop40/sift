Dubai International expects about 70 million passengers this year, down 26% from 95.2 million in 2025. Almost half its airlines have not returned since the U.S.-Iran war. It served 50.4 million passengers in the first nine months, down 28.1%, and is now served by 57 airlines against 108 last year.

Third-quarter traffic fell 22.1% to 18.8 million from 24.2 million. August was the busiest month at 6.6 million. British Airways resumed services this month, joining Royal Air Maroc, SunExpress and Cebu Pacific. More airlines are due back in the coming weeks.

For anyone routing through the Gulf, fewer carriers means fewer options and less competition on fares. A consultant quoted says airlines are rebuilding their exposure cautiously. Rival hubs have picked up disrupted traffic, especially international transfers, so alternative connections may now be better established.
