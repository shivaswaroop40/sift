Free carbon allowances for airlines under the EU emissions trading system ended in 2026. Airlines must now buy every allowance they need. The share of costlier sustainable aviation fuel they must use also keeps rising.

International Airlines Group, owner of British Airways and Iberia, paid €359 million ($395 million) in carbon costs in the first nine months of 2026. That is up 21% on a year earlier. The group said the end of free allowances in the EU and UK contributed to the rise.

Airlines flying within Europe must surrender one allowance for every metric ton of CO2 or equivalent they emit. Until this year they received part of their allowances free. The UK runs an equivalent scheme and also stopped giving free allowances. The source text is cut off after this point, so further detail on other airlines is not available.
