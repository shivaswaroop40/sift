Transport for Wales launched the TaithCymru coach service in October. It runs daily between Holyhead and Carmarthen, with nine stops including Caernarfon, Porthmadog, Dolgellau, Machynlleth, Aberystwyth and Aberaeron. The Guardian writer's trip from Caernarfon took about five hours. Tickets are online only, and you cannot get off between stops.

The train alternative leaves Wales. The fastest Marches route via Chester, Crewe and Shrewsbury takes about five and a half hours and costs around £105. The article's headline claims the coach saves half an hour. Fares are low: £19 for an open return from Caernarfon to Carmarthen, and £25 for the full Holyhead to Carmarthen open return.

For independent travellers, it gives a through route down west Wales without a car. Holyhead links to Irish ferries, and Carmarthen's rail hub connects to Pembrokeshire and Cardiff. Eight coaches run overnight, with toilets and wifi on board. The writer was the only passenger and the driver mentioned teething problems without detail, so check timetables and reliability before relying on it.
