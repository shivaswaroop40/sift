During the longest US nationwide ground stop since September 2001, some United passengers at Chicago O'Hare were kept on board for seven or eight hours on Friday. One passenger on a flight to Washington Dulles got off after about seven hours. Another, bound for Charleston, waited eight hours before the flight was cancelled.

US rules normally require airlines to offer disembarkation after three hours on domestic flights and four on international ones. Airlines must give a status update after 30 minutes. By two hours they must provide food and water. Lavatories must work and medical aid must be available.

The rules have exceptions. Passengers need not be allowed off if it poses a safety or security risk, or would disrupt airport operations. So the three-hour limit is not absolute, and a long ground stop can push delays past it.

The passenger who flew to Dulles then spent four hours queuing at O'Hare on Saturday to rebook. United also had a separate system outage on Monday evening, which stopped departures for about an hour. Airborne flights and United Express services were not affected.
