Google changed hotel and flight search results in Europe this week, following an EU antitrust enforcement decision. The new page has two units: an aggregator unit showing third parties such as Booking.com and Agoda, and a supplier unit listing direct providers like individual hotels and airlines. In the aggregator unit, the top-ranked provider’s results are expanded by default. A search for Madrid hotels from Spain shows the new interface.

The change matters because it separates intermediaries from direct sellers in search results. For travellers, that means a clearer choice between booking through an OTA and going direct. For hotels and airlines, the supplier unit could affect direct booking volumes, though the source does not give data on that impact.

The redesign is meant to help Google avoid fines from EU regulators. The text raises questions about how the restricted supplier unit will affect direct bookings, how the rules will apply to AI Overview and AI Mode, and whether similar regulation could spread to other markets. It does not answer them.
