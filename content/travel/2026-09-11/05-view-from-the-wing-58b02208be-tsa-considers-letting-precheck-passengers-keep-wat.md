The TSA is considering letting PreCheck passengers take full bottles of water through airport security, but only at checkpoints using Analogic CT scanners that can identify the liquid inside the bottle. The initial change would cover PreCheck travellers, water only, and selected airports. Former TSA administrator John Pistole called for the move in 2014, and the New York Times reports it is unlikely before the holidays.

For the independent traveller, the practical gain is not having to dump a bottle of water before security and buy another airside. But the 3.4-ounce rule would probably remain for other liquids, so coffee and peanut butter would still be confiscated. The benefit depends on which checkpoints take part and whether the PreCheck lane is open, so it will not be uniform.

The restrictions date from August 2006, after British authorities disrupted a plot to use liquid explosives on transatlantic flights. PreCheck passengers are vetted and unlikely to be plotting such attacks. The Analogic scanners were first used at Dallas/Fort Worth in November 2017, but TSA says only that change will come as technology evolves.
