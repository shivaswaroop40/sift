September and October have become increasingly popular for travel, according to a New York Times Travel article. That popularity has changed the traditional shoulder season. The remaining off-season now consists of sporadic midweek troughs and a few weeks in November.

For independent travellers, November is the practical window for off-season deals and quieter trips. September and October no longer offer the same shoulder-season advantages. Midweek departures may still be cheaper, but the pattern is uneven. Travellers should look for November dates rather than assume the whole autumn is quiet.

The article does not name specific destinations or quantify savings. Its claim is general, so treat November as a starting point rather than a guaranteed deal. Readers should check individual routes and fares before booking. The shift is described in broad terms, not as a data-backed trend.
