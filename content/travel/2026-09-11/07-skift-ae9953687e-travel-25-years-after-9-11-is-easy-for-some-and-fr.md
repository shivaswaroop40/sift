A Skift essay marking 25 years since 9/11 argues that the most obvious changes to travel are the least interesting. Airport security, shoe and liquid rules, hardened cockpits and longer lines are familiar to every traveller. Some of those rituals are now disappearing, but the larger system built around them remains.

The deeper shift, it says, is the role of identity in travel. The industry has become very good at making trips easier to search, buy and navigate, while governments have become more sophisticated at deciding who gets to make those trips. For some travellers the journey is more seamless than in 2001; for others, friction begins before the airport.

The author says he has lived both sides of that change. A decade ago he wrote a Skift essay about moving through airports as a Muslim. The piece is paywalled after that point, so this summary is based only on the opening sections.
