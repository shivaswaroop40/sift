Eurail, the train pass for non-Europeans since 1959, is being renamed Interrail from 10 September. The brand says the change is in name only. Same benefits, same price, and existing Eurail passes remain valid. Interrail was the equivalent pass for European residents, introduced in 1973.

For decades Eurail was shorthand for flexible, unlimited rail travel across Europe, covering 33 countries by 2020. The name carried emotional weight. Rick Steves said it meant freedom and ad-lib travel, such as choosing Amsterdam over Paris on a platform whim. The product itself is unchanged, but the familiar name is gone.

The company found its core market early. It introduced a student pass in 1971, and North American sales jumped almost 50 percent to more than 130,000 that year. Today, 49 percent of pass holders are aged 12 to 27. The Interrail name now applies to all customers globally, but no route or pricing changes have been announced.
