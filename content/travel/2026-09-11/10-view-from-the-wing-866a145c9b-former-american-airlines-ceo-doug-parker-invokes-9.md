Former American Airlines CEO Doug Parker has revived a campaign to shut down Dallas-based carrier JSX, invoking the 9/11 attacks on the Airlines Confidential podcast. He said scheduled airlines were flying without full TSA-level security and that credential authentication was a must-have that not all scheduled airlines used. Parker previously lobbied TSA Administrator David Pekoske with Southwest's chief operating officer.

The campaign matters because American and Southwest, both based in Dallas, see JSX as unfair competition. JSX offers all-first-class service from private terminals, with drinks, bags, snacks and wi-fi included, often priced below American's first class. American's current CEO Robert Isom said in leaked comments that the push was a commercial dispute in which they enlisted government to block a competitor.

Parker's security claim is thin. JSX participates in Secure Flight and does weapons detection and explosives screening. Former TSA Administrator Pekoske, whom Parker lobbied, said addressing security vulnerabilities does not necessarily require government screeners. Parker does not identify an actual vulnerability or explain why JSX's procedures fail. Aircraft size, passenger volume, access controls and separation from commercial terminals mean different risks.

Parker also attacked airlines with marketing relationships with JSX, by extension United, which has a frequent flyer partnership and an ownership stake. American itself operates charter flights away from commercial terminals without traditional TSA and advertises private handling and screening. Parker does not argue that private jet departures should face TSA.
