TSA has begun rolling out Gateside by TSA PreCheck at 13 US airports. The programme allows eligible non-ticketed visitors to pass through security after advance approval, so they can accompany a passenger to a departure gate, meet an arriving traveller airside, or visit someone during a layover. Visitors must be eligible for PreCheck and hold a Known Traveler Number.

The pass is free, but visitors must apply online one to three days before the visit and select the participating airport. Approved visitors bring ID, go through a PreCheck checkpoint, and remain subject to normal screening. Approval is valid for the designated calendar day and allows re-entry to the secure area.

It matters because most airports currently restrict the secure side to ticketed passengers. Gateside is broader than airline-issued escort passes: it does not require the visitor to be helping someone who qualifies for special assistance. The likely users, says travel rewards editor Jennifer Yellin, are families seeing off or welcoming travellers, nervous fliers, and people helping others navigate unfamiliar airports.

The programme is an operational assessment, not a return to pre-9/11 open access. Similar visitor-pass schemes already exist at airports such as Seattle and Orlando, but Gateside is tied to PreCheck eligibility and uses the PreCheck lane. Yellin expects it to remain niche because advance approval and a real reason are needed.
