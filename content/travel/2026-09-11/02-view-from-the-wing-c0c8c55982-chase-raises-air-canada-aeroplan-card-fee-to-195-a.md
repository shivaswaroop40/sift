Chase is doubling the annual fee on its US Air Canada Aeroplan credit card from $95 to $195. From September 10 the renamed card gives primary cardholders automatic Aeroplan 25K status, 15% off eligible Air Canada award flights, 3 points per dollar on travel and up to $100 a year in Air Canada statement credits. Existing cardholders get the new benefits immediately; the higher fee applies at renewals from December 1.

The refresh matters because automatic 25K status previously required $15,000 of annual spending after the first two years. The 15% award discount stacks with Priority Rewards, which can cut the points price by 50%. A cardholder redeeming 100,000 points would save 15,000 points, and that saving alone can justify the higher fee for someone who does not put substantial spending on the card.

There are trade-offs. Grocery and dining earnings fall from 3 points per dollar to 2, the monthly 500-point bonus for each $2,000 spent disappears, and the 10% bonus on Ultimate Rewards transfers of at least 50,000 points ends on December 31. The advertised up to 5x on Air Canada purchases includes two points earned from Aeroplan status, not the card, so 3x on Air Canada spend is mediocre.

The card also contributes up to 25,000 Status Qualifying Credits per year. It gives 5,000 for holding it, 10,000 after $25,000 of spending, and another 10,000 after $50,000. Automatic 35K status arrives at $75,000 of annual spending. These thresholds were already part of Aeroplan's 2026 elite status overhaul.
