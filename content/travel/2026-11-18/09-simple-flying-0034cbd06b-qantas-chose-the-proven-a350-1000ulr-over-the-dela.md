Qantas picked the Airbus A350-1000ULR, with Rolls-Royce Trent XWB engines, for Project Sunrise rather than the Boeing 777X and its GE9X engines. It announced the choice in 2019 and placed the order in 2022. The GE9X is described as more thermally efficient, but it is new and unproven.

Sydney to London Heathrow will be 9,188 NM (17,016 km) and take over 20 hours. It is due to launch in October 2027, once Qantas has three aircraft. The first A350-1000ULR arrives in April and will fly short-haul crew familiarisation flights. Sydney to New York follows.

For travellers, the practical point is timing. The 777X is one of the most delayed programmes in recent history, and its early production is expected to be slow. The article says Qantas wanted a type already in service, with an engine that has been largely trouble-free.

Melbourne to London and New York appear to be a lower priority and may only come with the final deliveries. Qantas also plans to use the A350-1000ULR across its network, mainly replacing Boeing 787s.
