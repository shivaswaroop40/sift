Chase will add Qantas Frequent Flyer and Cathay Pacific Asia Miles as Ultimate Rewards transfer partners on 18 November. Both transfer at 1:1. Eligible cards include Sapphire Reserve, Sapphire Preferred, Sapphire Reserve for Business, Ink Business Preferred and J.P. Morgan Reserve. These are Chase's first new airline partners since Air Canada Aeroplan in August 2021.

The main gain is award space. Qantas and Cathay keep more premium cabin seats for their own members than they release to partner programmes such as American AAdvantage, Alaska and British Airways. The author has found Qantas business class awards between Australia and the US by staying flexible on gateway and date, often about 60 days out.

Cathay's rate beats American Express, which gives 800 Asia Miles per 1,000 Membership Rewards points. A 100,000-mile award costs 100,000 Chase points against 125,000 Amex points. Asia Miles can also cut fuel surcharges on British Airways awards compared with booking through AAdvantage. Asia Miles prices can still be high.

There are limits. Qantas now requires Silver status or higher, and a minimum age of nine, to book Emirates first class. The author prefers Copa ConnectMiles for Emirates business class, at 80,000 miles one way between the US and Dubai without surcharges. Amex, Citi, Capital One and Rove already partner with both airlines, so Chase is catching up.
