Southwest Airlines is preparing to acquire dozens of Boeing 787 widebodies, primarily for European flights, marking its first move away from an all-737 fleet. The airline needs a premium cabin to make the economics work. Leased or used aircraft could start operations sooner than new builds, though preparation will take years.

For Southwest, European widebodies serve an existing customer base already flying those routes on competitors and drive loyalty programme spending. The airline must negotiate a new pilot contract covering higher pay, additional crew on long flights, rest facilities and overseas operations.

Winter capacity remains the central challenge. Summer Europe flights alone lose money. Southwest could deploy aircraft to Hawaii, southern South America or the Caribbean during the Atlantic off-season, or concentrate year-round service on London and Paris with reduced frequencies.
