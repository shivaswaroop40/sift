US Customs and Border Protection ceased routine passport stamping on October 1 to speed up processing and cut costs. Travellers now receive only an electronic I-94 record instead of the dual physical stamp and digital entry.

For independent travellers, losing the stamp removes a built-in travel log. Passport stamps have served as immediate proof of entry dates, visa categories and permitted stay duration—information now locked in an electronic system.

Travellers can still request a stamp but it is no longer guaranteed. Immigration experts recommend saving copies of your I-94 record after each US arrival, since physical stamps once caught errors before travellers left the airport. At least 35 other countries have already phased out passport stamping.
