A reader reports seeing passengers stage injuries on flights in Asia, claiming they were hurt by someone taking a bag from an overhead bin. He first noticed it on an AirAsia flight, where passengers in the first five rows were targeted, then watched what he believes was a coordinated attempt in Singapore Airlines business class. Claimants cry out even when untouched, demand medical attention and a police report, and block the aisle.

The recent sightings come from a single reader, but the scam has precedent. In 2011, Chinese prosecutors said a man deliberately engineered collisions with passengers handling luggage, claimed a diamond had been knocked from his watch, and demanded compensation. One victim paid 15,000 yuan, about $2,300 at the time. The man was arrested on suspicion of extortion after taking 29,000 yuan and a phone from four passengers. Premium passengers appear to be the targets.

Genuine injuries do happen, and under the Montreal Convention an airline can be liable for bodily injury onboard or during boarding and deplaning, even when another passenger handled the bag. If someone claims you hurt them, involve the crew, ask them to arrange medical attention, and have them document your account and witnesses with contact details, since third-party bookings may leave the airline without any.
