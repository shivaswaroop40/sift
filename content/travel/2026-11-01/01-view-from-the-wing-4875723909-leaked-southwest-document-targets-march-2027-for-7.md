An internal Southwest rollout document, shared with View from the Wing, puts the airline's first passenger flights on the Boeing 737 MAX 7 in March 2027. The FAA certified the aircraft on August 3 after years of delays. Deliveries of 27 already-built jets begin in mid-to-late December 2026, with newly built aircraft arriving from February 2027. Each prebuilt plane gets at least five preparation flights, including a simulated extended-overwater operation lasting more than 3.5 hours. The March date is marked subject to change.

The jets will carry 150 Recaro seats with extra-legroom indicators, in-seat power, larger overhead bins and three lavatories, one more than Southwest's 737-700s have. Starlink wifi is due fleet-wide by autumn 2027. The first 27 aircraft take over extended overwater routes from 28 older 737-800s, including Burbank to Honolulu, Phoenix and Las Vegas to Hawaii, and interisland flying.

The seat count is deliberate. Southwest needs one flight attendant per 50 passenger seats, so a 151st seat would require another crew member. Replacing a 137-seat 737-700 with a 150-seat MAX 7 adds 13 seats, about 9.5%. The rollout is mostly fleet replacement rather than growth. Southwest expects 67 deliveries and roughly 60 retirements this year, and still operates 271 737-700s averaging 20 years old.

One gap remains. The new jets will have no first class cabin, and Southwest will soon be the only major US airline without one. Frontier launches its own first class on March 5, 2027, the same month these planes are due to enter service. Extra legroom seats are the most that Southwest will offer.
