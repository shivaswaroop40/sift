Hunnu Air, Porter, Airlink, TUI fly Belgium and Widerøe are flying Embraer E195-E2 jets on long leisure routes, with sectors such as Ulaanbaatar to Phu Quoc (2,598 miles), Ottawa to Liberia (2,468 miles) and Cape Town to Mauritius (2,557 miles) running five to six hours block time. Porter has scheduled more than 40 warm-weather routes for winter 2026/27 from Canadian cities to Mexico, Costa Rica and the Caribbean.

The E195-E2's 3,000 nautical mile full-passenger range and 132-seat, 2+2 cabin let carriers open thin long-haul leisure markets that cannot fill a 180- or 190-seat A320 or 737. Lower trip costs also make it easier to test seasonal demand. Most operators still use the type on short European or North American hub feeds, where block times rarely exceed two hours.

By early 2026 Embraer had logged 504 firm E2 orders, 213 deliveries and a 291-aircraft backlog, with Porter the largest operator at 54 of 75 on order, followed by Azul, Avelo, SAS and KLM. Air Peace, which ran a roughly 2,809-mile Lagos to Johannesburg sector on the type, withdrew from the route in February, so the long-sector concept is still being proven commercially rather than established.
