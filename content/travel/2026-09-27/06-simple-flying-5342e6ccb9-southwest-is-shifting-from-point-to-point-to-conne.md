Southwest Airlines, the largest US low-cost carrier, is moving away from its traditional point-to-point model and towards connections through its busiest airports. Dallas Love Field, Denver International and Baltimore/Washington will each run more than 150 daily departures, with Chicago, Phoenix and Austin among the key connecting points. The airline avoids the word "hub" and prefers "high-density stations".

The shift follows rising labour and fuel costs and growing competition on US point-to-point routes, where Frontier and the legacy carriers now overlap. Southwest also wants to expand internationally and attract business travellers, which requires one-stop options and higher load factors than a single direct city pair can deliver.

For travellers, the practical changes are more connections, longer itineraries on some routes, and a different network shape from the airline they may have booked for direct flights. Free checked bags are also being removed as part of the wider shake-up of Southwest's offering.

The source is a Simple Flying explainer and is light on confirmed dates or route lists beyond the named high-density stations, so the scale of the change is still a direction of travel rather than a fixed timetable.
