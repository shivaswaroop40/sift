Southwest has abandoned nearly every consumer-friendly feature that defined it: free checked bags, open seating, bundled fares, direct-only distribution. The article argues these changes were inevitable once the airline joined Google Flights in May 2024 and began selling through Expedia in February 2025, because its bundled fares could not compete on price comparison displays.

The shift matters to travellers because the airline now charges $45 for a first checked bag and $55 for a second, offers paid seat assignments, and sells basic economy fares. Rapid Rewards points have been devalued, and first class is expected to follow. The practical experience of flying Southwest is converging with other US carriers, while the airline has yet to match the premium products they offer.

The piece traces the logic step by step. Distribution forced unbundling. Unbundling made free bags costly to keep. Premium seating required assigned seats. Each decision was defensible on its own, but the chain ended with an airline that looks like every other US carrier but, in the author’s view, offers less.

Southwest’s own research showed 97% of travellers knew about Bags Fly Free and projected a net $300 million annual loss from charging for bags, though actual revenue has lagged that projection. An activist investor accelerated the timeline, but the author argues the old model had run out of room to grow regardless.
