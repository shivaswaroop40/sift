Airlines including Air France, KLM, Delta and United are selling 'basic' business class fares that strip out lounge access, checked bags and flexibility while offering only marginal savings, often 7% to 10% below the standard fare.

The article argues these new fares are unlikely to open up premium cabins to new buyers. Delta's own example showed basic business at $2,689 versus classic at $2,889, and a United fare difference of just $100. Removing perks does not create new demand when almost every Delta One customer already buys the seat for the flat bed.

Flying Blue will extend the model to awards from 8 September, lifting a New York to Paris business award with full benefits from 60,000 to 75,000 miles, a 25% increase. The piece warns airlines risk publishing lounge values that become refund benchmarks when lounges close or overcrowd, and may push their most valuable frequent flyers towards the cheapest fare.

The underlying problem is revenue management. Airlines have built huge premium cabins with seats that spoil if empty, but the old 50-day advance purchase fence no longer separates leisure from business travellers, and basic business restrictions are too weak to replace it.
