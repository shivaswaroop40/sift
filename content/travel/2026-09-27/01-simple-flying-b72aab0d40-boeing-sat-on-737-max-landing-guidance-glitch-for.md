Boeing identified a software glitch in November 2024 that can disable automated flight guidance during a missed approach on 737 MAX 8 and 9 aircraft running version U.14. It did not notify operators until August 2026 because it did not consider the issue a safety risk.

It matters because Boeing held the information for almost two years while carriers continued flying affected aircraft. Pilots can fly the plane manually if the autopilot drops out, but the delayed disclosure raises questions about how Boeing weighs software faults against commercial pressure to keep the MAX family delivering.

US carriers Southwest and United say none of their MAX jets have the U.14 software installed, and United has stopped accepting new deliveries with that version. Both are pushing Boeing to halt U.14 shipments entirely and use earlier software instead.

The FAA has set up a Corrective Action Review Board and is assessing whether the glitch affects the just-certified MAX 7 and the pending MAX 10. A permanent software fix is in development.
