OAG data shows British Airways adding the Boeing 787 to seven long-haul Heathrow routes between October 2026 and October 2027. Johannesburg, San José and Tampa start on 25 October 2026. Melbourne via Kuala Lumpur starts on 9 January 2027. Bahrain, Rio de Janeiro and San Francisco follow on 28 March 2027. The schedule reflects what is known as of 12 November and may change.

San José and Tampa move from Gatwick, where the 777-200ER flew them. San José will be a first for Heathrow, up to five weekly with the 787. Tampa gets up to daily flights. Melbourne returns to BA's network after 20 years, daily and entirely on the 787-9. Bahrain is currently suspended, and the 787-9 will replace the 777-200ER there.

Share of flights on the 787 varies widely. Johannesburg is only 10% and replaces the A380 up to the end of March 2027. Rio is 57% on the 787-8, and San Francisco is 24% on the 787-9. If you want a Dreamliner on those routes, check the aircraft type before you book, because the rest of the flying stays on other types.

On 28 October 2026 the 787 also returned to Heathrow to Madrid, all three variants rotating on BA460 and BA461. The article notes BA sometimes swaps in an A320, though Flightradar24 shows the 787 has appeared as scheduled so far. The 777-200ER is due to replace the 787 on this route next summer.
