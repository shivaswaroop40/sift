United Airlines announced on 11 November that third-party AI agents may not book, change or service flights, or buy products, on a passenger's behalf. The rule is added to its Contract of Carriage as Rule 6.J.6. It covers United's website and app, travel agencies and booking platforms. United is the first major US carrier to announce such a ban formally.

The ban applies even if the passenger oversees the agent or approves its actions. Rule 5.H lets United cancel any reservation made or managed by an AI agent without notice. The definition is broad and includes AI assistants, standalone agents and AI-enabled browsers.

United cites data security, unintended purchases or MileagePlus mile use, and federal rules. Under 49 CFR 175.25, passengers must acknowledge dangerous goods information at check-in, and United says an agent could bypass that. The report also points to commercial motives. CEO Scott Kirby does not want another middleman, and an analyst noted that bulk automated searches cost airlines money.
