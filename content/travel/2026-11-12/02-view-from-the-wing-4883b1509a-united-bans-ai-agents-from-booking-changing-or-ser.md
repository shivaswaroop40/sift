United's contract of carriage revision of 11 November adds AI agents to its prohibited ticketing practices. Rule 6.J.6 bars using them to make, modify or service a ticket, or to buy any product or service. It covers agents reaching United through a travel agency, and human supervision or approval does not exempt them. Rule 5.H lets United cancel reservations made that way.

The Wall Street Journal says agents can still show United flights and prices. A customer who researches with AI and then buys on their own does not break the rules. CEO Scott Kirby said he does not want to create "another middleman". United also cites privacy, unintended purchases and regulatory requirements.

For travellers who use assistants to compare fares or handle bookings, United is the first big carrier to say that is not allowed. Enforcement looks hard. If an agent completes a purchase on Expedia, United sees only an Expedia booking. The author notes that United says its systems can identify booking sources, but argues a contractual ban is not a technical one.

The author sees real concerns about bargaining power, accountability for errors and search load. He calls the safety argument weak, since the hazardous-materials rule does not require manual completion of every step. He compares it with American's retreat from denying mileage on some agency bookings. Agent bookings are said to be rare for now.
