Qantas has taken its Boeing 787-9 Dreamliners off three international routes this year, according to Cirium data cited by Simple Flying. Brisbane to Auckland switched to the older Airbus A330-200 in May. Sydney to Honolulu moved to A330-200 and A330-300 aircraft from October. Sydney to Dallas/Fort Worth moved to the Airbus A380 from September.

Dallas/Fort Worth is still served by the 787-9 from Melbourne. The A380 on the Sydney route runs daily. Qantas has 10 A380s with 485 seats: 14 first, 70 business, 60 premium economy and 341 economy. The 787-9 has been redeployed to other trunk routes, though the article does not say which ones gained capacity.

For travellers, the aircraft on a route decides the cabin you get. Dallas from Sydney now offers first class and a larger premium economy cabin. Honolulu and Brisbane-Auckland move to A330s, which are older. Check the aircraft type before booking, because the swaps are tied to specific months.

Competition on Brisbane-Auckland is growing. Air New Zealand, China Airlines, Jetstar and Solomon Airlines already fly it, and Qatar Airways plans a fifth freedom service from next June. The article gives no reason for the Qantas cuts beyond adjusting to market demand.
