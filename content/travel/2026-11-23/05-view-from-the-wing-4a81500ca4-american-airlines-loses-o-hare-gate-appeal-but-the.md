A US appeals court has upheld the refusal of a preliminary injunction that American Airlines sought to undo Chicago O'Hare's gate reshuffle. The 19 November ruling does not decide whether the city broke the 2018 lease. It only finds that the trial judge did not abuse his discretion in denying emergency relief.

The dispute turns on timing. American says the lease promised three extra gates on the L concourse extension, and that its protected twelve months should start only once they opened. Its last gate opened on 14 March 2025. Chicago counted from December 2023 and redistributed gates in 2025. The trial judge read the lease as unambiguous and found no such condition.

The appeals court said American had not shown that losing gates would destroy its hub. It would fly the same schedule from fewer gates, which is a measurable cost. United projected over 2,000 cancellations if the changes were blocked, which the author calls overblown. American also waited until July to seek relief, after suing in May.

The author argues that United's attempt to squeeze American out has not worked. American bought two gates from Spirit and regained space through its own growth. The source text is cut off before the Federal Aviation Administration's role is explained, so that part is not covered here.
