Delta Air Lines cancelled at least 92 mainline flights on Monday, about 2% of its schedule, by 7:15am EST. This followed a Sunday outage in a system that automates weight and balance calculations. Delta said it restored the system by late Sunday afternoon, but disruption at Atlanta spread to other airports.

On Sunday, roughly 6 in 10 Delta mainline flights were delayed, more than 2,100 departures, per FlightAware. At least 274 were cancelled, about 8%. Endeavor Air added over 300 delays. Delta said about 70% of disruption came from the tech problem. The rest came from weather and air traffic control delays.

Weather made it worse. Low cloud and poor visibility hit LaGuardia, where nearly 60% of flights were delayed and 42 were diverted, many to Philadelphia, Albany, Harrisburg and Syracuse. Rights depend on cause. For the tech outage, Delta may cover hotels, meals and ground transport in a major delay. For weather, compensation is less likely, so keep receipts for a possible card insurance claim.

A refund is due under US Department of Transportation policy for a cancelled or significantly delayed flight, but only if you choose not to travel. The source expects Sunday 29 November to be among the busiest flying days of the year. Last week's FAA technology outage led to more than 18,000 cancellations.
