Korean Air's chief marketing officer, Kenneth Chang, told Simple Flying that the airline's cabins now fill in the opposite order to before the pandemic. Premium seats sell out first and economy follows. Chang said people are travelling more on prestige than economy, and that their expectations on board are different as a result.

The airline says it has responded by raising its investment in premium meals and service. Cabin crew training was rebuilt rather than adapted, because new seats and fleets need a different service pattern. Wine and menus rotate quarterly. Long-haul Prestige Suites 2.0 flights have moved to dine-on-demand, though the source text cuts off before explaining how it works.

For travellers, the practical point is availability. If premium cabins fill first, last-minute upgrades and cheap premium fares become harder to find. Chang's claim comes from Korean Air's own booking data, which the article does not publish, so the scale of the shift is unclear.

The London route currently uses a 777-300ER with the older cabin. It is due to move to the 787-10 with Prestige Suites 2.0 after the merger with Korean Air in December. Prestige Suites 2.0 is a 1-2-1 layout with a 78-inch flat bed.
