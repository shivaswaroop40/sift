Ryanair has said it may impose temporary pay freezes as it faces a projected €2 billion ($2.24 billion) rise in its fuel bill for the 2028 financial year. The freeze, still under review, would suspend inflation and merit pay rises rather than salaries. It is one of several measures being weighed, alongside negotiations with airports, lower crewing ratios and wider use of AI in back-office roles.

The trigger is hedging. Some 80% of Ryanair's fuel for the 2027 financial year is fixed at $67 per barrel, but those cheaper contracts run out during 2027. Ryanair expects fuel prices to stay high unless the Middle East conflict is resolved swiftly, and says higher fares alone will not cover the gap. Fare rises from 2028 look likely.

The near-term outlook is better. Traffic is projected to grow 3% in the 2027 financial year, Q3 bookings are strong and fares are trending modestly ahead of the same quarter last year. Ryanair cautions that passengers are booking closer to departure, which limits its visibility, and that Q3 fares will depend heavily on close-in school holiday bookings.
