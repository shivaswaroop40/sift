Arun Bansal, chief executive of Adani Airport Holdings, said one or two international airlines may stop serving Mumbai for a couple of years rather than move their operations to Navi Mumbai International Airport. He made the comment on Adani Enterprises' second-quarter earnings call. He argued the pause would have almost no effect on total passenger numbers, and said talks with most international airlines are nearly done, with only a few still open.

For anyone booking flights to Mumbai, a paused carrier means one fewer direct option, possibly for a couple of years, and journeys routed through other hubs. Longer term, if well-known long-haul carriers stay away, Navi Mumbai's international network could be mostly Indian airlines, which narrows choice for global travellers. The shift forms part of a wider operational change at Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport, which Adani also runs.

The detail is thin. Bansal did not name the airlines or give dates for the shift. The reassurance on passenger numbers comes from the airport operator itself, not from the carriers. The article is also short and much of it sits behind a paywall.
