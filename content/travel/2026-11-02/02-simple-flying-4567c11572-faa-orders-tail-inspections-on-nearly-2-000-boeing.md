The Federal Aviation Administration has issued an airworthiness directive covering nearly 2,000 Boeing 737 Next Generation aircraft. Multiple operators reported incidents in which the jets' noses moved up and down during flight. The directive targets excessive movement in the hinges and the jackscrew that support and move the horizontal stabiliser in the tail.

The hinges and jackscrew sit in the tail and move the horizontal stabiliser, so excessive movement there speaks directly to the nose-up, nose-down behaviour the operators reported. Nearly 2,000 jets are in scope. Travellers booked on a 737NG may well be flying an aircraft now due for these checks.

The report as available gives the fleet count, the reported symptom and the parts named in the directive. It does not state the compliance deadline, the inspection procedure or any effect on flight schedules. Those details are not yet confirmed here.
