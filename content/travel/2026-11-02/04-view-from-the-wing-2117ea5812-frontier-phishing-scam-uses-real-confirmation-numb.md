Frontier Airlines passengers are receiving phishing emails that quote their real flight details and confirmation numbers. The messages promise refunds or claim a booking has not been paid, then ask for card details. Frontier has reported an active campaign. Passengers say the confirmation numbers matched actual bookings, including trips already taken and travel still ahead. One refund offer was about five times what the customer had paid.

The genuine booking data makes the emails persuasive, and some passengers have responded with card numbers. One tried several other cards before the fake site accepted the card originally used to book. A rejection message does not mean the number was not collected. Web forms can capture typed details before submission.

Where the reservation data came from is unclear. Frontier disclosed an October 2 security incident affecting its customer care database, and a breach affecting 11,482 people was reported in July. Security firm VenariX analysed leaked samples and found booking codes, flight numbers and dates, plus evidence of overly broad access permissions on Microsoft customer service portals.

Frontier says it never emails customers to reconfirm a reservation, re-enter card details or supply payment information for a refund. Trips should be checked through the airline's app or by typing its website address directly. Anyone who entered a card should contact the issuer and lock every card tried, including ones the page said it rejected. Changing a frequent flyer password may not close the route, since a confirmation code and last name can still open changes and cancellations.
