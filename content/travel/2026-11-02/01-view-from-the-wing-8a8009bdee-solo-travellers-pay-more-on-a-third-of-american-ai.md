A fare comparison by Thrifty Traveler found solo passengers paying more on 33.5% of American Airlines flights and 10.6% of United flights. The study covered 5,000 domestic nonstop one-way fares, 1,000 on each of the five largest US carriers. The average gap was over $90 on American and over $64 on United. No such gap appeared on the Delta, Alaska and Southwest flights checked. On one Miami to Charlotte flight, a solo traveller paid $496 while two people on one reservation paid $155 each.

The habit of searching for one passenger first can now hide cheaper fares. Some low fares carry an accompaniment restriction and appear only when at least two people book together. American calls its version Multi-Pax Fares. The fix is to search with the real number of travellers, then compare the total price against a solo search before booking.

Airlines use party size as a proxy for trip type, assuming solo bookers are business travellers whose employer pays. The gap was most common on routes with no nonstop competitor, especially in American's Charlotte and Philadelphia markets. Buying two tickets and cancelling one may not work. American's rules say the remaining ticket must be repriced when a cancellation leaves one passenger on a multi-passenger fare.
