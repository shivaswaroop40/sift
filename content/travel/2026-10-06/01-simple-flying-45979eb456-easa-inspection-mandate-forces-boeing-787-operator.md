European regulators have imposed a hard inspection limit at 7,800 flight cycles for low-pressure turbine discs on all Boeing 787 variants equipped with Rolls-Royce Trent 1000 engines. The Airworthiness Directive AD 2026-0117R1, effective August 19, requires engine removal and full teardown for inspection when this threshold is reached.

For long-haul operators, this matters because it cuts short the operational life Rolls-Royce promised through costly durability upgrades. The manufacturer invested over £1 billion to extend engine life, doubling or tripling how long powerplants could stay on the wing, but the new inspection limit forces removal before the redesigned hot-section cores reach their potential.

Inspecting the affected discs requires complete engine removal from the wing and disassembly of the six-stage low-pressure turbine at an MRO facility. Technicians use fluorescent penetrant and eddy-current testing to detect micro-cracking, a labour-intensive process that will strain already tight maintenance capacity and delay high-value long-haul routes.
