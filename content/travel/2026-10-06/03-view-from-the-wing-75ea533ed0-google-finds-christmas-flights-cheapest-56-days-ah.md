Google's analysis of five years of flight data shows Thanksgiving fares hit their lowest point 34 days before departure, and Christmas fares 56 days out. But these historical averages may not apply when airlines adjust schedules in response to higher fuel costs and change how much discount inventory they release.

For independent travellers, the practical lesson is to watch prices from several months out, buy when fares look reasonable against your expectations, and choose tickets that let you claim a credit if the price drops. The specific booking date matters little; departing on a weekday rather than weekend saves far more.

Airlines forecast passenger behaviour and adjust their pricing in response. If travellers start booking later than historical patterns suggest, airlines may protect cheap seats for those later bookings instead of releasing them early. Fewer flights and higher fuel costs mean airlines have fewer discount tickets to offer overall.
