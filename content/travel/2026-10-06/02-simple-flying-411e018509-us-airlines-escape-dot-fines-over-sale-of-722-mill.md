The Department of Transportation has concluded a multi-year review into data privacy at major US airlines and found no violations, despite airlines selling access to over 722 million passenger travel records to federal agencies including Customs and Border Protection, the Department of Homeland Security, and the Internal Revenue Service.

This matters because the records were sold without warrants or court oversight, and included names, itineraries, and payment details. The practice has angered senators who argue the DOT's findings fall short and that airlines misused passenger information for profit.

The Airlines Reporting Corporation, a data broker jointly owned by six major carriers, ran the Travel Intelligence Program until shutting it down in November 2025 under pressure from privacy advocates and lawmakers.
