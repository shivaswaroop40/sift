Some British travellers say the EU's entry/exit system (EES) has wrongly recorded them as overstaying. Novelist Jojo Moyes was detained by French border guards after a holiday, though she had spent only 23 days in the EU over six months. Her May departure from Portugal was not logged, so the 180-day clock kept running. She was warned of a €10,000 fine and a two-year ban.

The fault affects people whose biometrics were taken on arrival but not on departure. The system then fails to show that they left. Jane Nolan and her husband spent 40 days in France in April, yet the EU's online tool shows zero days remaining. It does not say which record caused the result. Their passports were stamped by hand in June because France had suspended EES.

This matters if you use the 90 days in 180 allowance. A missing exit record can block a booking or lead to detention at the border. The Foreign Office says it is raising inaccurate and incomplete EES records with the European Commission. It advises carrying proof of when you last left the EU and raising anomalies with border officials.

You can check your remaining days by entering your passport number on the EES website, which covers visits since 10 April 2026. If the total looks wrong, you can ask for a correction before travelling through the Travel to Europe data protection page. Moyes says she has tried every route without success and has hired an immigration lawyer.
