Turkey earned 5% more from tourism in the third quarter from 1% fewer visitors. The tourism minister, Mehmet Ersoy, credited longer stays and higher overnight spending. Average stays rose to 10.7 nights from 10.2. Spending per night rose 6.7% to $107, and total spend per visitor rose about 12% to $1,145.

Hotels did not drive the gain. Lighthouse data put the average hotel rate at $148 a night in dollar terms, down fractionally from $149 a year earlier. Package tours made up 32.3% of tourism income, food and beverage 20.5% and accommodation 12.5%, according to the Turkish Statistical Institute.

For travellers, this suggests Turkey is not getting dearer in hotel terms, even as visitors spend more overall. Money is going on packages, meals and other costs rather than rooms. Skift's own take is that the lowered $65 billion annual target could be met even if fourth-quarter revenue fell 9%, so hitting it says little about a recovery.
