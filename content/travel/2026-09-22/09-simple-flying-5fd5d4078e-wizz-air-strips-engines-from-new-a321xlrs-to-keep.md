Three brand-new Wizz Air Airbus A321XLRs are parked at Bydgoszcz airport in Poland without engines, while close to two dozen of its A321neos also sit grounded. Wizz Air has pulled the Pratt & Whitney PW1100G engines from the XLRs and fitted them to standard A321neos, because Pratt & Whitney cannot repair or produce enough engines to keep the wider fleet flying.

This matters because it signals how severe the PW1100G supply crunch has become for one of Europe's biggest operators. Wizz Air holds 269 aircraft in service and nearly ten percent of its A321neo fleet is parked for engine reasons, so the airline is cannibalising its newest jets to keep older ones revenue-generating.

The XLRs were ordered for the now-defunct Abu Dhabi subsidiary, which Wizz Air shut down in July 2025 citing geopolitical and operational issues. With only five XLRs flying mostly short European routes, and 36 of 47 original orders converted to the A321neo, the XLR no longer fits the carrier's network. The XLR is also heavier and burns more fuel than the A321neo on two to three-hour sectors.
