The Refuges d'Art is a 10-day, 150km hiking route through the Haute-Provence UNESCO geopark, starting in Digne-les-Bains. Seven restored shepherd huts, farmhouses and chapels each contain a site-specific work by land artist Andy Goldsworthy and can be booked free of charge as overnight shelters.

Three dry-stone cairns known as sentinels stand along the route. Each was built without mortar or cement, relying on the weight and shape of local stones. The refuges are unfurnished beyond sleeping platforms and need a key collected from Maison Alexandra David-Neel in Digne-les-Bains.

The walk is graded as a grande randonnée and involves real elevation gain. Trail markers are white and red horizontal lines. The author found sections steep enough to panic a first-time backpacker carrying 9kg, though the full route can be broken into shorter sections visiting one to three refuges.

Overnight stays come with basics but no electricity or running water. Hikers bring sleeping mats, food and water, and should expect cold nights, wildlife visitors and the kind of logistical planning that makes the experience closer to wild camping than a hotel stay.
