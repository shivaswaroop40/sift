An FAA communications system outage in Philadelphia triggered more than 1,100 flight cancellations across the US by late Monday evening, according to FlightAware data cited by The Points Guy. Newark Liberty bore the worst impact, losing more than half of its planned flights, while Philadelphia saw roughly a fifth of departures shuttered.

Ground stops were also issued for the three major New York hubs and Boston Logan, stranding passengers and diverting aircraft. United Airlines, which runs a major Newark operation, had logged more than 350 cancellations by Monday evening, with United, American, Delta and Southwest all issuing flexible rebooking waivers covering Newark and Philadelphia.

Travelers affected remain entitled to a full refund under US Department of Transport rules even though the disruption was beyond airline control, which can free them to switch carriers or abandon the trip. Airlines are unlikely to cover hotel or meal costs for ATC-related delays, though credit card travel insurance may reimburse some expenses if receipts are kept.

Operations could spill into Tuesday as carriers reposition aircraft and crews, so affected passengers should monitor their airline app rather than queue at the desk or hold for an agent.
