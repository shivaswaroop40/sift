Midway Atoll Airport, the remote Pacific strip known by code MDY, faces an uncertain future because maintaining the runway would cost nine figures and only a few dozen residents use it. The article explores the debate over whether to keep it open.

The airport matters well beyond the island. It serves as a critical emergency diversion point for transpacific flights, including the world's longest commercial route. If it closes, airlines lose a key safety margin and may need to reroute or carry extra fuel, raising costs across the Pacific.

The piece also weighs the political and conservation interests at stake, since Midway is a national wildlife refuge. The trade-off is between the cost of upkeep and the strategic value of having a runway available for diversions and oceanic crossings.
