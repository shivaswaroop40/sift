Henderson Field on Midway Atoll has no scheduled passenger service but serves as the main ETOPS diversion airport for transpacific flights, including widebody services from US West Coast and Asian carriers such as Zipair, Japan Airlines and Qantas. The FAA is weighing a $100 million upgrade covering runway milling, repaving and seawall repairs, with officials warning the runway could reach severe deterioration within five years.

If the work does not happen and the airport loses its ETOPS alternate status, airlines would need to reroute flights, adding fuel burn and time. Carriers have told the FAA that closures of Midway would push up fares, and Qantas has warned its planned Project Sunrise ultra-long-haul services would be disrupted without a suitable diversion in range.

The airport is also the only practical diversion option for hundreds of miles in either direction, with at least three documented emergency widebody landings since 2011, including a Hawaiian A330 in 2021 and a United 777 and Delta 747-400 earlier. The FAA has invited aviation stakeholder comments as it decides whether to fund the upgrade.
