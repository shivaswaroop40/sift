Villa Vie Residences, a company that sold lifetime residences on its cruise ship, has halted operations after running out of money. Residents were given just hours of notice before being forced off the vessel in Cambodia. The halt leaves them without the home they had bought at sea.

The collapse matters to anyone drawn to permanent cruising, a niche that has grown as cruising itself has become popular. Buyers purchase a residence onboard and then depend on the operator staying solvent for the rest of their lives. This case shows how quickly that arrangement can fail, and how little notice residents receive when it does.

The available report does not say how many residents were aboard, what they paid for their homes, or whether any refunds are being offered. It also cuts off before explaining how the money ran out or what the company has told buyers since. Those details will matter to anyone weighing a similar purchase.
