Condor is moving away from secondary US cities such as San Antonio and Baltimore toward larger markets. The shift follows Lufthansa's decision to end a long-standing agreement that fed connecting passengers to Condor at Frankfurt, a dispute a court settled in Lufthansa's favour in 2025. Condor's sole US addition is a daily Frankfurt to Chicago O'Hare service from 14 May to 12 September 2027, flown with an Airbus A330-900 seating 30 in business, 64 in premium economy and 216 in economy.

The change matters for route planning. Nonstops between Germany and mid-size US cities will become rarer, though Anchorage and Portland continue because both are Alaska Airlines hubs. Condor also signed an interline agreement with American Airlines in September, allowing single-ticket bookings and bag transfers at Boston, Los Angeles, JFK and, from May, O'Hare. Reciprocal frequent flyer benefits are not yet part of that deal.

Condor needs connecting traffic to fill its large long-haul fleet at Frankfurt. It has added its own flights to Barcelona, Gatwick and Venice from Frankfurt this year, and holds a similar interline deal with Southwest. Alaska Atmos Rewards and JetBlue TrueBlue members can already earn and redeem points on Condor. Chief executive Peter Gerber concedes the airline will never match Lufthansa's network breadth, and sees long-haul growth in Latin America, especially Mexico.
