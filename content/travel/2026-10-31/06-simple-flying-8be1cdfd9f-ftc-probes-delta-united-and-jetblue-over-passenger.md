The US Federal Trade Commission has ordered Delta, United and JetBlue to explain how they set fares, giving them 45 days to disclose whether customer data plays any role. The orders were issued under Section 6(b) of the FTC Act. The three carriers are among nine companies under scrutiny, alongside Uber, Lyft, DoorDash, Instacart, Target and Walmart. The probe covers base fares, discounts, paid upgrades, targeted sales and loyalty programme membership.

For frequent flyers, the loyalty angle matters most. Delta SkyMiles, United MileagePlus and JetBlue TrueBlue are specifically named. FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson said consumers deserve to know whether companies use web surfing habits or other personal data to estimate the highest price each person can be charged. JetBlue also faces a class action after advising a customer hit with a $230 fare increase to clear cookies or book in an incognito window.

All three airlines deny personalised pricing. Delta says its AI tool, built with Israeli firm Fetcherr, receives only route-level demand and market information. JetBlue says fares depend on demand and seat availability. United has not commented. The FTC also lacks direct enforcement power over airlines; any penalty would fall to the Department of Transportation. Senator Ruben Gallego's One Fair Price Act, now before Congress, would ban surveillance pricing and give the FTC that authority.
