A View from the Wing analysis disputes a New York Times essay by former Spirit executive Mark Kahan that blames airline credit card programmes for Spirit Airlines' failure. The piece argues Spirit itself raised $1.45 billion through loyalty-backed debt in 2020 and 2022, using the same mechanism available to larger carriers.

Spirit was already losing $72.7 million a month in November 2025, months before the Iran war and jet fuel spike. Its operating cost per available seat mile rose 41.5% between 2019 and 2025, while its product fell behind rivals on schedule, seats and recovery options.

The article also contests Kahan's claim that Spirit's exit raised fares 23.4% by August. Most of that rise came before Spirit's final flight on May 2 and reflects rising fuel costs, not lost competition. July fares remained roughly 20% below the 2016 inflation-adjusted average.

The author previously argued in 1991 that frequent flyer miles should be banned, a position that would reduce flights and connectivity at network carriers like Southwest and Delta.
