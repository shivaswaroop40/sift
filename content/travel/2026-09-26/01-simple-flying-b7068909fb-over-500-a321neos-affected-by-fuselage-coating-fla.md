Airbus has confirmed a coating flaw on more than 500 A321neo aircraft, affecting around 250 jets already in service and another 250 in production. The problem is an excessively thin layer of anti-corrosion primer on stringers in the lower forward fuselage, traced to Italian sub-tier supplier TESI, which works for Leonardo.

Airbus describes the issue as a quality concern rather than a safety one, and says a fix is available without disassembling the stringers. Airlines are continuing to take delivery of affected aircraft and have been told there is no immediate requirement to carry out repairs. The A321XLR is not among the affected types.

The news comes as Airbus pushes to hit around 870 commercial deliveries in 2026, with 475 already handed over between January and August. A321neo output is being raised from about 60 to 75 aircraft a month by the end of 2027. A similar panel-thickness issue at Sofitec Aero in late 2025 forced Airbus to cut its 2025 delivery target by around 30 aircraft.
