Turkish Airlines is preparing to add Brisbane and Perth to its Australian network, initially via Singapore and later nonstop from Istanbul when its Airbus A350-1000s enter service around mid-2027. A spokesperson told Turkey Today that the two new gateways are the next step in the carrier's longer-term expansion plan.

The routes matter because Turkish currently uses only eight of its 35 weekly traffic rights to Australia, leaving plenty of room to grow. Existing Sydney and Melbourne services have load factors above 85% and passenger numbers up 19% year on year, suggesting strong demand that new nonstop options could pull further from competitors.

Sydney will switch its one-stop routing from Kuala Lumpur to Singapore on October 26. Until the A350-1000 is delivered, any Brisbane or Perth flights would also rely on a Singapore connection, keeping journey times long.

Booking data shows Turkish's Australian passengers mostly connect to Western European cities such as London, Paris, Rome and Athens, so Brisbane and Perth would lean more on that traffic than the Balkans and Turkey that dominate the current Sydney and Melbourne flows.
