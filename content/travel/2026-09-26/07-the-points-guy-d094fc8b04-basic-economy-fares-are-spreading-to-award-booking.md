Delta, JetBlue and Southwest now sell basic economy award tickets, joining Delta which has offered them since the late 2010s. JetBlue started its basic TrueBlue bookings last year, and Southwest's shift to basic economy coincided with opening those fares to Rapid Rewards redemptions.

The lowest mileage price shown in a search is often a basic fare rather than a standard one. A JetBlue Boston to Orlando flight in December displayed 16,100 TrueBlue points for basic, with a standard economy ticket costing over 19,000 points, a 22% increase. A Delta Atlanta to Seattle flight showed 28,100 miles for basic, rising to over 35,000 miles for regular coach, a 27% jump.

Restrictions mirror those on paid basic fares. Passengers cannot pick seats, may be assigned a middle seat at check-in, lose lounge access even with eligible credit cards, and Delta basic travellers do not earn Medallion Qualifying Dollars. Award flexibility also drops: Delta charges 9,900 miles to cancel a domestic basic SkyMiles award, and JetBlue charges a $150 change or cancellation fee.

American, United and Alaska do not currently offer basic economy award redemptions. The piece notes basic business, domestic first and premium economy fares exist for cash bookings but not yet for points, and flags this as a likely next step.
