A US watchdog has found the FAA does not have a reliable cost estimate or integrated master schedule for its air traffic control modernisation, despite Congress having already provided $12.5 billion for the work. A September 14 GAO report identified 11,389 separate project schedules that were not coordinated, even when work was happening at the same facility.

The FAA is now asking for around $30 billion more. Without proper scheduling, installations risk disrupting controllers and the wider system. Roughly half of legacy copper wiring has been replaced so far, and a separate data communications deployment is running 51 months late, partly due to the 2019 shutdown and Covid.

The column argues long-term capital planning cannot work under annual congressional appropriations and recommends funding the air navigation service provider directly through user charges, similar to Canada's NAV CANADA model, which has operated since 1996 as a user-funded nonprofit. It also calls for separating the operator role from the safety regulator.

The underlying claim that current management is failing rests on GAO findings rather than independent data, and no alternative cost figure has been published.
