Jet fuel prices rose 102% year on year in mid-September 2026, yet record revenues have not fully covered the increase. Spirit Airlines failed in May after a second bankruptcy, while large North American carriers remained profitable through tight capacity and premium pricing. The impact has been uneven across the industry.

For independent travellers, this matters because it reshapes which airlines survive and how they compete. Margins now depend on five external relationships: aircraft manufacturers controlling delivery slots, unions locking in labour costs for five years, premium cabin demand, airport infrastructure investment, and bank partnerships funding loyalty programmes. Airlines have little direct control over any of them.

Manufacturers hold particular leverage until roughly 2033, when meaningful aircraft delivery slots become available again. Meanwhile, premium cabins now generate 24% of global airline passenger revenue, up sharply since 2020, creating both opportunity and oversupply risk. Labour costs are now the fixed base that every capacity plan must clear first.
