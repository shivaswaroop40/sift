Southwest Airlines plans to move to a rolling booking window of about 11 months, according to a report by Enilria. View from the Wing says it has seen the plan nowhere else. Southwest currently sells through 29 September 2027, with the next batch due on 17 December, opening flights to 6 November 2027, which is 324 days out.

Under batch releases the window shrinks each day until the next extension. A rolling calendar would give roughly the same horizon whenever you shop. That helps with fixed-date trips such as family holidays or cruises, and you would not need to watch for release announcements.

International partners also gain. Southwest lists 12, including Icelandair, EVA Air, Singapore Airlines and Turkish Airlines. If Southwest has not opened a date, a partner may sell a connection on another US airline instead. EVA Air sells up to 360 days ahead, so a window of about 330 days would narrow the gap without closing it.

There are caveats. Booking further out means more exposure to schedule changes, and the first day of sale is not necessarily the cheapest. Basic fare credits expire six months after purchase, so cancelling an 11-month Basic booking late yields nothing. Points bookings are refunded if cancelled at least 10 minutes before departure.
