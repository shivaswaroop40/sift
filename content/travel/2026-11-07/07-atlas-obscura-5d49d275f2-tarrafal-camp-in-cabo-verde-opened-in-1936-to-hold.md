The Tarrafal camp opened in 1936, when around 150 prisoners arrived from Lisbon. They were communists, anarchists, trade unionists and sailors who had mutinied against Salazar's dictatorship. The site in Chão Bom, Cabo Verde, was chosen for its isolation, heat, scarce clean water and malaria. Prisoners called it the Campo da Morte Lenta, the Camp of Slow Death. More than 30 Portuguese inmates died there, including Bento Gonçalves, secretary-general of the Portuguese Communist Party.

This is a site for travellers who prefer places with a story over resorts. The camp's force is in its deliberate design. Isolation, heat, bad water and malaria were not accidents of location. They were the punishment. That mechanism, more than any monument, explains what the Portuguese dictatorship did to the people it feared.
