Major carriers now sell international-grade premium economy seats on sectors under six hours. Cathay Pacific schedules A350-900s with 28-seat premium economy cabins on the Hong Kong to Singapore trunk, a flight of roughly four hours, with 40 inches of pitch in a 2-4-2 layout. Air New Zealand flies 787-9s on three to four-hour trans-Tasman hops to Sydney and Melbourne with 41 inches of pitch. Singapore Airlines adds seasonal A380s on the same corridor, its shortest superjumbo route.

For travellers this is a middle option between expensive short-haul business class and a cramped economy cabin. Wider seats, extra legroom and upgraded meals come at a fraction of long-haul business fares. On a four-hour daytime flight a lie-flat bed matters less, so premium economy captures most of the comfort. Simple Flying rates Air New Zealand's trans-Tasman premium economy as the smartest buy on price versus comfort.

Availability is the catch. Cathay Pacific rotates regionally configured A330-300s and regional A350s onto the same intra-Asia routes, and those aircraft have no premium economy cabin. Singapore Airlines also flies medium-haul A350-900 variants in a two-class layout without it. Aircraft swaps and seasonal scheduling decide what is on offer, so check the daily equipment before booking.

The article covers six carriers in total. Delta Air Lines has brought its international-grade Premium Select cabin to select North American routes. Asian carriers use widebodies to solve regional slot constraints, while US legacy carriers mix repositioned widebodies with next-generation narrowbodies on transcontinental markets.
