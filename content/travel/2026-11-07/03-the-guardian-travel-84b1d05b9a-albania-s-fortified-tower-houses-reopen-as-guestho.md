A project by the Albanian Agritourism Association is turning the country's kullas, fortified stone tower houses, into guesthouses and linking them with a new long-distance walking route. The Tower Trail runs more than 100 miles through the Mirdita, Mat, Bulqiza and Dibra regions of north-eastern Albania, designed as a nine-day guided walk through villages, high plains, forests and river valleys. Cycling and 4x4 routes are planned.

The kullas were built from local limestone and wood to shelter families during conflict, including the 15th-century Ottoman invasion and tribal disputes. Many were confiscated under communism between 1944 and 1991, then abandoned as rural Albania emptied into the cities. The trail gives remaining villagers a reason to restore them. Kulla Hupi in Dushaj, opened in 2021 by former miner Luli Hupi, buys its meat, cheese, honey and bread from more than 10 local families, who also lead hikes and cooking classes.

The region is almost untouched by tourism, and hospitality customs run deep. Many villages on the route have only one or two families left, and accepting money from guests conflicts with old traditions, so accommodation remains limited. Trail markings are sporadic and some bushwhacking is needed, which is why the route is walked with a guide. Long stages can be split over two days or shortened with a car transfer. Free maps are available from the Infokulla centre in Rubik.
