IAG, British Airways' parent, disclosed the dispute in its 6 November results. The British tax authority wants 20% VAT charged when Avios are issued, regardless of what members redeem them for, and the claim reaches back to March 2018. IAG has €638 million (US$710 million) at stake. That covers €516 million of historical payments it expects to recover if it wins, plus €122 million disclosed as a contingent asset. A tribunal hearing is expected later in 2026, with a decision in 2027.

Members could pay even if the tax authority never bills them. VAT is collected through the value chain and ultimately passed to consumers. A bank that buys Avios for its credit card rewards may absorb the cost, buy fewer Avios, cut earning rates or initial bonuses, raise card fees, or steer spending to another product. IAG could also change award prices, cash charges or redemption availability to lower its cost of honouring rewards.

IAG historically paid VAT based on what members redeemed, and most redemptions were flights taxed at a zero rate. It says its treatment relied on an earlier ruling from the same authority and on case law, and it is contesting whether the government can reverse that treatment retrospectively. Some of the disputed payments are not recoverable from commercial partners, which could push IAG to reprice those agreements or revalue the points themselves.
