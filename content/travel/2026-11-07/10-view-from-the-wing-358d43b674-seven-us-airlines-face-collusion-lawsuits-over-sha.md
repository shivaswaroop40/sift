Two new class actions, Ward v. ATPCO in Seattle and Brown v. ATPCO in Virginia, accuse American, Delta, United, Southwest, Alaska, Hawaiian and JetBlue of using shared data to keep fares high. The suits also name ATPCO, the airline-owned fare distributor, and its 3Victors subsidiary. They come as government figures show airfares rose 22.4% over the past year.

The mechanism matters to anyone comparing fares. Iris pools shopping data from participating airlines. PriceEye flags differences in rivals' offers. DemandView reads search activity to reveal demand before it becomes bookings. The suits argue this exposes offers that never sold, so a carrier that might discount to fill seats knows rivals will spot and match the cut almost immediately, and skips it instead.

The writer doubts the practices break the law. The same alerts can push fares down by warning an airline it is being underpriced, and better demand data can justify extra flights. Inflation-adjusted fares remain below pre-pandemic levels. To prove a Sherman Act violation, plaintiffs must reach discovery and find an agreement. If the cases go far, a settlement with money and tighter data-sharing limits is the likely outcome.
