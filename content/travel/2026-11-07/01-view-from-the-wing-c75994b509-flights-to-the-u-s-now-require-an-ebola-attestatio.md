The United States now requires an Ebola attestation before boarding any flight from abroad. The rule took effect for departures at or after 11:59 p.m. Eastern on 3 November 2026 and applies across airlines, including foreign carriers. U.S. citizens, green card holders, visitors and connecting passengers must each declare they have not been in the Democratic Republic of the Congo in the past 21 days. Domestic flights and flights from U.S. territories such as Puerto Rico are excluded. The order runs to 18 December 2026.

For most travellers this is one more check-in step, and the mechanics matter at the airport. The attestation is a declaration only, with no test or vaccination certificate. American Airlines collects it through its app and website during check-in, but its kiosks do not support it and will show a QR code directing you to the app. Paper forms and verbal attestations recorded by an agent are also accepted. Each traveller, including children, needs one, and a parent can complete it for a child.

The declaration can stop a trip. Anyone physically present in Congo in the past 21 days, even in transit, can be denied boarding, and U.S. passports are no exception. Day 22 after leaving is the first eligible boarding date. Leave Congo on 1 November and you can fly from 23 November. Uganda and South Sudan have separate rules. Non-citizens, including green card holders, recently in either must also wait outside the United States, while citizens return only through JFK, Dulles or Atlanta for screening.

The CDC counts 10,563 confirmed cases and 5,130 confirmed deaths through 1 November, almost all in Congo, the second-largest Ebola outbreak recorded. It is caused by the Bundibugyo virus, for which no approved vaccine or specific treatment exists. Ebola spreads through blood and body fluids, not through the air, and is not believed to spread before symptoms appear. The CDC reports no U.S. cases and considers introduction very unlikely.
