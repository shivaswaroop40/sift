The Netherlands Authority for Consumers and Markets investigated over 14 million airline transactions and found extensive dynamic pricing but little personalised pricing based on previous searches or device type. Prices changed frequently, but not because customers had searched before.

For independent travellers, this matters because you can search multiple times without triggering higher fares from the airline. The price swings you see reflect real changes in demand, competitor moves and inventory, not tracking of your behaviour.

Airlines have no economic incentive to raise fares when you've searched but not bought. A lower price might persuade you to book; a higher one sends you to a competitor. Yet 46 per cent of surveyed consumers still believe previous searches affect their price.

Logging out, using incognito mode or a VPN will not find cheaper tickets. Fares depend on trip parameters and available inventory, not your identity. Changing countries may expose different local fares, but that is not punishment for searching twice.
