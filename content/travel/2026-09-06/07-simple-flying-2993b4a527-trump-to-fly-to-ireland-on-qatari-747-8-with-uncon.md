Donald Trump is scheduled to fly to Ireland on September 12 to 13 aboard the ex-Qatari Boeing 747-8 gifted to the US, despite the aircraft not being confirmed as having full defensive countermeasures. The trip is his first international flight on the jet since an Iranian threat forced a secret plane switch in Ankara.

The visit to County Clare and Dublin comes weeks after security concerns prompted a switch to a C-32A mid-flight, with passengers left unaware. White House officials say the aircraft meets all presidential transport requirements, but security experts question whether its upgrades match those of the existing Air Force One fleet.

The $400 million VC-25B is being used as a stopgap while Boeing builds two new presidential 747-8s, expected for delivery in 2027 or 2028. The gifted jet is destined for Trump's presidential library once those replacements arrive.

The original Air Force One jets currently in service remain the more proven platform. Travellers tracking US presidential movements may see airspace and route changes around September 12 to 13, particularly on transatlantic corridors into Shannon or Dublin.
