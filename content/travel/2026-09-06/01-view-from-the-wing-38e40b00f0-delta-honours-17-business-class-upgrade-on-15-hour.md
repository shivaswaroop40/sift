A passenger booked a Delta One upgrade from Atlanta to Seoul for $17.34, or 1,700 SkyMiles, after the price collapsed from an earlier offer of $1,671. The fare drop followed several itinerary changes that shifted the return into Delta's lowest business class bucket and the outbound into a more expensive premium economy fare, narrowing the cabin difference.

Delta allowed the booking, the passenger flew seat 10D and posted photos of the meal service. The result suggests the airline's upgrade algorithm will accept almost any positive amount rather than leave a premium seat empty, even on long-haul international routes where upgrade prices once started around $599.

Long-haul upgrade offers have fallen sharply in recent years. Readers have reported Delta One upgrades to Paris and Seattle for $299 or less, New York to Paris for $50, and Amsterdam to Minneapolis for $87. Paid business class fares have risen at the same time, with sub-$2,000 Europe roundtrips now rare.

The exact mechanism behind the $17.34 price remains unclear. Commenters suggested a misplaced decimal, a glitch after multiple rebookings, or a revenue system designed to capture any revenue above zero. Delta has not commented publicly on whether the fare was intentional.
