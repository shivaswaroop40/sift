United Airlines has reportedly sent a memo to its 28,000+ flight attendants warning that deliberately triggering or "gaming" flight delays to collect pay protection will be treated as "impermissible and fraudulent" misconduct and could lead to termination.

The practice in question involves flight attendants taking actions such as refusing to work on the ground in order to extend a delay and trigger the contractual pay protection that kicks in after a set threshold.

The story was flagged by the aviation industry site PYOK and United has not publicly detailed how widespread the issue is or how it would detect such behaviour, though the wording of the memo suggests management sees it as a real enough problem to justify a blanket warning.

Passengers are not directly affected by the memo, but it offers a rare look at the internal pressure points between crew scheduling, union contracts and airline operations during irregular operations.
