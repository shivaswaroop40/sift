A drone and a large stratospheric cell tower balloon, owned by US aerospace firm Sceye and travelling from Japan back to New Mexico, were spotted near San Diego International Airport on 4 September, grounding departures and arrivals from about 2:00 PM. A ground stop was issued until midnight, though air traffic resumed around 4:30 PM, with the backlog still affecting hundreds of flights by 8:00 PM.

For independent travellers, the incident shows how a single unidentified object at a constrained airport can ripple through an entire day's schedule. San Diego operates the country's busiest single runway, leaving little room to recover lost slots, and the airport is a focus city for Southwest and Alaska Airlines, two carriers many flyers use for connections.

Around 400 flights were delayed in total. The TSA is investigating the drone, as police could not locate its owner. Operators resuming after the all-clear were warned to fly at their own risk, suggesting airspace confidence had not fully returned despite the official clearance.

Saturday operations were unaffected, but the episode highlights ongoing vulnerability of busy airports to unauthorised drone activity, a problem that continues to cause costly disruption across the industry.
