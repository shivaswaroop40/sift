Boeing CEO Kelly Ortberg has disclosed that around 30 already-built 777X aircraft stored at Seattle Paine Field will need a 'change incorporation' rework before delivery. Older airframes need structural changes; newer ones require mainly software and systems updates, with the full programme expected to run over several years.

The rework reflects design changes made during the protracted certification flight test programme. Boeing continued building the widebody to keep the assembly line running and to supply test airframes, but those early-production units now sit in storage waiting to be updated to the final certified configuration, which is why Ortberg has called the effort a 'massive activity'.

First 777-9 deliveries remain targeted for 2027, with the certification programme now in TIA phase 4a. The scale of the rework raises fresh questions about the real cost of Boeing's build-and-store approach for launch customers Emirates and Lufthansa, both of whom have large 777X orders.

Boeing has set up a dedicated team to standardise all stored jets to a common configuration before applying final changes. The source notes this is happening against a backdrop of recent engineering workforce reductions, which may complicate delivery timing.
