JSX CEO Alex Wilcox said the carrier will not launch planned service to Telluride and Truckee Tahoe until at least summer 2027, because supply chain and maintenance problems delayed the rollout of its ATR 42-600 fleet. The fourth ATR only entered service on August 30. Telluride and Tahoe both restrict jet operations, so turboprops are the only option for JSX there.

The delay matters because both airports are otherwise hard for JSX to serve, and the carrier has been using the ATRs on its Santa Monica network for nine months. ATR's own data puts JSX's turboprop net promoter score 2.5 points above its Embraer regional jets. Wilcox called the result promising but said the test was not yet conclusive on aircraft versus route effect.

A direct ATR-versus-ERJ comparison began on September 14 on a Hollywood Burbank to Las Vegas flight. The turboprop takes about ten minutes longer than the jet on the sector. Results from that A/B test will shape JSX's decision on whether to order up to 25 more ATR 42-600s, a call Wilcox now expects by mid-2027 rather than year-end.

Separately, JSX has placed a deposit for 50 hybrid-electric Heart Aerospace ES-36 turboprops, which seat up to 36 in a single class. Wilcox said certification is targeted for 2031 and that the ES-36 could cut operating costs by roughly 40 percent compared with fossil-fuelled planes. Air Canada and United have also placed orders for the type.
