Beginning 19 October, a new US Department of Transportation rule moves several disruption types into the "outside the airline's control" bucket, where compensation commitments do not apply. Affected scenarios include some mechanical problems that must be fixed before flight, FAA-required repairs, medical emergencies, removal of unruly passengers, certain cyberattacks, aircraft damage from weather or debris, and baggage delays caused by airport-operated systems.

Crew shortages, fueling issues and most airline computer failures remain the carrier's responsibility, so the typical hotel-and-meal commitments continue to apply in those cases. The change matters for independent travellers who book with major US carriers, because the categories that now fall outside carrier responsibility cover situations many fliers actually experience, such as overnight mechanical delays.

Major US airlines including American, Delta, United, Southwest, JetBlue and Alaska have not announced any broad rollback of meal-voucher or hotel commitments, and carriers can still choose to help. Refund rights are unchanged, so a cancellation or a domestic delay of more than three hours still triggers a refund regardless of the cause. If you are stranded, ask the airline how the disruption is classified and keep receipts for any costs, since travel insurance or credit-card delay cover may reimburse you.
