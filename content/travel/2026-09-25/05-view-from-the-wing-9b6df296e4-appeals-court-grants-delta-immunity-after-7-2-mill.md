A federal appeals court has ruled 2-1 that Delta Air Lines is immune from civil liability after a 2015 incident in which a passenger with diarrhoea was handcuffed, exposed naked and denied the lavatory on a flight from Atlanta to Salt Lake City. A jury had previously awarded the passenger, Atef Bandary, $7.225 million for bodily injury and emotional harm.

The Ninth Circuit majority held that protection under the Aviation and Transportation Security Act covers conduct flowing from law enforcement decisions once a suspicious-activity report is made. The dissent noted that a prior jury had found Bandary had not behaved suspiciously, but the majority said that vacated verdict no longer carried weight.

For travellers, the ruling means airlines face little exposure for physical harm caused after crew contact authorities, even if the underlying report was disputed. The decision aligns the Ninth Circuit with the Second and Fifth Circuits on the scope of immunity, making it harder to hold carriers accountable for restraint-related injuries.
