Frontier Airlines CEO Jimmy Dempsey says major carriers are using credit card revenue to subsidise basic economy fares, undercutting his ultra-low-cost model. The piece argues this misreads airline economics, since loyalty revenue and flight revenue work together rather than one funding the other.

Basic economy is a long-standing pricing tool that segments leisure and business travellers through product restrictions, not a loss-leader propped up by credit card deals. Frontier itself contributed to the problem by forcing legacy carriers to adopt basic economy in the first place, stripping out the advance-purchase and Saturday-stay rules that once protected fares.

The real competitive problem is product. Frontier's own UpFront Plus product doubled revenue from the space it occupies, and the airline is adding first class and Starlink wifi. Dempsey's predecessor released code-first on first class by saying affluent leisure customers want a better product. Frontier still does not offer lie-flat seats or a global award network, and its first class will sell food and drinks rather than include them.

The article concludes that Frontier needs to give customers a reason to return beyond the lowest fare, while keeping costs down. The piece is analysis rather than a Frontier announcement, so the airline has not publicly responded to the specific framing.
