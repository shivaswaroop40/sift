Following the November 2025 Louisville crash of UPS Flight 2976, UPS took a $137 million after-tax write-off in January 2026 to retire its 27 McDonnell Douglas MD-11 freighters permanently. FedEx, by contrast, funded mandatory pylon inspections and will keep its MD-11s in service until January 2032, creating two opposite responses across the express cargo sector.

For independent travellers the main impact is on the routes and hubs that handle their packages. The split accelerated UPS deliveries of 18 Boeing 767-300Fs, with 15 arriving in 2026, and pushed heavy international lifts onto Boeing 777 freighters. Travellers shipping time-sensitive parcels through Louisville, Memphis or Cologne may see different routing, frequency and reliability in 2026 and beyond.

Express operators are not relying on one single replacement type. The 767-300F carries up to 116,100 lb over 3,225 nautical miles with 24 main-deck pallets and burns roughly 35% less fuel per flight hour than the MD-11, so two MD-11 trips are being covered by three 767 rotations. The 777F carries 224,900 lb over 4,970 nautical miles on 27 main-deck pallets, roughly 20% lower fuel burn than the MD-11, and fits transpacific and transatlantic lanes without Anchorage or Shannon stops.

Constraints worth noting: 777F production is tight, and upcoming ICAO noise and emissions rules are arriving before factory lines can replace retiring trijets at the rate carriers want.
