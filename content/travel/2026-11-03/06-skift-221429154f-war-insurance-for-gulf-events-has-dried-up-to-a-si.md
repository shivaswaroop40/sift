Event organisers in the Gulf can still buy cancellation cover for bad weather, a period of national mourning or an IT failure. War and political violence are excluded from standard policies and must be bought as a separate layer. Since the U.S.-Iran war began in late February, that layer has become very hard to find. Panteha Peters of broker Marsh Middle East and Africa says only one insurer, perhaps two on limited terms, will consider war exposure, case by case, and generally no earlier than about a week before the event.

For travellers, this means events in the Gulf are more fragile than their calendars suggest. Skift reports that organisers are postponing events or carrying the war risk themselves, and that Saudi Arabia's event calendar has been paying the price. The one-week underwriting window also pushes go-or-no-go decisions very late. Travellers who book flights and hotels around a Gulf event face dates that may move with little warning, so refundable rates and flexible fares are worth the premium.

The detail rests mainly on one broker's account. Marsh is the only source named, and the firm sells this coverage. The article is also truncated behind a paywall, and the industry executives cited are not quoted by name in the visible text. The wider market picture is therefore hard to judge from what is visible.
