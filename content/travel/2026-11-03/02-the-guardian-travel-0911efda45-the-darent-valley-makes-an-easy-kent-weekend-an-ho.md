The Guardian publishes a weekend guide to the Darent valley in Kent, covering Shoreham, Otford and Eynsford. A twice-hourly train from London Blackfriars takes about an hour. The painter Samuel Palmer lived in Shoreham in the 1820s and called the area his Valley of Vision. The River Darent, a 21-mile chalk stream, runs from Westerham to the Thames, with a waymarked path beside it.

The villages sit minutes apart by train, so a car is optional. A gentle four-mile stretch of the Darent Valley Path links Shoreham to Eynsford, and the train back takes four minutes. Sights include Lullingstone Roman villa with preserved painted walls, Lullingstone Castle, the ruins of a 16th-century palace at Otford, and Castle Farm, one of the UK's largest lavender growers, with a farm shop and summer picnics among the rows.

Costs are modest. Shoreham Aircraft Museum, a volunteer-run museum on second world war air combat, charges £5 and serves cream teas from the proprietor's kitchen. The Samuel Palmer pub's seasonal discovery menu costs £48 a head. White Hill Woods, five minutes' walk from Shoreham station, has a shepherd's hut from £100 a night and a log cabin from £150, both with a two-night minimum.
