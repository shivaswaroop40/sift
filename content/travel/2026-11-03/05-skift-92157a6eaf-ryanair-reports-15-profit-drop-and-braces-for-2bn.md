Ryanair, Europe's largest carrier, reported a 15% drop in half-year profits on Monday. It warned of pay freezes, slower recruitment and AI efficiencies as it braces for a €2 billion ($2.24bn) rise in its annual fuel bill in fiscal 2027/28, when its cheap fuel hedges run out. CEO Michael O'Leary told analysts that a pilot strike in April or May would save the airline money in the short term.

For travellers, the pressure points are fares and reliability. Ryanair is Europe's largest carrier, and its fuel bill is rising as cheap hedges run out, a cost pressure other airlines share. The article's assessment is that if the strongest airline in Europe is bracing for a difficult 2027, weaker rivals face a much harder winter. Possible pilot strikes next spring could also disrupt flights.

The strike comments are O'Leary's remarks to analysts rather than a confirmed plan. The source text is also cut off mid-sentence where it discusses the multi-year union agreements negotiated in recent years, so the terms of those deals and the shape of management's tougher stance are not stated.
