British Airways has stopped flying its Boeing 787 on five routes from London Heathrow this year, and a sixth ends this month. Abu Dhabi lost the type on March 18 and the route is now suspended. Jeddah ended completely on April 24. Austin, Las Vegas and Vancouver switched on October 24 and now fly A350-1000s, with 777s joining from 2027. At Newark, no 787 flights are scheduled beyond November 30.

The swaps change the cabins on sale. BA's 787-8 has 204 seats in a less premium layout, and the incoming A350-1000s and 777s carry different cabins. The freed aircraft are moving to new markets, including San Jose in Costa Rica, Tampa, a Johannesburg return and Madrid, a short hop flown mainly for freight with Iberia. A daily 787-9 to Melbourne via Kuala Lumpur starts in January 2027.

Between December 2026 and October 2027, the 787 is scheduled to serve 46 destinations from Heathrow, where all 43 of BA's Dreamliners are currently deployed. Two caveats apply. Abu Dhabi is theoretically due back next winter but is absent from the published schedule, which runs to October 31, 2027. And 787-10s will eventually operate from Gatwick too.
