Norse Atlantic Airways has cancelled all flights and stopped selling tickets after its Norwegian operating subsidiary said it would file for bankruptcy. The board of Norse Atlantic Airways AS concluded there was no longer a basis for continued operations. A local court will appoint a trustee to oversee the process.

The collapse followed quickly. The low-cost, long-haul carrier suspended flights a day earlier, and two days earlier it had described its financial position as "critical". Flights operated by both the Norwegian and British airlines have been cancelled, according to the source.

The filing does not cover the listed parent, Norse Atlantic ASA, or the UK operating subsidiary. Norse says it is still looking for a solution that would let the remaining parts of the business keep operating. The source text is cut off, so it does not say what that might involve.

Anyone holding a Norse booking will need to look at alternatives and at how to claim a refund. The text available does not cover refunds or rebooking. Low-cost long-haul fares between Europe and the US also lose a competitor.
