A Simple Flying explainer compares how crews plan North Atlantic crossings on the Airbus A321XLR and the Boeing 787. The 787 can hold an ETOPS rating of up to 330 minutes from a diversion airport on one engine. The A321 family is certified to ETOPS 180, or three hours.

The article puts the A321XLR's diversion circle at roughly 1,350 to 1,500 nautical miles, depending on single-engine speed and wind. The 787's circle is put at about 2,500 to 2,750 nautical miles. Planners must show every point on the filed track lies within 180 minutes of a suitable airport.

The piece says this can leave the A321XLR with fewer usable tracks on a given day. If weather closes an alternate such as Keflavík or Kangerlussuaq, its options shrink more than the 787's, which keeps extra airports in range.

The text also explains Equal Time Points, where the time to continue equals the time to return. They shift with wind, and separate ones are calculated for different failures such as engine-out or depressurisation. The source text is cut off before the depressurisation detail.
