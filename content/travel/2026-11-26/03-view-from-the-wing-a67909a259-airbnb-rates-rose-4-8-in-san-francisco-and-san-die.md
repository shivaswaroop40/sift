A November working paper studied entire-home Airbnb listings in San Francisco and San Diego after California required mandatory fees to appear in the headline price. It compared them with matched listings in Seattle and Chicago, using July 2023 to July 2025 data. Rates rose 4.8% relative to the comparison cities.

The price measure includes cleaning fees but excludes Airbnb's service fee and taxes. Excluding storm-affected comparison months cuts the estimated rise to 2.9%. Listings that leaned more on cleaning fees were more likely to leave the platform. Higher-quality listings were more likely to stay, and new listings charged lower fees and prices than incumbents.

The authors still conclude guests came out ahead, but only once the changing mix of listings is counted. Holding the available properties fixed, the model finds higher prices more than wipe out the direct gain from disclosure. Hotels are not included, and the welfare estimate rests on more assumptions than the observed rate rise.

For you, the headline price is now closer to what you pay, so comparing listings takes less arithmetic. Repeat guests to the same property may pay more. Airbnb made total price display standard worldwide in April 2025, generally before taxes, so the California result hints at what may follow elsewhere.
