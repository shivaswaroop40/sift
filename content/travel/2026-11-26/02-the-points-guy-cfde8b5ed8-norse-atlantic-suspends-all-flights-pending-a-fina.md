Norse Atlantic Airways, the Norway-based long-haul budget airline, suspended all flights on Thursday "pending clarification as to whether a viable financing solution" exists. It said there was no assurance one would be found. Flight tracking showed all its services cancelled late on Wednesday. Pakistan International Airlines, which contracts Norse for some flights, said it was given no prior notice.

At the time, Norse flew one US route, Orlando to London Gatwick. In summer it also ran New York JFK to Athens, Gatwick and Rome. At its 2024 peak it served five US destinations, including Las Vegas, Los Angeles and Miami, from European gateways.

This removes one of the last cheap options across the Atlantic. Play, which used a Keflavik hub, shut down in September 2025. Cheap transatlantic fares now rest on French Bee and the budget arms of larger groups: Discover, Level and Edelweiss Air. Less competition tends to mean fewer low fares.

Norse had called its finances "critical" and dependent on short-term funding. The article points to record jet fuel prices, closed airspace over Russia and parts of the Middle East, and high interest rates. The suspension is not yet described as a final closure, so the outcome is uncertain.
