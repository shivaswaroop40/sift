A Condé Nast Traveler writer sailed from Brazzaville on the Princesse Ngalessa, a multi-level riverboat run by Ducret Expeditions. The piece describes it as the only passenger vessel of its kind in the Congo Basin. Eight days and 600 miles later, the boat reached Ouesso on the Sangha River.

Onboard comforts include air-conditioning, three meals a day and private bathrooms, with local guides. Stops included a forest spirit dance at Ngabe, kayaking in the flooded forests of the Alima River and trading smoked fish and fruit with fishermen. The writer contrasts this with the crowded daka-daka barges on the Kinshasa to Kisangani route.

From Ouesso the writer took a launch into the Central African Republic and stayed at Sangha Lodge. There the Ba'aka people offered a hunt, and the region includes Dzanga Baï, known for forest elephants. The article also notes that gorilla tracking is possible.

The piece is part travelogue, part argument against the 'heart of darkness' image and against adventure-influencer spectacle. It gives no prices, dates or booking details in the text provided, and the source is truncated before its conclusion.
