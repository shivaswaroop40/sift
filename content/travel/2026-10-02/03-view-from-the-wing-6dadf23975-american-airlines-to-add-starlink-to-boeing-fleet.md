American Airlines now plans to install Starlink satellite internet on Boeing 737s, 777s and 787s, not just Airbus narrowbodies as originally announced in May. This would put high-speed connectivity across its mainline fleet starting in early 2027, fixing a major product weakness on older widebodies with poor Panasonic wifi.

For independent travellers, this matters because working wifi influences airline choice. American's older widebodies are currently unusable for productivity, making paid premium seats poor value. Starlink's low latency makes ordinary browsing instantaneous and serves a full cabin of passengers.

Delta won't start installing Amazon Leo until 2028 at earliest, and the product isn't yet in beta. Half their fleet will remain without upgraded wifi. Meanwhile, American is also improving business class, lounges, food and domestic seating, building a stronger premium proposition.

American's on-time performance trails Delta's (76.43% versus 80.90% in 2025), but United—considered premium—has only a 2% advantage. If American accelerates seatback screen rollouts delayed until 2028, fuller Starlink coverage could genuinely challenge Delta's premium status.
