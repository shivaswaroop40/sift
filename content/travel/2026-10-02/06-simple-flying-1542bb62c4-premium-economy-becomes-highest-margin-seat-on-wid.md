Airlines have reconfigured long-haul cabins to prioritise premium economy, which now generates more profit per square foot than business class. A business suite occupies 25 to 30 square feet but a premium economy recliner uses only 10 to 12 square feet. Airlines price premium economy at 40 to 100 percent above economy while business class commands 250 to 500 percent premiums, making the maths favour mid-tier seating.

For your fare planning, this shift matters because airlines are removing standard economy rows to fit wider premium economy layouts on transatlantic and transpacific routes. Seats cost under $15,000 to install versus over $80,000 for business class suites. Lower catering and maintenance costs mean nearly every premium economy dollar converts to profit, making airlines aggressive about filling these cabins at higher fares.

The expansion creates a risk for airlines: sustained demand relies on high-spending leisure travellers willing to pay cash upgrades, as corporate travel budgets tighten. Carriers including Air France, Lufthansa, United and Emirates have already rebalanced their configurations around this financial model.
