Camp Butner rose across 40,000 acres of North Carolina farmland in months during World War II, displacing hundreds of local families. Nearly 1,800 military buildings created a temporary city that also held thousands of German and Italian prisoners of war.

More than 250,000 troops trained there, including the 78th Infantry Division, which later fought in Europe and crossed the Rhine at Remagen. The museum now preserves uniforms, maps, archaeological finds, and personal objects from civilians, soldiers, and prisoners whose lives intersected briefly at the camp.
