The Boeing 737 MAX 10 is set to receive FAA certification as early as next week following a successful type certification board meeting on Friday. However, a software flaw affecting the autopilot's go-around function remains unresolved. Though the FAA ruled the issue poses no safety risk, multiple airlines have told Boeing they will refuse aircraft equipped with the affected software versions U14 and U14.1.

This matters because major carriers including United, American, Southwest and Alaska Airlines have already rejected the newer software for years, preferring the older U13 version. The MAX 7 and MAX 10 variants cannot use U13, creating a delivery problem for Boeing's newest aircraft. First customers expect deliveries from spring 2027, but acceptance hinges on resolving the software standoff.

Boeing says a permanent fix will arrive in 2028, with pilot procedures coming sooner. The company is working with airlines on a workaround that would let pilots re-engage the autopilot mode if the problem occurs during flight.
