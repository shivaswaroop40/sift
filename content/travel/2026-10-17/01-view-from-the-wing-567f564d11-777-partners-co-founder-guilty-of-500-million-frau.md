Josh Wander, co-founder of 777 Partners, has pleaded guilty to wire and securities fraud. Prosecutors say his Miami firm obtained $40 million by pledging settlement assets it did not own to finance Boeing aircraft for Bonza, an Australian budget airline that collapsed in 2024.

The fraud matters because 777 Partners was a major financer of Flair in Canada and Bonza in Australia. Both relied on aircraft funded through a network of false collateral and doctored bank records, making these airlines' failures directly tied to investor deception.

777 used multiple schemes to hide funding gaps. Employees altered records using Microsoft Paint to show non-existent cash, pledged the same assets to multiple lenders simultaneously, and temporarily reassigned collateral before lender inspections. Wander blamed outdated computer systems when confronted.

Wander faces 27 to 40 years imprisonment at sentencing in March 2027 and must forfeit at least $500 million. 777 Partners filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in August with $2.7 billion in financing obligations.
