British Airways operates 12 Airbus A380s, tied with Singapore Airlines as the second-largest operator outside Emirates. All 12 aircraft fly from London Heathrow to seven or eight destinations, reaching fewer than 4% of the airline's global network. Johannesburg and Miami receive twice-daily service; Washington Dulles and Singapore Changi lost A380 flights entirely in 2026.

Heathrow operates under a hard legal cap of 480,000 annual movements and has run at 99% capacity for two decades, making it the world's most slot-constrained airport by some measures. British Airways cannot buy additional slots at reasonable cost, so it maximises revenue per slot by deploying its largest aircraft on its most profitable routes instead.

An A380 carries roughly 50% more passengers than a 787 using the same takeoff and landing slot. The retrofit announced in October 2026 cuts total seating to 417, converting the upper deck to 106 business-class seats. This reflects the airline's conclusion that premium demand on these routes is more reliable than filling economy cabins year-round.
