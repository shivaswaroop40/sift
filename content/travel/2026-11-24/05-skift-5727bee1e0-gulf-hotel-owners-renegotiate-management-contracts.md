Gulf hotel owners are reassessing their management agreements with operators. The U.S.-Iran war, which began in late February, has weighed on regional hotels for more than six months. So far owners are renegotiating terms rather than going to arbitration, according to Skift.

Under these agreements, an owner pays a brand to run the hotel, usually through a base fee tied to revenue. The fee is paid even when the hotel loses money. Owners therefore carry most of the downside while operators keep collecting.

Dubai's October figures show the scale. Occupancy fell to 68.4% from 84.9% a year earlier, per CoStar's STR. Average daily rate dropped 28.1% to $181 and RevPAR fell 42% to $124. Visitors in the first ten months of 2026 were 9.7 million, down 38% from 15.7 million.

The source text is cut off by a paywall, so detail on specific contract terms or operators is not available. Skift's own framing is that the current patience depends on a recovery that has not yet come.
