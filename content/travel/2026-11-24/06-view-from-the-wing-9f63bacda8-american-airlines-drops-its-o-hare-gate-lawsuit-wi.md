American Airlines has asked to dismiss its contract case over Chicago O'Hare's 2025 gate redistribution, with prejudice. That includes its claim for damages. Chicago and United do not object, and each side would bear its own costs. The move follows an appeals court upholding the denial of a preliminary injunction. That ruling did not decide whether Chicago breached the 2018 lease.

American says it still believes the process broke its agreement, but it is putting its effort into growing the hub. Two later gate redeterminations have increased its allocation, and it paid $30 million for Spirit's G8 and G10 gate leases. It now runs more than 500 daily departures to more than 185 destinations.

For travellers, the practical result is a stronger American presence at O'Hare, not a retreat. New routes include Naples, Honolulu, Maui from 17 December and Tokyo Narita from 19 March 2027. Chicago also gets the 787-9 with 51 business suites first, and a premium cabin now appears on every O'Hare flight.

The author argues that FAA summer flight caps, based on approved 2025 schedules, protected American more than the lawsuit did. American still has to make the flying pay. A new L concourse Admirals Club, over 10,000 square feet, replaces the current lounge next year.
