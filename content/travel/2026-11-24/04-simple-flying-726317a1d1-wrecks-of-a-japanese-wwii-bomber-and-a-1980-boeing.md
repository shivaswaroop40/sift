Simple Flying's Sumit Singh visited Yap in the Federated States of Micronesia in late 2026. Beside the functioning airport is an overgrown Japanese wartime airfield. There lie a Mitsubishi G4M 'Betty' bomber and the burnt-out remains of an Air Micronesia Boeing 727, which crashed on landing in November 1980. Local volunteer pilot Amos Collins showed him both.

The 727 touched down 13 feet short of runway 07 and the right main gear separated. It came to rest about 1,700 feet on. All 73 occupants got out within about a minute. The probable cause was the captain reducing thrust too early on a shallow approach. The aircraft then burned, and the airport fire truck reportedly had no water. Only the tail section remains.

The author found around seven Japanese aircraft wrecks, plus Corsair engines and B-24 propellers. The airstrip now serves as a potholed village road. The land is private, so visitors are expected to ask residents for permission. Many wrecks are unmarked, and the bomber's plaque is covered in algae.
