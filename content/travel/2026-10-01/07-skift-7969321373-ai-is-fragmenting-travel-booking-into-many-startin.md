Conversations at Skift Global Forum with CEOs from Uber, Booking, Expedia and Airbnb reveal that AI agents are splintering travel distribution. Rather than one gatekeeper replacing Google, travellers now have multiple entry points to book trips.

For two decades, the path was clear: search intent flowed through Google, then to airlines and hotels. Travel companies spent billions competing for visibility at that chokepoint. AI promises to change this by letting travellers tell an assistant what they want instead of repeating requests across platforms.

The question of who owns the customer relationship becomes murkier as AI fragments the funnel. Intent is no longer concentrated at a single discovery point where travel companies can intercept it early.
