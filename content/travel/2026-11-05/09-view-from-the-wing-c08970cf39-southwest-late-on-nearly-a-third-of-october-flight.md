Southwest was on time for 68.90% of flights in October, last among ten North American airlines in Cirium's monthly report and down from 75.5% a year earlier. Delta led with 86.86% on time and a 0.24% cancellation rate. Southwest's cancellations rose to 0.51%, more than twice Delta's. Every other US airline improved on September; Southwest fell 2.24 points. Cirium counts a flight as on time if it reaches the gate within 15 minutes of schedule.

For two months Southwest's weak punctuality was offset by cancelling fewer flights than any rival. That advantage vanished in October, when Delta, United and Frontier all cancelled a smaller share. Delta was nearly 18 percentage points more likely to arrive within the on-time window. Changes to gate bag checks and flight closing, with agents using an iPad at the aircraft, are due in November. Adjusted schedules follow in January.

Chief operating officer Andrew Watterson said the weakness is concentrated in delays of roughly 15 to 45 minutes, with turnarounds starting well and losing time at the end. Gate checks have grown because checked bags now carry a fee and passengers bring more onboard. Fast turns let Southwest fly each aircraft more, but the savings shrink when turns overrun. Chief executive Bob Jordan has acknowledged that poor punctuality is adding to costs, including from misconnecting passengers.

Delta took first place from WestJet, ending the Canadian airline's five-month run, and cut cancellations from 1.77% in August to 0.24% in October. JetBlue gained most, up 7.63 points to 82.27%, ahead of Alaska and American. Six US airports entered Cirium's top 20 for on-time departures, up from two, led by Minneapolis-St. Paul at 87.00%. Those airport figures measure departures, not arrivals, and can shift with seasonal weather.
