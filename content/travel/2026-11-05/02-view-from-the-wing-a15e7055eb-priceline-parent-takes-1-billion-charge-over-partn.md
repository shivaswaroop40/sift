Booking Holdings, Priceline's parent, has taken a $1 billion accounting charge toward a possible Federal Trade Commission settlement. The filing shows a $900 million revenue reduction and a separate $100 million loss. The case centres on Guest Reservations, a partner that San Francisco's lawsuit says designed its sites to make travellers think they were booking with the hotel directly.

The alleged markups are large. A refundable Palace Hotel room in San Francisco cost $419 on Marriott's site and $775 on Guest Reservations, an 85% difference with the same cancellation deadline. A Google search for "palace hotel sf" showed the sponsored Guest Reservations page above Marriott's own site. Travellers who book through such intermediaries also give up hotel points, elite benefits and the ability to deal directly with the property when plans change.

Priceline did not own Guest Reservations, but Priceline Partner Solutions was reportedly its exclusive inventory supplier, providing booking technology and payment processing. In a New York Times case, an 85-year-old woman who requested a standard room at Bologna's Grand Hotel Majestic was charged $16,559 for a suite. Priceline Partner Solutions, as merchant of record, successfully sought reinstatement when she disputed the charge. Priceline says fewer than 0.09% of Guest Reservations transactions drew complaints.

Guest Reservations says it identifies itself as an independent platform and discloses its fees before purchase. The outcome is uncertain. Charging more is not illegal, and the FTC must show Priceline knew of the deception or had authority to control it. The Supreme Court also limited the agency's refund route in 2021. Booking says the FTC could seek more than $1 billion, and a settlement would likely include consumer refunds and changes to Priceline's practices.
