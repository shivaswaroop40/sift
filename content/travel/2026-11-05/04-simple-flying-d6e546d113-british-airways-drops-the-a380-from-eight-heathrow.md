British Airways has withdrawn the A380 from eight Heathrow routes: Hong Kong, Frankfurt, Madrid, Vancouver, Chicago O'Hare, Doha, Washington Dulles and Dubai. The main reason is aircraft leaving service for cabin refits. This winter the type serves only four routes, Johannesburg twice daily plus Miami, San Francisco and Singapore daily. Superjumbo departures are down 27% on last winter, and BA has fallen from second to sixth among A380 operators by flight numbers.

The refit changes what each flight offers. BA's 12 A380s currently have 469 seats. The retrofit takes them to 421, cutting 88 economy seats while adding 13 business suites and 29 premium economy seats. Fewer economy seats to sell, more premium ones. Travellers who pick flights by aircraft type have just four A380 routes out of Heathrow this winter, and Los Angeles waits until 2027 for the type to return.

Dubai is the exception with a political cause. BA pulled the A380 from the route because of the war in Iran, and a daily Boeing 787-10 took over at the start of November. The superjumbo may return next winter. Singapore went the other way. The A380 came back on 6 September after a gap since early 2025, making it BA's only Asian A380 destination. Heathrow to Singapore now has three A380 operators and four daily superjumbo flights.
