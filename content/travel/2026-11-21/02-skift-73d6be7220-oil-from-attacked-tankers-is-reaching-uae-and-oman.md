Oil from tankers attacked in the Strait of Hormuz is washing onto UAE beaches and threatening some of Oman's best-known dive sites. Greenpeace and Kpler data, current to Nov. 19, identify more than 600 oil spills in and around the Gulf since the U.S.-Iran war began on Feb. 28. Greenpeace counts over 160 tanker attacks, some causing spills.

An interactive map shows slicks along much of the UAE's Gulf coast, through the Strait of Hormuz and down Oman's coast as far as Muscat. The article says the most damaging UAE incident followed a strike, but the text is cut off before giving details of the beach closures and reopenings.

The spills add to a tourism downturn now in its ninth month. CoStar's STR reports Dubai hotel occupancy at 68.4% in October, down 16.5 points from 84.9% a year earlier.

Skift's view is that beach access on the UAE's east coast and northern emirates now depends on local notices that can change with each tanker strike. It says Dubai and Abu Dhabi do not carry that risk yet. For beach or dive plans in these areas, check current local advice close to travel.
