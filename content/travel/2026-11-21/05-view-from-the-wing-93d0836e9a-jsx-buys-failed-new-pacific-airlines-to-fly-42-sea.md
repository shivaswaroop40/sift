JSX has acquired New Pacific Airlines, a failed carrier that was formerly Northern Pacific, and plans to revive it with 42-seat Embraer ERJ145-LR jets. That is 12 more seats than JSX sells today. New Pacific has filed its intent to resume flying and asked for a waiver of the 45-day advance filing requirement. JSX has not set out a strategy.

The filed projection calls for two aircraft at first, rising to six by month five. It forecasts 10,106 flights and about 309,000 boardings in year one, averaging 1.2 hours gate to gate, with 104 staff. The bankruptcy disposed of the airline's 757s, but it kept its Part 121 certification. JSX's own flying runs under Part 135 public charter rules, capped at 30 passengers.

For travellers, this could mean a different JSX product in new markets, with more passengers sharing each departure's cost. Seats would be tighter than the current 30-seat cabins, though still more generous than a typical 50-seat regional jet. Federal security rules allow flights with 31 to 60 seats to use private terminals outside the usual secure area.

There are limits. The writer says it would not work at every airport JSX serves because of airport certification rules. Part 121 also brings a mandatory pilot retirement age of 65, so JSX could not use its older ex-American and Southwest captains. Part 121 may also hedge against rule changes, but the FAA lists public charter rulemaking as a long-term item with no next step set. The writer says that motive may be overread.
