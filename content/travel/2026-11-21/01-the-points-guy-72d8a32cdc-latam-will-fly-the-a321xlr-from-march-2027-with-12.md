LATAM Airlines will introduce the Airbus A321XLR in March 2027. Each aircraft will have 174 seats. Business class has 12 lie-flat suites from Thompson Aero Seating in a 1-1 layout with privacy doors and 19-inch screens. Economy has 126 Recaro seats with 13-inch screens and USB-C outlets, including 36 extra-legroom seats.

LATAM has not said where the XLRs will fly. Executives have previously pointed to routes linking the US and South America, possibly from its Lima hub (LIM). Those plans were questioned in May after a connection fee was added at LIM. LATAM did not comment on specific routes.

For travellers, a lie-flat narrow-body could bring business class to thinner routes that cannot fill a widebody. LATAM and Delta already serve six US cities from Lima: Atlanta, Los Angeles, Miami, New York JFK, Orlando and, since January 2026, Salt Lake City. Delta is a close partner, so any new routes may be bookable through both airlines.

Treat the route hopes as unconfirmed. The Lima fee is a real risk to any plan built around connecting traffic there. The XLR is also LATAM's second new type in two years, after its first Embraer E195-E2 arrived in October for Brazilian feeder flying.
