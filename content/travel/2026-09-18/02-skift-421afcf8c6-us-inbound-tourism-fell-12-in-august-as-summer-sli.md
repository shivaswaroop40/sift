The US received close to 3.1 million international visitors in August, an 11.8% drop year on year, according to National Travel and Tourism Office data released on Thursday. The figure follows a 7% decline in July and undershoots an already weak summer that disappointed expectations, including for World Cup travel.

Arrivals fell across every region. Africa dropped 25.5%, Central America 20.6% and Western Europe 14.8%. The breadth of the decline suggests the slowdown is not tied to a single origin market and raises questions about the US goal of 100 million annual visitors by 2030.

Canada and Mexico figures, not included in the source data, will shape the overall picture when NTTO releases them. The article also flags which US policies may be driving the drop, though specific policy detail is thin in the available excerpt.

The trend matters for independent travellers planning US trips because softer demand can shift airline pricing, route choices and loyalty programme incentives, though the source does not specify which carriers or routes are adjusting.
