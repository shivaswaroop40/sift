Airport expansions in Venice, Barcelona and Mallorca can undercut local efforts to curb overtourism and may enable more visitors to arrive, according to a New York Times Travel report. Runway and terminal growth is happening in places where city officials have publicly demanded fewer tourists.

The trend matters because travellers face the prospect of more flights into cities that are already saturated, potentially worsening crowding, rising fares on smaller local airports and strain on infrastructure that residents are pushing back against.

The piece notes the tension between regional governments promoting tourism revenue and the same authorities trying to limit visitor numbers, a contradiction that airport expansion can deepen.
