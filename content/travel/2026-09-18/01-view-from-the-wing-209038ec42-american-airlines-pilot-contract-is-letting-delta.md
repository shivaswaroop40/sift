American Airlines has retreated from Austin, cutting most of the regional flying it launched in 2021 and 2022 as part of an aggressive growth plan. Delta has used the gap to build a focus city, flying regional jets from Mexico border cities and signing up credit card customers along the way.

The constraint is a clause in the American pilot contract requiring at least 85% of regional flying to touch a qualifying hub. Austin did not meet that threshold, so every non-hub regional route ate into a 15% allowance shared across the network, and pilots grieved the flying. Mainline flying from Austin is too expensive for a developing market.

Austin matters because credit card spending is where airline profits live, and Delta chief executive Ed Bastian has publicly named Austin as a priority for card acquisitions. Under the new airport lease, American gets 9 gates, Delta 15 and Southwest 18, with American's growth tied to a new concourse in the 2030s.
