The Federal Aviation Administration ended a nationwide ground stop at about 8:15 p.m. ET on Friday, more than four hours after a communication issue hit the Air Route Traffic Control Centers. These are the high-altitude facilities that manage flights between airports. Officials said no cyberattack was indicated.

FlightAware counted more than 5,700 cancellations by Friday night. Airlines had already cancelled 980 flights for Saturday. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said instability at radar and communication systems began at 3:36 p.m. ET, with 3,500 planes in the air.

Skift notes this is only the third nationwide ground stop in 25 years. The last, during a 2023 system outage, lasted about two hours. Lifting the stop does not clear the backlog, so aircraft and crews will be out of position for days.

The source text is truncated, and it does not give the cause beyond a communication issue. It also does not say which airlines or airports are worst affected.
