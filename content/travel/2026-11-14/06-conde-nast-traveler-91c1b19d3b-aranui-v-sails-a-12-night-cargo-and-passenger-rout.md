Aranui Cruises, a locally owned French Polynesian line, has run combined passenger and cargo voyages for over 40 years. A Condé Nast Traveler writer joined the 12-night, nearly thousand-mile sailing from Papeete to the Marquesas on the purpose-built Aranui V in March 2026. The ship carries cargo at the front and passengers at the back.

While freight is unloaded, passengers go ashore on included excursions, such as car tours of archaeological sites on Nuku Hiva and guided hikes. Optional extras cost more, including a scenic drive on Fatu Hiva. Local residents guide guests in their own cars, as the islands have no buses. Fatu Hiva and Tahuata have no airport and can only be reached by sea.

The crew are all Polynesian, many from the Marquesas, and the writer says passengers and crew mix freely in the bar. Onboard facilities are basic, with a pool and cocktail bar, plus lectures on Marquesan culture. The piece is a first-person account and gives no fares, so cost is not covered.
