The FAA lifted a nationwide ground stop at 8:15pm EST on Friday, allowing US flights to resume after about four hours. Most departures had been halted in the late afternoon after communication problems hit air traffic control centres. Officials said the outage happened during a technology upgrade. It was only the second nationwide ground stop since 9/11.

FlightAware counted more than 6,000 cancelled departures by 8:45pm Friday, plus at least 1,400 already scrapped for Saturday. That figure was expected to rise. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said it would take time to unwind. Some flights still operated, including flights within Alaska, Hawaii inter-island services and West Coast departures to Hawaii. United handled international departures case by case.

Travellers should expect knock-on disruption through the weekend. Under Department of Transportation rules, passengers on cancelled or significantly delayed flights can claim a refund to the original form of payment if they choose not to travel. A significant delay generally means arriving three hours late on a domestic trip or six on an international one.

The source suggests booking backup options early, as airport hotels and rental cars can sell out. Using miles for a flight the next day is another option, if you check the refund rules first. Card trip-delay cover may not apply to FAA groundings, so keep receipts.
