The FAA lifted a rare nationwide ground stop after more than four hours on Friday. Trouble began at 3:36 PM, when air traffic control centres reported unstable radar and communication systems. Overloaded routers and inconsistent data followed. Airspace reopened at 8:15 PM, but departures resumed only gradually. Aircraft already airborne were able to land safely.

CNN figures cited in the report put cancellations at more than 7,500 flights within, into or out of the US, with over 10,000 delayed. More than 1,500 Saturday flights were already cancelled. Airlines for America says thousands of crew members and many aircraft are in the wrong cities. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said recovery will be slow.

If you are flying in the US or connecting through it, expect knock-on disruption for several days. Crew and aircraft out of position cause cancellations well after the original fault is fixed. Check your flight status before leaving for the airport. International flights to the US were also delayed. The report says the upcoming Thanksgiving rush is not expected to be affected.

The cause is disputed. Officials said the outage came from patching a new system into older infrastructure, and denied any threat or link to Thursday's Microsoft outage. Some controllers told CNN they do not accept this, saying such switches are normally done midweek at night. Duffy warned of further disruption unless systems are updated.
