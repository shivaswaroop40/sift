From October 19, airlines are no longer required to provide lodging and meals during mechanical delays that fall under ten new exemptions added by Congress in the 2024 FAA reauthorization. These include unscheduled maintenance like hydraulic leaks, engine warnings, cracked windshields, and brake defects discovered during preflight or after arrival.

This matters because it substantially narrows when you can claim compensation for being stranded. Airlines previously had to cover these costs when mechanical problems caused delays. Now they can avoid reimbursement for routine maintenance issues that ground aircraft, even if they strand you overnight.

The change was required by law, not a Trump administration decision, though a Republican representative sponsored the broader amendment. The Department of Transportation's defence of the rule uses examples like volcanic ash and cyberattacks, which are rare, to minimise discussion of common maintenance scenarios where airlines now dodge their obligations.
