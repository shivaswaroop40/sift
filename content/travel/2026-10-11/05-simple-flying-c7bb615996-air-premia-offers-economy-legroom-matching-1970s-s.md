South Korea's Air Premia provides 35 inches of pitch on some Boeing 787-9s, matching economy seats from the 1970s. This applies to only three of its nine aircraft. Most long-haul economy now sits at 31 to 32 inches, with ultra-low-cost carriers squeezing down to 28.

For independent travellers choosing transpacific routes, Air Premia's legroom matters. The airline operates Seoul to Los Angeles, Newark, and Honolulu. But you need to check your specific aircraft: one-third of bookings land on tighter 33-inch configurations.

Air Premia sacrificed seat width for pitch. Economy seats are a narrow 17 to 17.5 inches, the industry standard for nine-abreast 787s. Japan Airlines remains the outlier, fitting eight seats across its 787s to reach 18 inches of width while holding 33 to 34 inches of pitch.

Pitch advantage erodes quickly on newer aircraft. Korean Air's latest 787-10s and A321neos run 31 to 32 inches, not the 33-34 inches on older widebodies. Generous legroom tends to exist on older aircraft approaching retirement.
