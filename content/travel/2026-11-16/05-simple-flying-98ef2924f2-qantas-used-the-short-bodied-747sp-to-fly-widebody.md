Simple Flying looks at how Qantas used the Boeing 747SP to serve Wellington, whose runway is under 6,000 feet (1,829 metres) long. Standard jumbos such as the 747-200B could not take off from it with a viable load. Airlines had mostly used smaller, shorter-range narrowbodies, which limited capacity on the trans-Tasman routes.

Boeing built the 747SP in the early 1970s for long, thin routes. Shortening the fuselage by about 47 feet (14.3 metres) cut 45,000 to 50,000 pounds from the empty weight compared with the 747-200B. The wingspan stayed the same. Less weight meant the aircraft could use shorter runways or carry more fuel.

The shorter body cost tail authority, so Boeing fitted a taller fin of 65 feet ten inches (20.06 metres). It also swapped triple-slotted flaps for single-slotted ones, saving weight and drag. The article says this gave good low-speed handling and braking, which suited steep approaches and quick stops on short runways.
