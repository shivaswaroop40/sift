Korean Air is expected to take over Asiana Airlines' six Airbus A380s at the end of the year, adding them to its own five. One Asiana aircraft appears to be parked, and it is unclear whether it will fly again. If not, Korean would have 10 operating A380s, the same as Qantas, which is down from 12.

The two fleets are very different. Asiana's A380s seat 495, with 12 Business Suites, 66 Business Smartium seats and 417 economy seats. Korean's seat 407, with 12 first class suites, 94 business seats in 2-2-2 and 301 economy seats. Premium seating is 15.8% of Asiana's cabin and 26.0% of Korean's.

Asiana's brand is due to be retired in mid-December 2026. Its A380s will get Korean Air's livery, and products such as Smartium may be rebranded or changed. Korean's cabins are dated, with doorless first class and window-seat business passengers lacking aisle access. Neither fleet has premium economy, and the article says new cabins are unlikely.

Korean Air would probably have retired its A380s already. It had planned to retire them all by 2026 but was forced to extend their service, according to its chief Walter Cho in mid-2025. The text cuts off before giving the reason.
