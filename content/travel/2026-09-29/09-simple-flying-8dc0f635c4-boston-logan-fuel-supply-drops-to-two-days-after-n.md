A weekend nor'easter trapped barges in New York, cutting jet fuel deliveries to Boston Logan and leaving roughly two days of reserves. Airlines including JetBlue and Delta have been told to tanker extra fuel in and top up with trucks from neighbouring regions. About a quarter of Saturday's flights at the airport were delayed or cancelled, though it is unclear how much was due to fuel versus the weather itself.

The disruption matters because tankering works on short hops but is hard to operate on transcontinental or long-haul flights, which is why some departures may have needed fuel stops at other US airports. Boston is a Delta hub and JetBlue focus city, so routing changes and uplift limits will hit a wide range of domestic and international services, including busy routes to London Heathrow, Paris and Dublin.

Massport expects normal barge deliveries to resume once the storm clears, with the worst of the weather now past the city. The episode highlights how reliant a major coastal airport is on a single fuel supply chain by water.
