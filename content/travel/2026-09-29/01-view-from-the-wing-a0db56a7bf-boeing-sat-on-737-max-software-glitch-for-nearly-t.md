Boeing knew about a 737 MAX flight management software bug for almost two years before telling operators in late August 2026. The fault can cause vertical navigation mode to drop to a simpler automation mode after a go-around. It first logged the issue in November 2024 and decided it did not warrant a safety review at that stage.

Southwest and United are now refusing to accept newly delivered MAXs running the current software, though American and Alaska have not received affected aircraft yet. A permanent fix is targeted for early 2028, though Boeing says it is trying to bring that forward. Boeing argues pilots are trained to handle the situation safely.

The FAA has said it will defer MAX 10 certification, which was thought to be days away, and convene a Corrective Action Review Board. The MAX 10 remains uncertificated as a result, with no confirmed new timeline from the agency.
