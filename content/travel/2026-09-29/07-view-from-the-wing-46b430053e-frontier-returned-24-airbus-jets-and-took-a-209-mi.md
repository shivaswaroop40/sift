Frontier Airlines has handed back 24 A320neos to AerCap and booked $209 million in related charges across the first half of 2025. New CEO Jimmy Dempsey said the airline had more aircraft than it could use productively, finishing June with 165 planes after also taking new deliveries.

The source argues the problem predates former CEO Barry Biffle. Indigo Partners, Frontier's controlling shareholder until April 2024, negotiated group orders of 430 and 255 Airbus jets in 2017 and 2021 that required Frontier to take aircraft it did not need. Dempsey was CFO and then president during the buildup, and Indigo's Bill Franke still owns 43% of the now-public Frontier Group Holdings.

Frontier inflated earnings through sale-leaseback transactions, selling aircraft to lessors and leasing them back at higher future cost. The source says this explains Frontier's only post-pandemic annual profit, which was not an operating profit. Frontier projects third-quarter capacity growth of 17-18% and fourth-quarter growth of about 7% over 2025.

The article does not quantify how many more aircraft Frontier may still return, or whether further charges are expected. The claim that the over-ordering was an Indigo-level decision is the source's interpretation, based on Dempsey's own public statements.
