The Peaks of the Balkans is a 119-mile inn-to-inn circular trek across Albania, Kosovo and Montenegro. A late 2025 study in the Journal of Outdoor Recreation and Tourism estimated about 43,000 hikers walk it each year, up from roughly 20,000 before the pandemic. It put spending at more than €25 million a year.

The trail began in the early 2010s as a cross-border peace and tourism project. Its aim was to give residents of villages hollowed out by war and emigration a reason to stay or return. Many family homes and shepherd settlements now run as guesthouses with meals included.

Success is starting to strain some places. Ricardo Fahrig of Zbulo, a company running Peaks trips for years, says the problem is distribution, not total numbers. Dobërdol, at 5,700 feet, struggles with volume in a short season that runs from about June to September, with some hikers walking into early October.

The writer, on a self-guided ten-day itinerary built with Zbulo, left the main route after Dobërdol and found near-total quiet on an alternative path towards Gjeravica, Kosovo's highest peak, and the village of Junik. The piece is a first-person account, so the crowding evidence beyond the study is anecdotal.
