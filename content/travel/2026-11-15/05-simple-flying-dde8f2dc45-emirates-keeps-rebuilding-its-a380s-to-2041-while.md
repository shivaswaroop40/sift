Emirates is stripping its Airbus A380s to bare metal and rebuilding the interiors as part of a retrofit programme it values at $5 billion. The plan covers 219 aircraft, 110 A380s and 109 Boeing 777s. So far 47 A380s and 53 777s are done, at about 28 aircraft a year. Emirates reportedly plans to fly the A380 until 2041.

The airline says the A380 was its most profitable asset last year. Sir Tim Clark is quoted saying so, in a year of record profit. Emirates reported $6.6 billion for 2025-26. The type matters most at slot-limited airports such as Heathrow, Schiphol and Sydney, where one large aircraft replaces more flights.

Refitted A380s add a Premium Economy cabin for the first time, in a 2-3-2 layout with leg and footrests and a 13.3-inch screen. Starlink WiFi is being installed across 232 aircraft. For travellers, this means a better chance of a new cabin on A380 routes, though fleet-wide completion is still years off.

Qantas is bringing forward the retirement of its A380s, four years early according to the headline, in favour of newer twin-engine aircraft. The source text is cut off before the detail. Emirates also expects to start receiving the 777-9 in 2027, which seats about 150 fewer passengers.
