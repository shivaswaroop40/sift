American Airlines says a focus on local customers has brought a "material margin improvement" at LaGuardia. Chief Commercial Officer Nat Pieper told the Skift Aviation Forum that Chicago's biggest opportunity is corporate traffic. He is also reworking Los Angeles schedules to suit people who live there, not just visitors.

This reverses the post-merger plan of 2014, which built New York schedules around visitors arriving in the morning and leaving in the evening. American dropped LaGuardia flights to Cleveland, Minneapolis and Atlanta and added Roanoke and Wilmington, North Carolina. The author calls the original approach a mistake that ignored the card spending of New Yorkers.

The author argues that airline route accounting can mislead. Credit card revenue is credited to flights where miles are redeemed, not to the schedule that won the card holder. Cutting a route can look better on paper while losing the customer's other business. American's CFO said its usual profitability systems do not fully capture these card effects.

On Los Angeles, the author says 25 preferential gates is the documented allocation after Terminal 5 reopens, per the lease amendment approved in December 2025. Pieper wants 30, which is LAWA's figure for the whole two-terminal complex. The author is unsure how American can promise the extra five gates.
