Korean Air will add Newark Liberty (EWR) as a second New York-area gateway, with daily flights from Seoul Incheon (ICN) starting 22 May 2027. It will use a Boeing 777-300ER. The airline has not said which cabin layout the aircraft will have. Its 777s vary, and some older ones have eight first class suites.

The route sits alongside Korean Air's long-standing twice-daily service to New York JFK. After the Asiana merger, schedule data from Cirium shows up to four daily JFK flights including Asiana, and Korean Air's schedules keep that level through June 2027. EWR is its first new US destination since Boston in 2019.

For travellers, Newark gives a nonstop to Seoul from the New Jersey side, which may suit those living or connecting there. It also links to Delta, Korean Air's joint venture partner. The two coordinate fares and schedules between the US and South Korea, so expect aligned options. United has flown EWR-ICN nonstop since September on a 787-9.

The timing overlaps with the Asiana integration. Korean Air will complete the customer-facing merger on 17 December, which will significantly increase its share at Incheon. The merger adds no new US destinations.
