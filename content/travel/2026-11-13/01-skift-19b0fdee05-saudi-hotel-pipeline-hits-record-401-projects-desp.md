A month after a Houthi missile killed 13 people at Riyadh's main airport, Saudi Arabia's hotel pipeline is at a record. Lodging Econometrics counts 401 projects in development, up 15% on a year ago. They would add 109,320 rooms, a 16% increase. Developers and operators say they know of no projects cancelled because of the attacks.

The attacks have closed airports, cancelled the kingdom's biggest events and raised the cost of insuring against conflict. Skift says the main damage is to Saudi Arabia's image as a safe destination. Investors are still signing deals, betting that domestic and religious travel will carry the market until the fighting stops.

Saudi Arabia holds 52% of the Middle East's hotel projects and 58% of its rooms. Skift's view is that the bet leans on domestic and religious demand, while the international visitors that Vision 2030 relies on are the ones being put off.
