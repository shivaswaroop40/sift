Austin's mayor, Kirk Watson, wants to restart planning for a light rail link to the airport. It would be a three-mile extension of the planned line, mostly elevated, with a station at the airport. He says the Federal Transit Administration has agreed to a streamlined environmental review. The next nine to twelve months would go on costs and funding.

Voters backed a property tax rise in 2020 for a $7.1 billion project that included airport rail. Costs then climbed. The rail component rose from $5.8 billion to $10.3 billion by 2022. Phase one was cut from 20 miles to 9.8 miles and from 26 stations to 15. The airport and downtown subway were dropped.

The reduced line is now put at $8.2 billion, with opening delayed from 2029 to 2033 and no airport stop. Watson says the extension must fit within the existing voter-approved budget, with no new taxpayer money. The idea is that the airport would pay part of the cost.

The article says FAA rules allow this in part. A 2021 change let on-airport rail infrastructure use airport funding. 2025 guidance lets airports contribute to ground access projects, including off-site ones, if airport funds cover only the airport-related share. This is a plan to plan, not a funded project.
