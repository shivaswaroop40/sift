Maya Leibman, who ran AAdvantage before becoming American's Chief Information Officer, said on the Airlines Confidential podcast that the airline kept charging a $75 fee for award bookings within 21 days of departure. This was after the process became automated. She said the transaction was "all done automatically". American dropped the fee in January 2020.

The fee was meant to cover the extra work of expediting a booking. Once online booking removed that work, the justification went, but the charge stayed. Elite members were exempt from the $75. American also announced a $5 fee for booking awards online in May 2008, but never implemented it.

For anyone who books awards late, the point is that fees follow revenue opportunity, not cost. The author argues that airlines can price by demand, and that a last-minute award can reasonably cost more. He says that should be handled through revenue management, not a processing fee.

Removing a cash fee did not necessarily make awards cheaper. The author says United and Delta moved towards charging more miles for close-in awards instead. Elites who once had the cash fee waived do not get the extra mileage waived. He also says American stopped discounting awards to elites.
