Simple Flying says Mataveri International Airport on Easter Island is the only potential diversion point for aircraft crossing the South Pacific between South America and Oceania. Even with long ETOPS ratings, such as the A350-900's 370 minutes, flights need a fallback. The article notes that Mataveri has a 10,800 foot runway, built in 1987 as a planned US Space Shuttle abort site.

Chilean authorities restrict the surrounding airspace. Only one aircraft may be in the vicinity at a time. Once a jet passes the halfway point between South America and Easter Island, no other plane can enter. The reason given is that an incident at Mataveri would leave no suitable diversion airport at all.

The best-known route that depends on it is Qantas's Sydney to Santiago service. It was cancelled in March 2020, revived on 31 October 2022 with the 787-9, and now averages four flights a week. The only scheduled airline to Mataveri is LATAM Chile, which flew 1,094 flights from Santiago in 2025.
