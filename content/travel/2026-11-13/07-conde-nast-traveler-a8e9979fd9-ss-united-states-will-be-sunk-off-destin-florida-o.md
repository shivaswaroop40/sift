The SS United States, America's flagship ocean liner, will be sunk off Destin, Florida, on 12 November. She will become the world's largest artificial reef. The ship is nearly 1,000 feet long. She is currently moored in the Mobile River, Alabama, rusted and stripped inside, after almost six decades of changing owners.

Alex Fogg, natural resources chief for Destin-Fort Walton Beach tourism, led the project. More than 600 artificial reefs have been placed there since 2017, from concrete blocks to whole ships. The area has little natural reef. The ship is expected to draw divers and fishers, and marine life should arrive within hours.

The ship held the transatlantic speed record for an ocean liner, set on her 1952 maiden voyage in 3 days, 10 hours and 40 minutes. Passenger service ended in 1969 after 17 years. Deals to refurbish her, including a Manhattan pier plan, fell through. The conservancy that owned her did not first choose sinking.

For travellers, this is a new wreck dive and fishing site, and the author's tour was one of the last above water. The source says the ecosystem will take at least a year to fully develop. The text is cut off before the sinking plan's details.
