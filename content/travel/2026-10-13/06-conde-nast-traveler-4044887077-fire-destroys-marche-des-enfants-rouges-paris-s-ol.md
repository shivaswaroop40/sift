A fire broke out early on Monday 12 October at the Marché des Enfants Rouges in the Marais district. Around 150 firefighters and 50 vehicles fought the blaze, which destroyed about 1,000 square meters of market stalls. The fire brigade described the covered market as completely destroyed, though neighbouring buildings were spared. No serious injuries were reported.

The market has operated since 1615, when it took its name from a nearby orphanage. It became a destination for independent food traders selling Moroccan, Italian and Japanese cuisine alongside fresh produce. Recent viral videos of large sandwiches had drawn international visitors to a place Paris residents relied on for daily shopping and meals.

The market survived a demolition threat in the 1990s and underwent renovation before reopening in 2000. Authorities have launched an investigation into the fire's cause and will now assess whether the historic monument, listed since 1982, can be rebuilt.
