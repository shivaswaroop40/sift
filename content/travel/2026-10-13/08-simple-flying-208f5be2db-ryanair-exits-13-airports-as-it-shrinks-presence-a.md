Ryanair has ended all flights to 13 European airports in 2026, with 12 closures announced through October and a 13th to follow in March 2027. The exits span Spain, France, Portugal, Turkey, Greece, Serbia and Germany. Most served tiny networks; the Jordanian capital Amman saw suspension due to regional conflict.

For independent planners, this signals how Ryanair's route map tightens when airport charges rise. The airline explicitly cited air traffic control fees, security charges and passenger duties at Bremen, which it is leaving in March 2027 after 19 years. Ryanair controls enough European capacity to walk away rather than accept cost increases.

The Bremen departure is particularly stark: the airport loses all UK flights and a third of its total seat capacity, despite Ryanair adding 8% more seats there in 2026. The airline uses exit threats as negotiating leverage across its network. Smaller operators cannot do the same, making Ryanair's decisions a bellwether for airport viability.
