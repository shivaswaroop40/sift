Transport Canada announced an $85.5 million investment to rehabilitate Rankin Inlet Airport in Nunavut as a military dispersal site for F-35 fighters. The project covers 1.52 million square feet of airfield pavement, replacing standard asphalt with reinforced concrete that can withstand the F-35's engine heat without damage.

The upgrade matters because Canada's F-35 strategy depends on spreading aircraft across remote northern bases to prevent concentrated losses in conflict. Rankin Inlet's 6,000-foot runway is tight for F-35 operations, which typically need 8,000 feet, so the Canadian fleet will use drag chutes for landing there.

The work is part of a broader $2.67 billion investment across four northern sites and feeds into Canada's $35 billion defence blueprint. The project also supports the new CC-330 transport aircraft that will ferry fuel, parts and ammunition to these remote dispersal bases.
