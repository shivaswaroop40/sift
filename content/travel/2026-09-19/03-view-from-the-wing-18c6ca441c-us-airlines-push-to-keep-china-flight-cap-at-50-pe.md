Before the pandemic, US and Chinese airlines each operated about 150 weekly flights between the two countries. Today only around 48 of the 50 permitted flights on each side are running, leaving capacity at roughly one-third of pre-2020 levels.

US carriers oppose increasing that cap for two reasons. They want to protect higher fares, and they argue Russian airspace restrictions make their routes uncompetitive because Chinese airlines can overfly Russia while US flights cannot.

Air China has requested two extra flights tied to President Xi Jinping's visit. American, Delta and United want them treated as charters rather than scheduled services, fearing that regular approvals would set a precedent for expansion.

For travellers, the stand-off keeps transpacific fares elevated and limits onward connections through Chinese hubs into Asia. The dispute hinges as much on fare discipline and Russia overflight rights as on bilateral diplomacy.
