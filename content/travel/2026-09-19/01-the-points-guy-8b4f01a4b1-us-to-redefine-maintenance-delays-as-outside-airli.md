The US Department of Transportation has finalised a rule, due to take effect on 19 October 2026, that will classify unexpected aircraft maintenance issues as outside an airline's control. The DOT expects the number of delays and cancellations for which carriers provide amenities and compensation to fall as a result.

For travellers, this means airlines will have more leeway to refuse meal vouchers, hotel rooms and ground transport when a mechanical problem grounds a flight. The change was required by the 2024 FAA Reauthorisation Act, though it follows a Trump administration decision last year to scrap a separate Biden-era cash compensation proposal.

The rule only covers maintenance that cannot wait until after the flight, usually safety-related fixes. Routine maintenance delays will still count as the airline's responsibility. Other disruptions newly classed as outside carrier control include a passenger death on board, removal of an unruly flyer, and cyber attacks on carriers meeting cybersecurity rules.

No US airline has confirmed it will stop offering amenities, and industry officials argue market competition keeps customer service standards high. Travellers worried about being stranded without help are advised to hold travel insurance or use a credit card with trip delay cover, and to keep all receipts.
