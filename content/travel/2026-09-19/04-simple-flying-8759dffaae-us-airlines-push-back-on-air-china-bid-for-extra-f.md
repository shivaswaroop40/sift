Airlines for America, representing American, Delta and United, is opposing Air China's request to add two flights each to Washington Dulles and New York JFK for President Xi Jinping's September 24 state visit. The carrier already operates five weekly flights to each airport, with the US-China bilateral capped at 50 round trips per week.

US carriers argue that granting extra scheduled services would create a backdoor around the 50-flight limit. They want any additions classified as one-off charters rather than permanent commercial flights, a distinction that would keep the cap intact for future requests.

The dispute centres on Russian airspace. Since 2022, US airlines have been barred from overflying Russia, while Chinese carriers still can, gaining shorter routings, lower fuel burn and reduced costs. A US DOT proposal in October 2025 to ban Chinese airlines from the same corridor was dropped ahead of trade talks.

Before the pandemic, more than 300 weekly flights operated between the two countries. Today's 50-flight cap keeps capacity tight and fares comparatively high, which is why US carriers are determined to block anything that might shift the balance further towards Chinese airlines.
