The Climate Change Committee has told the UK government that a third runway at Heathrow does not currently fit the country’s net zero target, and that aviation emissions are forecast to rise even without expansion. Its chair, Nigel Topping, said there is no credible plan to cut flying emissions and that decarbonisation costs are not being borne by the industry today.

For independent travellers, this matters because demand management measures such as a frequent flyer levy could reshape fares, route economics and loyalty incentives well before any new runway opens. The committee suggested that if technology fails to close the gap, limiting passenger numbers may be the only path to meeting climate commitments.

The story flags demand management as a conditional possibility rather than policy, leaving the expansion business case, timeline and any levy structure open questions for future government decisions.

Note: aviation’s 2.5% share of global carbon emissions is referenced in the original but the figure is cut off in the available text.
