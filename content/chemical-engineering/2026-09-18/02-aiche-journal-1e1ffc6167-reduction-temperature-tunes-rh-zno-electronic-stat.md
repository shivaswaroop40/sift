Atomically dispersed Rh on ~2 nm ZnO islands on SiO2 shows a dynamic electronic metal–support interaction in which H2 reduction removes lattice oxygen and shifts the Fermi level of the ZnO islands. The shift scales with oxygen-defect concentration between 100 °C and 600 °C and drives continuous electron transfer to Rh.

In styrene hydroformylation this produces a volcano-shaped activity curve with reduction temperature. The best sample, pretreated at 400 °C, is 3.5 times more active than the worst and 1.5 times more active than the next best, attributed via operando DRIFTS and DFT to a Sabatier optimum where moderate electron enrichment balances competing intermediate adsorption.

The result matters because it gives catalyst developers a single reduction-temperature knob to set Rh electron density on an oxide support, rather than relying on alloying or ligand chemistry. The ultrasmall ZnO islands are the key, since bulk ZnO does not lose lattice oxygen as readily under the same conditions.
