A NEDO-led consortium in Japan has run a naphtha cracking test furnace at 85% ammonia co-firing on a heat-input basis, using a newly designed burner. The hearth burners, which supply most of the heat to the cracking tubes, ran on 100% ammonia, while wall burners stayed on methane off-gas. NOx emissions matched those of conventional commercial furnaces, according to the project team.

The result matters because naphtha crackers are major CO2 sources at petrochemical sites, and replacing the methane fuel with ammonia cuts combustion-related CO2 without fuel-derived carbon. Holding NOx at existing-furnace levels shows that ammonia's lower burning velocity and fuel-bound nitrogen can be managed in a real cracking furnace, not just a lab flame.

The work was carried out under NEDO's Green Innovation Fund project on CO2-based plastic raw materials, with Mitsui Chemicals, Maruzen Petrochemical, Toyo Engineering and Sojitz Machinery as partners. The consortium will now target 100% ammonia firing across the whole furnace by extending ammonia use to the wall burners.
