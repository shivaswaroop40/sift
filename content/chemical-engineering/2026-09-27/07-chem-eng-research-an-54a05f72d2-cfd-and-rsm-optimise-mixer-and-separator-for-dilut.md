Researchers modelled and optimised a static mixer and gas-liquid separator for a low-concentration coal-mine methane blending system using CFD, response surface methodology (RSM), and the NSGA-II multi-objective algorithm.

The work targets equipment that handles dilute methane streams, where poor mixing and inefficient separation reduce the calorific value and stability of the blended gas sent downstream.

By coupling CFD-derived pressure and separation data into RSM surrogate models, the team ran NSGA-II to find Pareto-optimal geometries balancing pressure drop against separation efficiency.

The paper reports design points from the optimisation but the abstract does not give numerical results, so the practical gains in mixing uniformity, pressure drop, or separator efficiency are not quantified.
