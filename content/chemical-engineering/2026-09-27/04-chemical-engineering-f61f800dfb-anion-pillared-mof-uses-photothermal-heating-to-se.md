Researchers have built an anion-pillared metal-organic framework (MOF) whose structure doubles as a photothermal absorber, enabling adsorptive separation of propylene/propane mixtures driven by light rather than conventional heating.

The framework combines anion-pillar chemistry, which governs olefin/paraffin selectivity, with broad solar absorption so that illumination provides the thermal energy needed for adsorption and desorption cycling, potentially cutting the energy penalty of C3 olefin/paraffin splitting.

The work is at the adsorption-material stage. The source text is only a title, so reported selectivity values, uptake capacities, cycle stability and propylene purity under light heating are not disclosed in what is available.

The authors claim the design opens a route to light-driven propylene/propane separations, though engineering data on sorbent scalability and real mixed-gas performance would be needed before process relevance can be judged.
