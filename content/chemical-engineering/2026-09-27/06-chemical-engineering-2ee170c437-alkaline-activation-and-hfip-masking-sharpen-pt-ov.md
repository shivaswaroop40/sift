Researchers report a dual-site modulation strategy that combines alkaline activation with hexafluoro-2-propanol masking on P204-P507 impregnated XAD-4 resin to improve Pt and Co separation selectivity.
The aim is to suppress cobalt uptake while retaining platinum loading during solvent-impregnated resin extraction, a common problem in platinum group metal refining feeds where base metals co-extract and erode product purity.

For a process engineer, the approach is interesting because it tackles selectivity at the resin formulation stage rather than through additional separation stages, which could reduce downstream stripping load and waste volumes.
The paper describes a coordination-chemistry mechanism in which HFIP blocks non-selective sites and alkaline treatment activates Pt-selective sites, giving the resin two distinct functional environments within the same bead.
Practical details such as capacity numbers, equilibrium isotherms and breakthrough curves are limited in the available abstract, so the quantitative gain in separation factor is not yet stated.
