Researchers have separated geometric from electronic influences in Pt-catalysed furfuryl alcohol hydrogenation by tuning the surface coordination of Pt nanoparticles, moving beyond the usual focus on particle size.

The work shows that surface coordination numbers regulate product distributions in ways that cannot be explained by geometric models alone. By varying coordination, the authors link specific active-site environments to selectivity patterns.

The approach lets designers decouple nanoparticle size from active-site structure, giving a cleaner view of which surface features drive hydrogenation selectivity. This matters for catalyst screening, where size alone often fails to predict performance.
