A Nature Chemical Engineering paper describes a metal–organic framework with a turnstile-type pore that separates benzene, cyclohexene and cyclohexane. The separation runs in two fixed-bed columns, one at room temperature and one at a higher temperature. The authors attribute selectivity to thermally controlled pore gating and to molecular orientational locking, where guest interactions fix the orientation of molecules in the pore.

The mixture matters because cyclohexene and cyclohexane have very close boiling points, which makes distillation hard. This system appears in benzene partial hydrogenation, where all three species are present. A sorbent that splits them stepwise at modest temperatures could offer an alternative to energy-intensive distillation or extractive routes.
