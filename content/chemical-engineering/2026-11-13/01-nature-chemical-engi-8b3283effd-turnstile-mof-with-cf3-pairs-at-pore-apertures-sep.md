A new metal–organic framework carries pairs of –CF3 groups at its pore apertures. The paper in Nature Chemical Engineering describes these as a turnstile. It combines thermally controlled pore gating with interaction-induced locking of molecular orientation.

The authors say this allows stepwise separation of benzene, cyclohexene and cyclohexane. These cyclic C6 hydrocarbons are hard to separate industrially because their sizes and boiling points are close, and the source describes the separation as crucial but challenging.

The source is only a short summary. It gives no figures for selectivity, capacity, operating temperatures, cycling stability or performance on real mixtures, so the practical strength of the claim cannot be judged from it.
