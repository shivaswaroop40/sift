Researchers studying CO2 storage in basalts have identified how cations drive transformation between minerals to lock away CO2. The study reveals the mechanisms by which CO2 mineralizes during geological sequestration in basaltic formations, focusing on how cation movement facilitates inter-mineral conversion processes that trap CO2 permanently.

Understanding these cation-driven pathways matters for engineers designing carbon capture and storage operations. Basalts are attractive for permanent CO2 sequestration because the mineral transformations convert dissolved CO2 into stable carbonate phases. Knowledge of these mechanisms helps predict storage capacity and durability at depth.
