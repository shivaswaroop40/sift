A study in Chemical Engineering Journal reports on niobium-modified nickel electrocatalysts for alkaline water electrolysis. The stated aim is enhanced durability under intermittent operation, a condition typical of electrolysers coupled to renewable power. The available source is only the title, so specific performance data are not yet accessible.

For process engineers, intermittent operation is a known stressor for electrolyser electrodes, causing catalyst degradation and shortened stack life. Nickel-based catalysts are common in alkaline systems, and niobium modification may offer a route to stabilise them. The paper's value depends on the experimental evidence, which is not summarised here.

Readers should treat the title as a claim rather than a demonstrated result. The full article would need to be consulted for cycling protocols, overpotential changes, niobium loading effects, and comparison with unmodified nickel. Without those details, the practical benefit remains unquantified.
