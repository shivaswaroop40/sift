A paper in Chemical Engineering Research and Design reports that nonmonotonic transport in proton exchange membranes contaminated with sodium and calcium ions is governed by ion-specific solvation and hydrogen-bond network reorganisation. The title identifies these molecular mechanisms as the cause of the unusual transport behaviour.

For engineers running PEM electrolysers or fuel cells, metal cation contamination is a known cause of performance loss. The finding that transport changes nonmonotonically, rather than simply degrading, suggests the relationship between contaminant concentration and membrane conductivity is more complex than a linear decline. Understanding the role of solvation and hydrogen-bond structure could improve diagnostic and mitigation strategies.
