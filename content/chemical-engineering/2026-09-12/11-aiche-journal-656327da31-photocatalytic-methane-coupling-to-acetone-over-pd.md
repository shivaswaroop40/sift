Researchers report direct photocatalytic synthesis of acetone from methane as the sole carbon source over TiO2-supported Pd clusters at ambient conditions. The catalyst achieved an acetone yield of 20.7 ± 1.1 μmol g−1 h−1 with a liquid-phase acetone selectivity of 59.7% under light irradiation.

Direct oxidative coupling of methane to C3 oxygenates is an attractive route for upgrading natural gas, but it is held back by difficult C-H activation and uncontrollable C-C coupling. This result matters because it shows a mild, light-driven path to a liquid C3 product, which could inform new process concepts for converting methane without high-temperature thermal coupling.

The authors attribute the performance to the electronic metal-support interaction between Pd clusters and TiO2, which enhances separation of photogenerated charge carriers. That promotes water oxidation by photogenerated holes to form hydroxyl radicals. In situ spectroscopy and theory calculations point to a hydroxyl-radical-mediated activation-coupling pathway on the Pd clusters.
