The study used computational fluid dynamics and dimensional analysis to model scale-up of spoked rotating disk reactors. It found that the window openings create two liquid films: a wall-bounded film and a confined-free film. These films allow gravity-driven stretching, thinning and normal-velocity fluctuations that solid disks do not produce. The analysis covered viscosity, rotational speed and window geometry.

This matters because spoked RDRs are used for gas-liquid mass transfer in high-viscosity systems, but their scale-up has been hard to predict. The new dimensionless scaling laws link film thickness, surface renewal frequency and energy consumption to window geometry. The authors conclude that mass-transfer enhancement at industrial scale depends mainly on geometric optimisation of the windows, not just operating conditions.

The findings come from simulation and dimensional analysis, not from physical experiments. The abstract does not report numerical values or validation data. Practitioners should treat the scaling laws as a framework for further testing rather than as proven design correlations.
