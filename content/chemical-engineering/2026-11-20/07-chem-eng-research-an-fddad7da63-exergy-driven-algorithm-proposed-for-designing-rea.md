A paper in Chemical Engineering Research and Design presents an exergy-driven algorithm for designing intensified reactive distillation columns. The title says it targets eco-efficient designs. It reports validation on two cases, ethylene oxide hydrolysis and cumene production.

The source text gives only the title. There is no abstract, so the method, the exergy metric used, the column configurations compared and any savings figures are not available here. No performance claims can be stated.

For practitioners, the interest is in the approach. Exergy analysis can locate irreversibilities that energy balances hide, and applying it to column design could guide choices on feed stages, reactive zones and internal heat integration. Whether this algorithm does that better than existing methods cannot be judged from the title alone.
