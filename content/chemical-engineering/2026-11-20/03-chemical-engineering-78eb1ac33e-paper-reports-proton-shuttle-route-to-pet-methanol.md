A paper in Chemical Engineering Journal describes a proton shuttle-mediated route to activate hydrogen at low pressure for the methanolysis of polyethylene terephthalate (PET). The title is the only text available. It names the approach and the reaction, but gives no catalyst, conditions, yields or selectivities.

Methanolysis is an established way to depolymerise PET to dimethyl terephthalate and ethylene glycol. Using hydrogen at low pressure, if the claim holds, could reduce reactor pressure ratings and compression costs. That would matter for any scale-up or retrofit of chemical recycling.

The title says only that the process is efficient. Without the abstract or data, it is not possible to say how the proton shuttle works, what the catalyst is, or how the route compares with conventional methanolysis.
