A paper in Chemical Engineering Journal addresses interfacial current crowding in CO2 electrolyzers. It proposes a dual-field transport design as the way to mitigate the problem. The source text gives only the title, so the mechanism, the cell format and any performance figures are not available here.

Current crowding means reaction rate concentrates in part of the electrode instead of spreading evenly. In CO2 electrolysis this can limit usable current density, and it can shift selectivity and wear the electrode unevenly. A design that evens out transport at the interface would bear on scale-up, where uniformity matters more than peak laboratory performance.
