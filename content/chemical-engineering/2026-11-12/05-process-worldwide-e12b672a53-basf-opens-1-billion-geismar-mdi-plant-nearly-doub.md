BASF has inaugurated its new MDI production facility at the Verbund site in Geismar, Louisiana. The project cost about $1 billion and is the company's largest wholly owned investment in North America. It almost doubles site MDI capacity to approximately 600,000 metric tons a year. BASF says it finished on time.

The work ran in three phases from 2018. A new MDI synthesis unit was commissioned in 2020, and several upstream units were expanded in 2021. The final phase added new upstream units and a splitter. BASF also raised aniline capacity at Geismar, which feeds both MDI and other markets such as agricultural chemicals, rubber, and dyes and pigments.

For those working on isocyanates or polyurethane supply chains, this adds a large block of North American MDI supply, integrated with upstream aniline on one site. BASF says the extra units increase production flexibility. The release gives no detail on technology, yields, or energy use, and the claims of state-of-the-art technology and top safety standards are unsupported by data.
