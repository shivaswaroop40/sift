Researchers combined an oscillatory flow reactor with a spray dryer to make binder-free UTSA-16(Zn) microspheres. The reactor produced powder with a median diameter of 3.74 μm in 15 min. The powder was redispersed in water and spray dried, giving microspheres of 54.51 μm median diameter at a recovery yield of 71.4%.

The microspheres kept the crystalline structure of UTSA-16(Zn) and showed nearly zero cohesion, which suggests good flowability. They were mesoporous, with a BET surface area of 562.0 m2/g. CO2 uptake was 2.97 mmol/g at 30°C and 1 bar.

After 100 temperature swing adsorption cycles, capacity fell by only about 1%. For anyone scaling MOF sorbents for carbon capture, shaping is a persistent obstacle, as binders usually dilute capacity and block pores. This route avoids the binder and uses short, continuous-style unit operations. The abstract gives no data on throughput, cost, flue gas conditions or mechanical strength, so scale-up claims remain untested.
