A study in Chemical Engineering Science reports that nanobubbles can act as nucleation sites to control pharmaceutical crystallisation. The work investigates how surface-stabilised gas pockets in solution lower the energy barrier for crystal nucleation and steer which polymorph or particle form appears.

For process engineers this matters because nucleation control governs crystal size distribution, polymorph selectivity and batch reproducibility. Using nanobubbles as seeds could offer a non-chemical route to tune product attributes without changing the active ingredient formulation or adding heterogeneous foreign particles.

The source provided is a title only, so the specific API, the bubble generation method and quantitative outcomes such as induction time or particle size are not stated. Readers should treat the mechanism as a reported finding rather than a confirmed scale-up route.
