A process-informed co-optimisation framework links liquid air energy storage (LAES) with AI data centre (AIDC) workload scheduling, capturing charging start-up dynamics and off-design discharge behaviour rather than treating the store as a black box. The model is formulated as a mixed-integer program with a robust extension for joint energy and reserve market participation.

Tested against CAISO, PJM and ERCOT data, LAES–AIDC integration cuts operating costs by 9–50.9% in energy markets and up to 66.1% when reserve services are included. Optimal scheduling is governed by both price signals and the need to keep LAES near efficient thermodynamic regimes, with AIDC workload flexibility reshaping demand to suit the store.

The work shows that process-level detail, particularly off-design discharge performance, materially changes the value of thermal storage in grid applications. For engineers designing hybrid energy-storage and computing infrastructure, it indicates that embedding first-principles thermodynamics into dispatch models is worth the added complexity.
