The study takes an oscillating feedback microreactor as a model chaotic microreactor and scales it up proportionally in three dimensions. It combines CFD with experiments. The authors link flow structures, chaotic intensity (Lyapunov exponent) and mixing performance through empirical correlations with inertia-intensification, intrinsic and scale-factor terms.

The correlations describe 1× to 3× reactors quantitatively. At 6× and 12× the kinematic predictions deviate, but the reactors still show the feedback-driven flow and effective mixing. The abstract gives no figures for the size of the deviations.

Scale-up of microreactors usually fails because mixing changes with size. Here the authors report continuous synthesis of FePO4 and Ce2(C2O4)3 at several scales, with consistent particle morphology and comparable LiFePO4 electrochemical performance. That is relevant to battery-material precipitation.

The evidence is a CFD and lab study on two precipitation systems. The correlations hold only up to 3×, so design at 6× and 12× rests on retained flow behaviour rather than quantitative prediction. Claims of industrial guidance should be read with that limit in mind.
