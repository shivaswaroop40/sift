Maire's subsidiary Nextchem has signed a binding agreement to acquire a metals and chemicals processing technology business from Metso. It covers fluidized-bed roasting and treatment of sulphur-bearing metallurgical off-gases. The enterprise value is €40 million, with €32 million upfront and an €8 million earn-out. Closing is expected in mid-2027, subject to regulatory approvals and a carve-out.

The business holds more than 60 patent families, including fluidized-bed reactors, cyclone and gas-solid separation, and hydrogen use in fluidized-bed systems. It lists over 1,000 references, of which 300 are roasting, with a leading position in zinc and pyrite. It also has more than 100 gas cleaning installations for mercury, arsenic and fluorine removal.

For engineers, this moves a long-established roasting and gas cleaning technology supplier under an engineering group. Licensing, equipment and aftermarket service would sit alongside Nextchem's inorganic chemistry and critical raw materials offering. Metso says the sale sharpens its focus on minerals processing and metals refining.

The business has about 180 staff, with headquarters in Oberursel, an R&D centre in Frankfurt, and sites in Sweden and India. Nextchem expects commercial synergies with Tecnimont. The source gives no revenue or earnings figures for the business.
