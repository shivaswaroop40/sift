A paper in AIChE Journal presents a coupled CFD model at the local packed-bed scale for DMT hydrogenation in a trickle-bed reactor. It examines wetting behaviour, flow distribution, catalyst geometry and interactions between particles. For isolated particles, the authors relate internal effectiveness factor to Thiele modulus for complete and partial wetting.

They fitted empirical correlations that include wetting fraction. The reported fits are R² of 0.9984 and 0.9974, with mean absolute percentage errors of 2.50% and 3.21%, for the two wetting states.

External flow simulations showed that local velocity variations create uneven surface concentrations and diffusion limits. Higher liquid velocity eased this. Trilobe catalysts improved internal diffusion but added external transport resistance. In multi-particle arrays, liquid held up between particles weakened reactant transport downstream, and effectiveness factor fell progressively along the bed.

The correlations could help where effectiveness factors are currently taken from complete-wetting assumptions. The shape trade-off and the downstream decline are the practical points. The work is a simulation study of one reaction, and the abstract does not mention experimental validation.
