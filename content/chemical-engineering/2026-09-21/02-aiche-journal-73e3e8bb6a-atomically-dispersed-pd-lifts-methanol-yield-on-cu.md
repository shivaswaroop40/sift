Researchers in China report that atomically dispersed Pd on a covalent triazine framework-supported Cu catalyst boosts CO2 hydrogenation to methanol. Their best catalyst, 2.5Pd/CZC, gave a methanol space–time yield of 1217 mg MeOH per gram of Cu+Pd per hour with 80% selectivity at 240 °C and 5 MPa, 1.86 times the rate of the Pd-free Cu catalyst.

Bulk Pd is known to speed hydrogen dissociation on Cu but also drives side reactions that lower methanol selectivity. The team compared atomic and aggregated Pd on the same CTF support and found the atomic form balances H2 dissociation and H* binding, suppressing the competing pathways that aggregated Pd promotes.

The work points to atomic-scale promoter engineering, rather than simple metal alloying, as a route to higher selectivity in CO2-to-methanol catalysts. Practical reactor conditions, long-term stability and scale-up were not addressed in the abstract.
