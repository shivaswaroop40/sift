The authors made Pd@Aux core–shell catalysts with controlled Au coverage by H₂ purging, then tested direct H₂O2 synthesis in a microreactor at ambient conditions using pure water as the solvent. Activity followed a volcano trend with Pd/Au ratio, peaking at about 1:1, where the catalyst reached 1785 ppm H₂O2 and a yield of 190.8 mol per kg catalyst per hour.

DFT modelling reproduced the volcano behaviour and identified the Pd–Au₃ site as optimal. Its d-band centre sits at −1.80 eV, in the Sabatier regime, giving the lowest free energy for O₂ protonation to *OOH (−0.15 eV) and the highest barrier against the competing four-electron reduction to water at 1.31 eV.

For practitioners, the work links Au coverage to both Pd electronic structure and selectivity, offering a concrete design lever rather than empirical alloy screening. The 1785 ppm figure is a stated result from a small microreactor under mild conditions; scale-up, long-term stability, and selectivity at high conversion are not addressed in the abstract and would need checking before any process claim.

The paper claims rational guidance for PdAu catalyst design based on tuning d-band position through Au loading, but the data presented is limited to a narrow microreactor study and would need reactor and lifetime validation.
