A catalyst based on in-situ generated NiOOH species sustained urea-assisted water electrolysis at an industrial-scale current density of 1500 mA cm-2 for 1100 hours, according to work in Chemical Engineering Journal.

Urea electrolysis is of interest as a lower-energy route to hydrogen and a way to treat urea-rich wastewater, but anode degradation at high current has limited practical use. Operating continuously at 1500 mA cm-2 for over a thousand hours addresses that durability gap and brings the process closer to commercial electrolyser requirements.

The source text gives only the title, so the specific electrode composition, reaction mechanism and faradaic efficiency are not reported here and would need to be checked in the paper before quoting performance figures.
