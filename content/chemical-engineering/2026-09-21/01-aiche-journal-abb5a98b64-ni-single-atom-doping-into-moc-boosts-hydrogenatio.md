Researchers have prepared a nickel single-atom-doped α-MoC1−x catalyst with up to 5 wt% Ni via one-step carburisation, achieving high-density atomic dispersion within the carbide lattice. Ni promotes low-temperature formation of the α-MoC1−x phase, and Mo–C sites act as the real active centres for hydrogenation of carbonyls, nitroarenes, and N-heterocycles.

The work shows Ni single atoms trigger d-p orbital rehybridisation and an upward shift of the d-band centre, which optimises adsorption, dissociation, and migration of hydrogen species. For process chemists, this gives a cheaper carbide alternative to noble metals for selective hydrogenation, with the activity gain attributed to electronic restructuring rather than new active sites.

The claims rest on a model system tested on model substrates, and scale-up or long-term stability data are not presented in the abstract. Ni loadings up to 5 wt% were achieved in a single carburisation step, which is unusual for single-atom catalysts and may have implications for cost and reproducibility at scale.
