Researchers applied low-intensity ultrasound as a pre-acclimation step to partial nitrification sludge before exposure to the rare earth element La(III). The treatment improved ammonia oxidation performance and nitrite accumulation under lanthanum stress compared with untreated biomass.

The protective effect is attributed to two linked changes in the sludge. Ultrasound pre-acclimation increased extracellular polymeric substance production, which binds metal ions, and it amplified antioxidant enzyme activity, limiting oxidative damage to nitrifying bacteria.

For practitioners handling wastewater with rare earth inputs or metal variability, the work points to a non-chemical conditioning step that could stabilise partial nitrification. The reported benefit depends on ultrasound intensity and exposure time, which the source does not specify beyond "low-intensity".

The evidence appears limited to bench-scale experiments. Scale-up energy costs and any effect on downstream denitrification stages are not addressed in the available text.
