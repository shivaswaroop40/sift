Researchers report that adding boron to a ruthenium on carbon catalyst accelerates the alkaline hydrogen oxidation reaction by tuning both the metal's electronic structure and the interfacial water layer.

The doping raised activity in alkaline media, where the reaction is sluggish compared with acidic conditions. Faster hydrogen oxidation matters for anion exchange membrane fuel cells, which depend on cheap catalysts that survive in base.

Details on turnover rates, stability and competing mechanisms are limited in the available text. The boron effect is described as synergistic between electronic and interfacial water changes, though the source does not give a quantitative split.

The source is a paper title and abstract stub, so the strength of the claim is not assessable from this short text alone.
