Researchers have demonstrated a cost-guided autonomous synthesis framework, called CASS, that combines physical models with experimental feedback to optimise multicomponent Na superionic conductor (NASICON) solid electrolytes.

In 78 trials at the A-Lab, the system identified 18 promising phase-pure compositions. Two of these showed total ionic conductivity of 0.7 and 0.3 mS/cm, with bulk values of 3.96 and 3.17 mS/cm respectively.

For battery and solid electrolyte developers, the work shows that coupling physics-informed models with autonomous synthesis can cut through the combinatorial search space of doped structural frameworks faster than manual trial-and-error.

The framework also records failed syntheses, so the cost function can be refined as the campaign runs, providing interpretable design rules rather than a black-box recommendation.
