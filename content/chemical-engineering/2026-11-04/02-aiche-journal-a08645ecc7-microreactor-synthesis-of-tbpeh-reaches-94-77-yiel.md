Researchers built a continuous-flow microreaction platform for tert-butyl peroxy(2-ethylhexanoate), a polymerisation initiator, and fitted an apparent kinetic model to optimise it. KOH served as the base, and the team mapped the stable operating window of the tert-butyl hydroperoxide alkali metal salt system. At a KOH/TBHP ratio of 1.01, a TBHP/EHCl ratio of 1.25, 45°C and 140 s residence time, the process reached 99.71% EHCl conversion, 95.05% selectivity and 94.77% yield.

The work targets the low efficiency and poor process safety of the conventional semi-batch route. Peroxyesters are reactive initiators, and a microreactor's small inventory and rapid heat transfer suit their manufacture. The authors present the method as a template for continuous processes for related peroxyesters, so the operating window and model could transfer to similar chemistries.

Kinetic modelling showed both equivalence ratios affect performance, but the TBHP/EHCl ratio dominates. That points designers to feed ratio as the main lever, ahead of base stoichiometry. The model is apparent rather than mechanistic, so extrapolation beyond the studied window should be treated with care.
