A paper in Chemical Engineering Science reports that the heat of CO2 reaction in blended amine solvents is non-additive. The value cannot be obtained by averaging the reaction heats of the component amines. The authors attribute the deviation to synergistic interactions between amine molecules, driven by intermolecular hydrogen bonds.

Reaction enthalpy feeds directly into stripper heat duty and regeneration energy estimates for amine capture plants. If blended solvents deviate from linear mixing, screening and simulation cannot interpolate from pure-amine data. Property models would need to represent the hydrogen-bond interactions between amines instead.

Only the article title is available here. The amines studied, the size of the deviation from additivity, and whether the evidence is experimental or computational are not stated. The hydrogen-bond mechanism is the authors' explanation, not a claim checked against the full paper.
