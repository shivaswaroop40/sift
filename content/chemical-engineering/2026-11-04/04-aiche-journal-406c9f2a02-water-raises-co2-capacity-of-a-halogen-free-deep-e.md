Researchers built halogen-free deep eutectic solvents from tertiary alkanolamines and alkyl-substituted imidazoles. The amines were triethanolamine and methyldiethanolamine. The imidazoles were 4-methylimidazole, 2-ethyl-4-methylimidazole and 2,4-dimethylimidazole. The 2E4MIM:MDEA (1:2) solvent gave the best CO2 absorption of those tested. Cyclic tests showed good reversibility for the 2E4MIM-based solvents. Spectroscopy and quantum chemical calculations identified two plausible proton transfer pathways.

Water content governs the choice. With water added, the 2E4MIM and 2,4-DMIM systems turn solid after CO2 absorption, driven by bicarbonate formation. Solids in an absorption loop would hamper pumping and contactor operation. The 4MIM:MDEA (1:2) system stays liquid, and its capacity rises from 0.101 to 0.183 g-CO2/g-DES as water content goes from 0 to 20 wt%.

The halogen-free formulation targets non-corrosive operation, and reversibility in cyclic tests means the solvent can be regenerated and reused. Flue gas normally carries water, so tolerance of it matters at scale. The evidence is bench-scale absorption data with spectral and quantum chemical support. No pilot-scale or long-duration running is reported.
