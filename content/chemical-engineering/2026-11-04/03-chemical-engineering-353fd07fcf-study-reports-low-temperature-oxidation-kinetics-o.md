Chemical Engineering Journal has published a study of low-temperature oxidation kinetics in hydrogen-blended natural gas surrogates. The work used a jet-stirred reactor operated at 24 atm. Only the title is available at the time of writing. The blend compositions, temperature range and measured species data are not given here, so no quantitative findings can be reported.

The topic matters to anyone handling hydrogen in gas networks. Low-temperature oxidation chemistry governs autoignition behaviour, a concern wherever blended gas is compressed, stored or moved by pipeline. High-pressure jet-stirred reactor data of this kind feeds kinetic mechanism validation, which in turn supports autoignition prediction and safety cases for blended fuel systems.
