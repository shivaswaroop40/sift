Researchers have made a hydrophobic porous ionic liquid, LADDA-PIL, by adding silver to ZIF-90 and dispersing the material in a hydrophobic ionic liquid. The Type III porous liquid showed a CO2/N2 selectivity of 2021 for a 15/85 gas mixture, surpassing nearly all previously reported porous liquids, and high CO2 absorption capacity. Dynamic breakthrough tests showed it captured CO2 from humid flue gas.

Water normally interferes with CO2 capture from flue gas, so removing moisture adds cost. Here the hydrophobic ionic liquid acts as a water barrier, while the NH2 group in the ionic liquid helps transport and convert CO2. That points to a lower-energy route for carbon capture without drying the gas first.

The same material converted CO2 with propargylic alcohols into cyclic carbonates at ambient temperature. The reaction relied on pi-interaction between silver and the alkynyl group. Density functional theory and molecular dynamics simulations supported the mechanism. The authors describe the approach as a promising strategy for energy-efficient carbon capture under humid conditions.
