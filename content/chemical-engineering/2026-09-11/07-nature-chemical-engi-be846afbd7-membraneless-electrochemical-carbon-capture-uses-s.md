A study published online in Nature Chemical Engineering describes a membraneless architecture for electrochemically mediated carbon capture. The design uses solid-state counter-electrodes instead of ion-exchange membranes, which the authors say addresses the limitations of membrane-based systems. The work was published on 8 September 2026.

The study also identifies the mechanism behind unwanted charge loss. It is governed by ion diffusion through the solid, not by thermodynamics alone. That distinction gives a practical materials design principle for carbon capture and related energy technologies.

For process engineers, this matters because membrane degradation and resistance often limit electrochemical capture systems. A membraneless route could simplify cell construction and operation, though the study is at the materials and mechanism stage rather than a full process demonstration.
