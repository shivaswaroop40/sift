This paper investigates hydrate deposition in a deepwater well during shut-in, when flow stops and a sustained thermal gradient develops across the wellbore. It focuses on two factors: the volatility of the hydrate inhibitor and the phase state of the emulsion present in the system.

Shut-in is a high-risk period for flow assurance because stagnant fluids cool and hydrates can form and deposit on surfaces. The results should help engineers choose inhibitors and account for emulsion behaviour when planning shutdowns and restart procedures.
