Eni CCUS Holding has installed the Douglas CCS platform in Liverpool Bay, designed as the offshore hub for the HyNet industrial decarbonization cluster. The facility comprises a 1,885-ton topside, a 1,755-ton jacket and eight piles weighing 730 tons combined, receiving captured CO2 from North West England and North Wales for transport through repurposed pipelines to depleted gas reservoirs.

For process engineers, this demonstrates how existing offshore infrastructure can be adapted for CO2 transport and storage at scale. The project reuses depleted natural gas reservoirs and established pipeline networks, reducing capital cost and technical risk compared to building new systems from scratch.

The platform was delivered in 18 months on an accelerated schedule. The full Liverpool Bay CCS network is approximately 50% complete, with around 60% of expenditure flowing through U.K. supply chains and supporting over 2,000 construction jobs and 200 to 300 permanent roles.
