A biphasic solvent of phenoxyethanol and sulphuric acid at a 4:1 ratio removed 62.9% of lignin-carbohydrate complexes from bamboo residues and achieved 39.6% glucose yield after 72 hours of enzymatic hydrolysis. NMR showed that phenyl glyceride and benzyl ether linkages were the main degradation sites.

The finding matters because lignocellulosic fractionation is central to biochemical conversion of agricultural residue into fermentation feedstock. Improving pretreatment efficiency cuts enzyme loading and residence time, lowering capital and operating costs in biorefinery design.

Molecular modelling showed phenoxyethanol increases the solvent-accessible surface area of the complexes, allowing acid-catalysed protonation to cleave bonds while the solvent stabilises intermediates through hydrogen bonding. This dual mechanism explains why the biphasic system outperforms single-solvent approaches.
