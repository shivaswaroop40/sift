Researchers at Indian Institute of Science used machine learning and quantum mechanics to identify 9,389 chemical reactions on a copper catalyst for CO2 hydrogenation to methanol. Their original model contained only 152 reactions. The expanded network predicted CO2 conversion 40 times higher than the smaller model and correctly identified methanol as the major product, matching experiments.

The work matters because conventional mechanistic models typically omit hundreds of potentially critical reactions due to computational limits. Missing steps can lead to wrong product predictions and underestimated conversion rates. A complete reaction network is essential for rational catalyst design in CO2 utilization.

The study revealed an unexpected mechanism: hydrogen can transfer to intermediates as molecular H2 rather than only as separate atoms. This challenges conventional assumptions and suggests catalysts that interact more strongly with H2 could enhance methanol production pathways.
