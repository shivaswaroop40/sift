Layer melt crystallization separates ultrahigh-purity chemicals, especially difficult isomeric pairs. Researchers built a semi-analytical model for static LMC combining temperature evolution, heat flux, crystal growth kinetics, and solute partitioning. Tests on 2,4-/2,6-dinitrochlorobenzene isomers validated the framework experimentally.

For process design, latent heat accounts for over 95 per cent of total heat transfer, while thermal resistance exceeds mass transfer resistance by tenfold. This tells you where to focus control and equipment sizing: manage cooling rate primarily through thermal rather than mass-transport constraints.

Crystal growth rate follows a power law with interfacial temperature gradient. The solute distribution coefficient falls as growth accelerates due to impurity entrapment. Higher supercooling amplifies impurity enrichment at the interface, a trade-off to consider when setting operating conditions.
