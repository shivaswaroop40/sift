Researchers developed a photoelectrochemical cell that uses sunlight to drive lithium extraction from brine through an ion-sieve membrane. The system achieves selective lithium separation without external electrical power, relying instead on photogenerated voltage from illuminated electrodes.

For lithium processing operations, this matters because conventional extraction requires significant electrical input and consumes water. Direct solar driving could reduce operating costs and carbon intensity, particularly valuable for distributed brine sources where grid power is expensive or distant.

The ion-sieve selectivity addresses a practical problem: extracted lithium must be separated from competing sodium and potassium ions present in much higher concentrations. The photoelectrochemical approach couples this selectivity with renewable energy input.
