Researchers developed a deep eutectic solvent from L-arginine and glycerol that solubilizes kraft lignin and enables simultaneous depolymerization and fermentation by Pseudomonas putida KT2440. The system integrates solvent use, lignin breakdown, and microbial conversion in one process.

For process engineers, this addresses a major bottleneck in lignocellulose valorization. Kraft lignin is a pulping byproduct available in large volumes but poorly utilized. Combining solubilization and fermentation in a tailored medium could reduce unit operations and lower conversion costs.

The deep eutectic solvent composition is water-based and uses biocompatible constituents, which matters for downstream fermentation. The organism can tolerate the solvent environment whilst breaking down and consuming the depolymerized lignin fragments.
