Australian researchers demonstrated solar-driven conversion of glycerol, a biodiesel byproduct, into hydrogen and valuable organic chemicals using a nickel-cobalt modified zinc indium sulfide catalyst. Under visible light, the catalyst increased hydrogen evolution tenfold and steered glycerol toward glyceraldehyde and dihydroxyacetone, compounds worth 75 to 150 dollars per kilogram versus 0.11 dollars for crude glycerol.

For process engineers, this addresses a real disposal problem: biodiesel production generates 100 kilograms of glycerol per tonne of fuel. Photoreforming captures two values simultaneously, producing hydrogen whilst converting waste into pharmaceutical and chemical feedstocks rather than disposal costs.

Synchrotron spectroscopy revealed the mechanism: nickel and cobalt sites transform under illumination from positive ions to reduced states that form the active catalyst. These changes reverse when light switches off, showing the process is driven entirely by photons and repeatable at scale.
