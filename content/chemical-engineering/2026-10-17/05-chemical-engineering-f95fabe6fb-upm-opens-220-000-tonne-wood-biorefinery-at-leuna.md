UPM inaugurated its Leuna biorefinery in Germany in October 2026. The facility converts lignocellulosic biomass from sustainably managed forests and sawmill residues into biochemicals at 220,000 metric tonnes per year capacity. Enzymatic hydrolysis and lignin-derived specialty chemical production have reached stable operation, with sales ramping.

For process engineers, this demonstrates commercial-scale deployment of enzymatic hydrolysis for biomass conversion. The facility draws beechwood from within 200 kilometres, unsuitable for timber, converting it to industrial sugars and lignin that replace fossil inputs in chemicals, packaging, cosmetics and tyres. The €1.37 billion investment proves the economics work at scale.

The breakthrough matters because it shows renewable carbon solutions moving from pilot phase into competitive manufacturing. European manufacturers now have a domestic source of bio-based chemical feedstocks, reducing dependence on imported fossil value chains and improving supply security for downstream producers.
