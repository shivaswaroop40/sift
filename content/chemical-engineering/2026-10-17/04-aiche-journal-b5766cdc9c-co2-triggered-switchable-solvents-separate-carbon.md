Researchers anchored DBU/alcohol pairs onto UiO-66-NH2 nanoparticles to create switchable porous deep eutectic solvents. Carbon dioxide transforms these solvents into porous ionic liquids; nitrogen at 50°C reverses the process. The materials achieved good separation of CO2/C2H2 and CO2/CH4 mixtures.

The switchable mechanism matters for process flexibility. The DBU/alcohol pair selectively recognises CO2 through the functional layer, as confirmed by simulation. The approach is general: different porous hosts can replace UiO-66-NH2, widening the design space.

This is claimed as the first report of CO2-driven switching between deep eutectic solvents and ionic liquids in porous form. Reversibility by heating with inert gas suggests a regeneration pathway without chemical additives.
