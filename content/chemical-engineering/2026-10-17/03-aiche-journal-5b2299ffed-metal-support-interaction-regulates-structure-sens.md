Ni/MgAlOx catalysts derived from layered double hydroxides show nonmonotonic activity with a maximum at 9.2 nm particle size, while maintaining above 99% CH4 selectivity under high-conversion conditions. This contrasts with Ni/Al2O3, which shows monotonic activity increase with particle size. The MgAlOx support provides weaker Ni-O coordination than Al2O3.

For CO2 methanation design, this means the support environment fundamentally controls size-dependent performance rather than particle size alone. The findings help explain why catalysts with similar particle sizes perform differently across supports. Understanding this coupling is essential for optimizing catalyst selection and predicting scale-up behaviour.

MgAlOx regulates the reaction pathway by promoting a CO-mediated route over formate-associated transformation, balancing CO adsorption and hydrogen availability. This mechanism explains why the optimum particle size exists and why high selectivity is maintained despite high conversion.
