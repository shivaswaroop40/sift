Utility Global and Tata Steel have announced joint front-end engineering and design for an H2Gen unit at Tata's integrated steelworks in Kalinganagar, Odisha. The unit would use a mix of blast furnace gas and basic oxygen furnace gas to make 10 tons per day of hydrogen from water. The FEED work is meant to support a final investment decision.

H2Gen is proprietary. The companies say it makes hydrogen from water without electricity, using residual energy in industrial off-gases. It also yields a separate concentrated carbon dioxide stream, which is claimed to reduce the cost and complexity of carbon capture. At Kalinganagar, the hydrogen could be injected back into a blast furnace.

For blast furnace operators, the appeal is reuse of existing assets and off-gas rather than a new electrolyser plus renewable power. Ten tons per day is small against a steelworks, and it is Utility's largest announced H2Gen deployment. The source gives no cost, capture rate or emissions figures, and no FID has been taken. The economic claims come from the companies.
