Researchers studied how amine jets break apart and disperse when they hit rotating wire meshes in packed bed contactors. The meshes rotate at high speed, shearing the liquid jet into fine droplets and spreading them across the surface. This increases the contact area between amine solution and gas.

The mechanism matters for industrial CO2 capture. Better dispersion means higher mass transfer rates and more efficient solvent use in post-combustion or direct air capture units. Rotating packed beds already offer high throughput; understanding the jet physics lets engineers optimise column design and operating conditions.
