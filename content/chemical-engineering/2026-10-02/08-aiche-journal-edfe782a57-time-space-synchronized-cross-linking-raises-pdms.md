Researchers used a vapor–liquid interface-confined method to cross-link polydimethylsiloxane membranes such that all cross-linking sites react simultaneously across the entire surface. This approach eliminates the viscosity-driven defects that plague conventional pre-crosslinking. The resulting membranes achieved a flux of 3400 g m−2 h−1 and separation factor of 8.8 when separating 5 wt% ethanol–water solution.

Cross-linking uniformity directly affects membrane structure. Synchronized reaction yields larger free volume, more uniform pores, and higher cross-linking density than stepwise premixing. Higher flux with maintained selectivity cuts energy cost per unit separation. The team demonstrated stable performance at 2500 cm² scale, moving beyond laboratory synthesis.
