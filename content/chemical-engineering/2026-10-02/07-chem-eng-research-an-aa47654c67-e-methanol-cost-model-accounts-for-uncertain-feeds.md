Researchers applied a possibilistic uncertainty model to cost analysis of e-methanol production. The method captures ranges in feedstock and energy prices without assuming probability distributions. This allows the cost estimate to reflect real market variability rather than point estimates alone.

E-methanol is a route to decarbonise synthesis and fuels. Cost uncertainty drives investment decisions, so methods that map the full cost envelope matter to process designers and engineers evaluating project viability. Possibilistic models suit this where historical data are sparse or price volatility is structurally uncertain.

The approach complements deterministic techno-economic assessment and simulation. It helps identify which cost drivers have the largest impact on final methanol price, informing where to focus process improvements or supply chain strategy.
