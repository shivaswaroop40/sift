Researchers at Max-Planck-Institut für Kohlenforschung have demonstrated that bench-stable alkylthianthrenium salts can serve as carbene precursors, replacing diazoalkanes which are toxic, unstable and potentially explosive. The salts showed no detectable exotherm in thermal analysis.

For process chemists, this matters because carbene chemistry is widely used but has carried safety and handling risks. Sulfonium salts were previously unsuitable as general carbene precursors due to their nucleophilicity, but thianthrene's electronic and steric properties overcome this limitation.

The work demonstrated cyclopropanation with diverse alkenes including unactivated internal alkenes, sigma-bond insertion, and sigmatropic rearrangements. Decagram-scale reactions were achieved using solvent-free ball milling, improving scalability and reducing solvent waste.
