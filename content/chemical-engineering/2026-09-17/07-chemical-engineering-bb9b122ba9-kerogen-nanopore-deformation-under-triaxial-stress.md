A study in Chemical Engineering Science examines how kerogen nanopores deform under different triaxial stress regimes and how this affects CH4 and CO2 adsorption. The work links mechanical loading to sorption behaviour in organic-matter-hosted pores typical of shale formations.

For shale gas production, pore deformation under in-situ stress changes accessible surface area and gas capacity. For carbon sequestration, the same mechanism governs how much CO2 competing with CH4 can be held in the kerogen matrix, which matters for both injectivity and storage estimates.

The paper treats stress and adsorption as coupled rather than independent, which is closer to reservoir conditions than the usual isostress-free isotherms. Practical use of the results will depend on the kerogen type and stress path assumed, which the abstract does not specify.
