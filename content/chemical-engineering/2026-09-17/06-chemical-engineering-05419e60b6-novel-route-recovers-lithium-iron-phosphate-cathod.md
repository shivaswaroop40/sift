A study in Chemical Engineering Science presents a new method to recover spent lithium iron phosphate (LFP) cathode material by co-extracting Fe(III) and phosphorus in a single step.

The process targets one of the fastest-growing streams of end-of-life batteries, where conventional recycling struggles to capture iron and phosphorus economically alongside lithium.

Co-extraction avoids separate purification trains for iron and phosphate, potentially reducing reagent use and waste compared with staged leaching and precipitation routes.

The paper claims a more comprehensive recovery route, though the abstract does not give yields, purity figures or reagent data, so the practical gains remain to be quantified.
