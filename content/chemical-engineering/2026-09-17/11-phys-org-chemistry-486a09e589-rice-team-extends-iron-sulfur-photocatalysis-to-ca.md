Researchers at Rice University report a method to add carbon building blocks to alkene carbon-carbon double bonds using iron, sulfur and purple light, published in Nature Catalysis. The previous version of the system could only attach highly electronegative elements such as fluorine to alkenes.

Carbon's reluctance to hold electron density made direct carbon addition difficult. The team used two cheap carboxylic acids to temporarily polarise the carbon so it behaved like a more electronegative atom, allowing the existing iron-sulfur photocatalytic cycle to handle it. A small shift in light colour was also needed.

For practising chemists this matters because it offers an alternative to palladium-catalysed cross-coupling. The reagents are inexpensive and widely available, the carboxylic acid is washed away after the reaction, and the only reported waste is a small amount of carbon dioxide. The reaction is described as anti-Markovnikov alkene hydroalkylation via iron photocatalysis.
