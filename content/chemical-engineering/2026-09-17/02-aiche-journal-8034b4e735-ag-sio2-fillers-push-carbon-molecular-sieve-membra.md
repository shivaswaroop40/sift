Polyimide-derived carbon molecular sieve membranes were loaded with Ag@SiO2 core-shell particles before pyrolysis. The fillers improved precursor thermal stability and, at 0.5 wt% loading, produced an O2 permeability of 1605.6 Barrer with an O2/N2 selectivity of 8.1, above the 2015 Robeson upper bound.

Gas adsorption measurements indicate Ag sites enhance O2 transport through facilitated adsorption, while SiO2 modulates the carbon matrix microstructure. H2 and CO2 permeabilities also rose with filler loading, though H2/N2 and CO2/N2 selectivities dropped slightly. Higher temperatures and lower feed pressures further improved gas transport.

For air separation practice, the work points to a simple filler strategy that could compete with polymeric and zeolite membranes for O2/N2 splits, though the claim rests on single-membrane lab data and the mechanism coupling Ag adsorption with SiO2 pore tuning needs independent confirmation.
