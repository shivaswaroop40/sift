Researchers have designed a porous liquid that pairs Mn-bpdc MOF particles with a dinitrogen-rich deep eutectic solvent (2EmimCl:CH3-Im) to separate 1,3-butadiene from C4 mixtures. The MOF contributes one-dimensional channels with molecular sieving and gate-opening behaviour, while imidazole rings engage in pi-pi interactions with the diene and chloride boosts affinity through an inductive effect.

At 5 wt% MOF loading, the 1,3-butadiene/1-butene selectivity rises from 9.20 to 17.21 and the material keeps its performance over five adsorption cycles. Quantum calculations point to a mix of hydrogen bonding, pi-pi stacking, dispersion and electrostatics driving the uptake.

The work is at bench scale and the source is an abstract, so absolute capacity numbers and regeneration energy demand are not given. Even so, the result suggests a route to replace energy-intensive extractive or membrane steps in C4 splitting for synthetic rubber and polymer feedstocks.
