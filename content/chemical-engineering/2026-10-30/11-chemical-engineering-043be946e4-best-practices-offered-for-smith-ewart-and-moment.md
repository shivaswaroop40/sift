A paper in Chemical Engineering Science sets out best practices for implementing two population balance approaches used in (mini)emulsion polymerisation modelling. One is the multidimensional Smith-Ewart equation framework. The other is the compartmentalised moment equation method. The focus, per the title, is on how these equation systems are built and solved rather than on new polymerisation kinetics.

Population balance models are the standard route to predicting particle size distributions and conversion in emulsion reactors. Smith-Ewart equations and moment methods are the two workhorse formulations. Their multidimensional forms carry real numerical demands, so implementation choices bear directly on model accuracy and computational cost. That makes the paper relevant to anyone building or maintaining a reactor model.
