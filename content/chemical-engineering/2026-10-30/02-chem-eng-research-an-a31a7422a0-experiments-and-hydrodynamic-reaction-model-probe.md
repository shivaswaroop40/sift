Chemical Engineering Research and Design has published a coupled experimental and modelling study of catalytic methane pyrolysis over nickel-iron catalysts. The reactor is a bubbling fluidized bed. The model links bed hydrodynamics with the reaction, so bubble behaviour and gas-solid contact enter the prediction alongside catalyst kinetics.

Methane pyrolysis splits methane into hydrogen and solid carbon, offering hydrogen without the direct carbon dioxide emissions of steam reforming. Bubbling beds suit the duty because they move the solid carbon out and transfer heat well. But bubbles bypass gas and shorten contact time, so reactor output hinges on hydrodynamics as much as on catalyst activity. Coupled models of this kind are what scale-up work needs.
