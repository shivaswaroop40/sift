Eneos has inaugurated a large-scale demonstration plant in Queensland, Australia, that makes methylcyclohexane (MCH), a hydrogen carrier, by its Direct MCH route. The process electrochemically synthesises the organic hydride in a single step. Trial operations come first, with demonstration runs due to start in January 2027 and continue for roughly two years. The plant uses a 1.2 MW-class electrolyzer, about eight times larger than the unit in its 2023 demonstration, and is designed for up to 9 tons of MCH per day.

The single-step synthesis is Eneos's route to cutting the overall cost of green hydrogen supply chains, where production and transport costs remain the main barrier. The 1.2 MW-class electrolyzer is the minimum unit size for commercial deployment, so the plant tests the building block a commercial chain would use. Over roughly two years of runs, Eneos will measure performance, degradation behaviour and other operational characteristics.

The Queensland site sits in one of the world's leading renewable energy regions, and Eneos chose it to surface technical challenges in plant operation early. Commercialisation is not assured. Eneos says it will assess technological progress, economic viability and market conditions before deploying the technology.
