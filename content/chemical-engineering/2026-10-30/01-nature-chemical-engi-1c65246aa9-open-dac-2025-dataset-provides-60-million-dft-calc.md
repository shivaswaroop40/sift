Nature Chemical Engineering has published the Open DAC 2025 dataset, an open collection of adsorption data for direct air capture sorbents. It holds nearly 60 million DFT calculations of CO2, H2O, N2 and O2 adsorption in roughly 15,000 deformable metal-organic frameworks. Machine-learned potentials built on the data enable high-resolution screening of MOF candidates.

Screening MOFs for direct air capture has needed accurate, scalable models, and running DFT across thousands of frameworks is expensive. An open dataset at this scale gives engineers a shared base for training and benchmarking adsorption models without funding their own quantum calculations. The gas coverage, CO2, H2O, N2 and O2, matches the components of ambient air.

The published abstract is brief. It gives no accuracy figures for the machine-learned potentials, no detail on how framework deformation was treated, and no indication of which MOF families dominate the 15,000-framework set. Engineers should read the full paper before relying on the data for design choices.
