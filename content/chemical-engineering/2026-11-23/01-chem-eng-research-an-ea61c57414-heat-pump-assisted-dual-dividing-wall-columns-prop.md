A paper in Chemical Engineering Research and Design describes intensified, optimised processes for recovering isopropanol, butanol and ethanol (IBE) together from fermentation broth. The title says the design progresses from a seven-column baseline to a heat-pump-assisted arrangement with two dividing-wall columns (DWCs).

The source text is the title only, with no abstract. It gives no figures for energy use, capital cost, purity or recovery, so the size of any saving cannot be stated from this text.

For anyone working on biobutanol or mixed-alcohol separations, the direction is clear. Fewer columns, thermal coupling through dividing walls and heat pumping are the usual levers for cutting energy and footprint in a multi-product alcohol train. Whether this design delivers a real gain needs the full paper.
