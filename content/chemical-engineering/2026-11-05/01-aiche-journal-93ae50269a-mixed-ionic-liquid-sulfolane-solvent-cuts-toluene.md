A study proposes separating toluene from methylcyclohexane with a mixed solvent of imidazolium ionic liquids and sulfolane. COSMO-SAC screening and liquid-liquid equilibrium experiments identified [MMIM][DCA] with sulfolane as the system with the highest toluene selectivity. Quantum chemical calculations attribute the separation to π–π interactions. An Aspen Plus extraction flowsheet with heat exchange cut total annual cost by 19.53% against the conventional sulfolane process.

Toluene and methylcyclohexane have close boiling points and molecular sizes, so extractive separation is the practical route and sulfolane is the incumbent solvent. The work links solvent screening, equilibrium data, mechanism and flowsheet economics in one workflow. For engineers on aromatics recovery units, it gives a quantified case that an ionic liquid blend can beat sulfolane on cost once heat integration is included.

The 19.53% saving is a simulated figure from the Aspen Plus flowsheet with thermal integration, not plant data. It rests on the COSMO-SAC screening and the measured liquid-liquid equilibria. The abstract does not report solvent losses, viscosity or ionic liquid cost, which would shape any industrial case.
