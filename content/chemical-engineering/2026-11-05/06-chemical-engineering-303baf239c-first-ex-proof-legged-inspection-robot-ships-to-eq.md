Anybotics has begun shipping ANYmal X, which it calls the first legged inspection robot certified for explosive atmospheres. The robot meets both ATEX and IECEx standards. Early customers include Equinor and DSM-Firmenich. It carries thermal, visual, acoustic and gas sensors to detect overheating, leaks, abnormal noise and equipment wear, and navigates steep stairs and narrow walkways without facility modifications.

The target is the hazardous-zone inspection round. Standard robots are banned in these areas, so operators still inspect on foot with PPE, handheld gas detectors and entry permits. Anybotics values the global oil, gas and chemical sector at $102 billion and calls it the largest market for autonomous inspection, with costs driven by skilled labour and downtime from preventable failures. Each reading logs against its asset, giving reliability engineers a repeatable baseline and feeding digital twins.

The hard problem was ex-proofing a walking machine. Fixed equipment achieves compliance with heavy enclosures or by limiting electrical energy. A legged robot needs high-power motors and a large battery in a frame light enough to climb stairs. Anybotics sealed the AI computer and inspection equipment in nitrogen-filled chambers, insulated metallic components with non-metallic shells, added conductive feet for grounding and temperature cutoffs, and enclosed all twelve leg actuators and the battery in flameproof casings.

Charging and data transmission use energy barriers, so no connection is exposed to the hazardous area. Onboard software checks the robot's condition before, during and after each mission, and durability is tested against shock, vibration, corrosion, solar radiation and salt mist. The robot runs on Anybotics' Shift platform, which links findings to SAP, Yokogawa and SLB systems under ISO 27001.
