ArcelorMittal, Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, BHP and Mitsubishi Development have completed phase 1 of a carbon capture trial at the Gent integrated steelworks in Belgium. MHI's Advanced KM CDR Process captured CO2 from blast furnace off-gas at pilot scale, at rates exceeding 90%, across more than 4,500 hours of operation. The off-gas carries dust, sulphur-containing compounds and other contaminants, which makes this a demanding service for the capture process.

Assessments based on the trial indicate that full-scale capture on pre-combustion blast furnace off-gas could reduce CO2 intensity at the Gent site by up to 24%. Blast furnace steelmaking accounts for around 70% of global production. A capture route proven on this contaminated gas stream therefore offers near-term abatement for the sector, and the operating data will inform engineering and deployment studies for a commercial-scale design.

Only the CO2 was captured in phase 1. The remaining carbon, held as carbon monoxide, is still burned for power and heat elsewhere on site, releasing CO2 on combustion. Phase 2 will test capture on flue gas from the hot strip mill reheating furnace, which fires a blend of coke oven gas, blast furnace off-gas, basic oxygen furnace gas and natural gas, and presents a more dynamic flue-gas profile.
