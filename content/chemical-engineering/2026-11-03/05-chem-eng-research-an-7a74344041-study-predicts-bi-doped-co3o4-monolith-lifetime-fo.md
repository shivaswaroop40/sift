A paper in Chemical Engineering Research and Design develops a kinetic model for bismuth-doped cobalt oxide, Co3O4, catalysts that decompose nitrous oxide. The model is then applied at laboratory scale to predict how long a monolith catalyst lasts when the exhaust stream contains SO2. The stated scope runs from intrinsic kinetics to a monolith-level lifetime estimate under sulphur-bearing conditions.

Catalyst life in sulphur-containing exhaust sets replacement intervals, monolith sizing and the economics of N2O abatement. A model that links laboratory kinetics to a lifetime prediction gives designers a route from bench rate data to expected service life. It also offers a basis for judging whether Bi-doped Co3O4 would survive a given duty before long-duration trials are run.
