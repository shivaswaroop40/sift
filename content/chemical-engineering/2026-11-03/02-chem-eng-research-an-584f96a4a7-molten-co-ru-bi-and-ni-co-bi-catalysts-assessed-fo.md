A paper in Chemical Engineering Research and Design studies hydrogen production by methane pyrolysis in a bubble column reactor. Two molten catalysts are examined. One is a Co-Ru-Bi melt of cobalt, ruthenium and bismuth. The other is a Ni-Co-Bi melt of nickel, cobalt and bismuth. The work covers catalytic performance and a technoeconomic assessment.

Methane pyrolysis splits methane into hydrogen and solid carbon rather than CO2. Bubble columns holding a molten catalyst are one way to run it continuously. A study that compares two bismuth-containing melts and then carries that comparison into process economics gives a practitioner the activity picture and the cost picture together.

Only the article title is available here, so no conversion, yield or cost figures can be quoted. The stated contribution is the pairing of catalytic testing with technoeconomic assessment for these two melts in one reactor type. The numbers sit in the full text.
