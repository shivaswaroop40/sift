A paper in Chemical Engineering Journal examines defective UiO-66 monoliths as catalysts for the transfer hydrogenation of furfural in a micropacked bed reactor. Its focus is cooperativity between Lewis and Brønsted acid sites in the defective framework. Only the article title was available for this item, so conversion, selectivity and stability figures are not given here.

The catalyst form matters as much as the chemistry. Casting UiO-66 as a monolith rather than a packed powder typically eases pressure drop and attrition in continuous flow, and the micropacked bed suits intensified small-scale operation. Transfer hydrogenation swaps hydrogen gas for a donor molecule, which simplifies supply. The paper ties acid-site design, catalyst shaping and reactor choice into one system.
