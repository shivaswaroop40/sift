Chemical Engineering Research and Design has published a techno-economic and environmental assessment of propylene/propane separation in two-stage membrane cascades built on ZIF-67, a zeolitic imidazolate framework. The study treats the olefin/paraffin split as a cascade problem and evaluates it on both cost and environmental grounds.

Propylene/propane splitting is a major energy consumer in petrochemical plants and is usually handled by distillation. Membrane cascades are a recurring candidate to cut that duty. A two-stage design with ZIF-67 is the kind of option engineers must judge on cost and emissions, not selectivity alone. That is the comparison this paper sets out to make.

The source text carries only the title, with no abstract or figures attached. No selectivity values, stage cuts, membrane areas, cost estimates, or emissions results can be confirmed from it. Read this item as notice of the paper's scope and method, not as a report of what the assessment found.
