ENEOS has built a large-scale demonstration plant in Queensland to produce methylcyclohexane, a hydrogen carrier, using its Direct MCH electrochemical synthesis method. The plant opened on 6 October 2026 and will begin demonstration operations in January 2027. It uses an electrolyzer about eight times larger than the 2023 pilot unit and is designed for 9 tonnes of MCH per day.

Methylcyclohexane is a liquid organic hydride that can carry hydrogen across long distances and be stored at ambient conditions, lowering transport costs for green hydrogen supply chains. Direct MCH synthesis in a single electrochemical step reduces complexity and cost compared to multi-step synthesis routes.

The two-year demonstration will measure electrolyzer performance, degradation, and operational characteristics needed for commercialization. ENEOS chose Australia for its abundant renewable energy and to identify technical challenges early. The 1.2-MW electrolyzer class represents the minimum size for commercial deployment.
