Nobian has received final environmental permits from the Province of Groningen for an electric caustic soda evaporation unit at its Delfzijl chlorine plant. Adven will develop, finance, build and operate it, with ownership passing to Nobian later. Start-up is expected in 2028. Nobian says it will cut energy use for this step by about 85% and avoid up to 25,000 t/y of CO₂.

The unit uses mechanical vapour recompression. Vapour from the evaporation is captured, compressed and reused as the heat source, so the process recycles its own heat. This reduces reliance on natural gas for steam. Nobian says it will be the first European use of MVR for caustic soda concentration at this scale.

Caustic concentration is a large heat load in chlor-alkali plants, so replacing steam with a heat pump is a direct route to electrification. Corrosion is the main technical risk, as the article notes that hot caustic is highly corrosive. The source gives no detail on materials, compressor choice or electricity demand.

The project is one of the initiatives under Nobian's agreement with the Dutch government. It supports the company's aim of near-zero CO₂ emissions by 2030, ahead of its earlier 2040 target. The 85% and 25,000 t/y figures are expected values, not operating data.
