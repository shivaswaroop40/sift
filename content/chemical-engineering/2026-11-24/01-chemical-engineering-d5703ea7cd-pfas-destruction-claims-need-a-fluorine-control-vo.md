A Chemical Engineering article argues that a parent PFAS vanishing from one analytical stream does not show its fluorine left the process. The compound may have adsorbed, stuck to equipment, moved to a residual, transformed into another organofluorine species, volatilised, or released fluoride without full destruction. The author proposes mapping inputs, outputs and equipment holdup before testing.

The article sets out a claim ladder. Removal means a defined concentration or mass fell in a defined stream. Transformation means the parent fell while other organofluorine products rose. Partial defluorination means fluoride rose but fluorine-bearing outputs remain unresolved. Destruction needs a materially complete fluorine balance and explicit treatment of products of incomplete destruction.

Results should be put on a fluorine basis. PFOA is about 0.688 g fluorine per gram, so 1.00 mg of PFOA holds roughly 0.688 mg of fluorine. Closure is measured fluorine in all output compartments over measured fluorine input. Compartments include solids, rinses, filters, condensate, impingers and exhaust. Omissions need a documented pre-test rationale.

Methods are matched to questions. EPA 1633A covers 40 targeted PFAS by LC-MS/MS. EPA 1621 gives adsorbable organic fluorine as a method-defined screen. Fluoride, high-resolution MS and fluorine-19 NMR each cover other gaps. OTM-45, OTM-50 and Methods 26/26A are reference frameworks, not ready bench-scale methods. The source text is truncated.
