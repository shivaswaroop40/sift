Researchers identified a dual corrosion mechanism in insulated subsea oil pipelines where oxygen concentration cells and thermogalvanic effects act together under temperature gradients. The study examined how thermal insulation creates internal temperature differences that accelerate localised corrosion at the pipe steel surface.

For pipeline operators, this mechanism explains unexpected corrosion failures in supposedly protected subsea systems. Understanding the coupled process matters for design of insulation schemes and cathodic protection strategies in deepwater fields where temperature control is difficult to maintain uniformly.
