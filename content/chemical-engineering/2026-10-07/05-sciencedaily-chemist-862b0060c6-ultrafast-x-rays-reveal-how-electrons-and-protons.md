Researchers at Pacific Northwest National Laboratory used ultrafast X-ray spectroscopy to observe proton-coupled electron transfer, a reaction central to photosynthesis and biological energy conversion. They tracked how electrons and protons move in coordinated fashion while surrounding water molecules reorganize simultaneously, capturing for the first time both electronic changes within a molecule and structural shifts in its environment.

This matters because coordinated proton and electron motion avoids intermediate steps, making reactions faster and more efficient than sequential transfer. Understanding this mechanism at the molecular level could guide design of better catalysts, fuel cells, and flow batteries for energy storage and conversion applications.

The team studied a ruthenium-based molecule that absorbs light and captures protons, using combined X-ray absorption spectroscopy and X-ray scattering alongside quantum chemistry calculations. Previous experiments could observe either electronic changes or solvent reorganization separately, but not both simultaneously.
