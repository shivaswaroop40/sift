Scaling water electrolysis from lab to plant requires moving beyond optimizing single parameters toward balancing competing demands across the full system. The paper argues that technology development must start from actual operating conditions rather than isolated performance targets.

Chemical engineers designing electrolysers need to understand how improvements in one area—efficiency, current density, or durability—affect capital cost, reliability, and maintenance in practice. Plant-relevant validation early in development prevents costly redesigns at scale.

Real operating demands should drive design choices from the outset. This systems perspective ensures that technologies proven in the laboratory remain practical and economical when deployed.
