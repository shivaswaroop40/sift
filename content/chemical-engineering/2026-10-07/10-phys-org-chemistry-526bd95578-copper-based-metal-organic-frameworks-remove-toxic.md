Researchers have demonstrated a copper-based metal-organic framework (MOF) that captures both toxic metals and rare earth elements from wastewater in a single process. One gram of the MOF adsorbed 490 mg/g of lead, 264 mg/g of cadmium, 226 mg/g of manganese, and 335-351 mg/g of rare earth elements including neodymium and yttrium.

For process engineers, MOFs offer a selective alternative to precipitation and coagulation. They work across broad pH ranges, avoid generating secondary sludge, and can be regenerated and reused. The approach removes 90-99% of contaminants without the large infrastructure and disposal costs of conventional methods.

The team tested their protocol on real wastewater: alkaline, turbid streams with high dissolved solids, artificial seawater, and e-waste samples. They stress that large-scale fabrication, cost analysis, and lifecycle assessment remain open questions before deployment.
