Researchers compared in-situ CO2 capture with conventional bulk pH-swing electrolysis. In-situ capture integrated carbon removal directly into the electrolyzer cell, while bulk systems operated at fixed pH. The in-situ approach showed superior performance metrics across the test conditions.

For process design, this matters because electrochemical CO2 utilisation efficiency improves when capture happens at the reaction site rather than in separate stages. Reduced equipment footprint and fewer process streams simplify operations and lower capital costs in industrial deployment.

The bulk pH-swing method adjusts electrolyte pH globally to drive CO2 evolution, introducing thermodynamic and mixing losses. In-situ capture avoids this penalty by working at local conditions where the CO2 product naturally separates.
