Researchers at the Indian Institute of Science used quantum mechanics and machine learning to map 9,389 elementary reactions on copper catalysts for CO2 hydrogenation. A conventional model with 152 reactions predicted formic acid as the major product; the expanded network correctly identified methanol and carbon monoxide, with a 40-fold increase in predicted CO2 conversion.

For process engineers, this matters because reaction networks are usually reduced to tractable size by omitting steps assumed to be unimportant. The expanded model revealed that this culling had masked critical pathways, leading to quantitatively and qualitatively wrong predictions of product selectivity and conversion.

The work also found that hydrogen can transfer to intermediates as intact H2 molecules rather than dissociated atoms, especially for oxygen-containing species. This mechanism was invisible in smaller networks but confirmed by explicit quantum-mechanical calculation.
