LNG Canada is proceeding with Phase 2 expansion, which will increase liquefied natural gas export capacity from 14 million to 28 million tonnes per year. A consortium of global energy companies is leading the project.

The expansion matters to process engineers because it represents significant growth in liquefaction infrastructure and export operations. Doubling capacity requires decisions on plant layout, utility integration, and debottlenecking of existing Phase 1 assets.

Whether Phase 2 shares the same site or expands elsewhere, and how feedgas availability and downstream storage are managed, will shape the engineering scope.
