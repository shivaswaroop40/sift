Voestalpine and Verbund are investing 16.4 million euros to expand their H2future pilot plant in Linz with a compression unit, purification equipment, and five storage tanks. The facility, operational since 2019, currently produces 1,200 cubic metres of green hydrogen per hour from a six-megawatt PEM electrolysis plant.

The expansion shifts focus from hydrogen production alone to its treatment, storage, and delivery for industrial use. Four tanks will buffer supply fluctuations; a fifth tank will feed the Hy4smelt demonstration plant via a one-kilometre pipeline from late 2027. Hydrogen can be compressed to 500 bar and transported by trailer to external customers.

This capability matters for steel decarbonisation. Voestalpine plans electric arc furnaces at two sites by mid-2027 and aims to cut CO2 emissions by 30 percent by 2029, with hydrogen playing a key role in long-term net-zero targets. Research results are expected by end-2026; the project concludes December 2030.
