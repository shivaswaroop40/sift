A strontium-cation-guarded chabazite zeolite separates deuterium from hydrogen isotope mixtures by quantum sieving. Strontium cations sit at the 8-membered ring windows of the CHA framework. They speed preferential D2 diffusion and enrichment during adsorption, while allowing rapid H2 release during desorption. At 40 K, the optimal adsorbent reaches a dynamic D2/H2 selectivity of 3.10 and a D2 capacity of 6.60 mmol/g.

D2 and H2 have nearly identical physicochemical properties, which makes high-purity deuterium recovery difficult. Deuterium is indispensable for nuclear fusion and isotopic labeling. The authors attribute the performance to a decoupling of adsorption and desorption dynamics. That principle, favourable kinetics combined with tailored equilibrium adsorption, could guide other separations of species that differ mainly in diffusion behaviour.

Breakthrough tests followed by two-stage desorption give the process numbers. Direct purging recovers 52.7% of the D2 at 73.3% purity. Subsequent heating recovers a further 47.3% at 98.9% purity. Operation is at 40 K, so any plant application would need cryogenic service.
