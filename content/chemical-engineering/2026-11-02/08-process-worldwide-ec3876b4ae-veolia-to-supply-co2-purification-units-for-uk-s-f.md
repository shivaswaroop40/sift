Veolia has won a contract from Kanadevia Inova, the engineering, procurement and construction contractor, to design and supply two CO₂ purification and dehydration units for the Protos carbon capture plant. The plant sits at Encyclis' energy-from-waste facility near Ellesmere Port, Cheshire. It will process up to 500,000 tonnes of non-recyclable waste each year, generate up to 49.9 MW of baseload electricity, and capture around 370,000 tonnes of CO₂ annually. Operations are due to start in mid-2029.

The units were designed as compact, modular packages to fit within the constrained space of the Protos energy recovery facility building envelope. They must bring the captured CO₂ to the purity required for pipeline transport and long-term geological storage. The CO₂ will move through the HyNet North West pipeline network to depleted gas reservoirs beneath Liverpool Bay. Once running, the facility will be a negative emitter.

The project is the UK's first full-scale carbon capture facility integrated with an energy-from-waste plant, and forms part of the HyNet North West decarbonisation cluster. For Veolia it marks entry into the full-scale carbon capture market, with the company citing active capture projects across Europe and the US.
