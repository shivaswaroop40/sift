A paper in Chemical Engineering Science reports a nickel phosphide catalyst decorated with cerium hydroxide, Ce(OH)3, for alkaline seawater electrolysis. The decoration is claimed to act at the catalyst surface in two ways. It promotes adsorption of hydroxide ions, and it repels chloride ions. The authors frame this pairing as the basis for durable operation on seawater feed.

The appeal for electrolyser work is direct. Chloride in seawater corrodes anode catalysts, and its oxidation competes with the oxygen evolution reaction. A surface that favours hydroxide and excludes chloride would address selectivity and lifetime at once. That is relevant to any scheme that wants to electrolyse seawater without full desalination upstream.

Only the paper's title is available in this digest. No overpotentials, current densities, stability durations, or synthesis details are given. The claims of promoted hydroxide adsorption and chloride repulsion therefore rest on the authors' framing alone and cannot be checked from this text.
