A paper in Chemical Engineering Research and Design addresses slot-die coating of lithium-ion battery electrodes. It presents a three-dimensional correction of the lower boundary of the coating window, the minimum condition at which a stable wet film can still be formed. It also analyses coating defects caused by die geometry. The two parts together link process limits and die design.

Slot-die coating is the route used for lithium-ion electrode manufacture, and the coating window sets the operable range of speed and wet thickness. The lower boundary marks where the film can no longer be maintained. A treatment that accounts for three-dimensional effects, and for die geometry as a defect source, bears directly on line set-up and die design.
