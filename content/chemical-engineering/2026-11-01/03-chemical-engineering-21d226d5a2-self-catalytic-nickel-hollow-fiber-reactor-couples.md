Chemical Engineering Journal has published a paper on reaction-separation coupling in a self-catalytic nickel hollow-fiber membrane reactor for ethanol oxidative steam reforming. In the concept, the nickel hollow fiber is itself catalytic, so a single element carries out both the reaction and the separation. The application is ethanol oxidative steam reforming, a route that runs steam reforming and oxidation together in one step.

Membrane reactors appeal to process designers because they merge operations that normally sit in separate vessels, and hollow-fiber geometries give high membrane area per unit volume. Nickel also costs far less than the noble metals often used in catalytic membranes. Ethanol oxidative steam reforming is a hydrogen production route in which oxidation supplies heat for the endothermic reforming, so thermal balance is a central design problem.

Only the paper's title was available when this item was written. No conversion, selectivity, flux or operating-condition figures can be reported, and the title does not say what the membrane separates. Treat the description above as the declared scope of the work rather than its results.
