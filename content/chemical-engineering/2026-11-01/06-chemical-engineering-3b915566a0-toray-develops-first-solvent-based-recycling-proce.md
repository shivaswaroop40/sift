Toray Industries has developed what it calls the industry's first solvent-based recycling process for recovering polyphenylene sulfide (PPS) from automotive waste. A proprietary solvent dissolves the PPS-containing waste. Insoluble material, including glass fibres and metal contaminants, is removed by solid-liquid separation. The PPS is then recovered by crystallisation, still in polymer form. Demand is driven by end-of-life vehicle rules such as those recently introduced in the EU.

The process tackles both standard problems. Mechanical recycling of PPS degrades the polymer and cannot remove glass fibres or metals. Chemical recycling needs depolymerization and repolymerization, which costs energy. Toray's route keeps the PPS in polymer form and is expected to save substantial energy as a result. Recovered PPS shows molecular weight similar to virgin material, with comparable melting and crystallisation temperatures. A preliminary lifecycle analysis suggests lower cradle-to-gate greenhouse gas emissions than virgin production.

The process is not yet commercial. Toray is verifying continuous operation and evaluating further application-relevant properties of the recovered polymer. Remaining challenges are securing waste feedstock, demonstrating continuous operation at larger scale and establishing customer acceptance. Commercial supply is targeted for 2030, and reuse of the recovered glass fibres is future work.
