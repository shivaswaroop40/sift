A paper in Chemical Engineering Journal examines a 130 kW PEM fuel cell stack operated with nitrogen-diluted hydrogen. It studies how the dilution and the inlet stoichiometry affect stack performance and the consistency of voltages across individual cells. This digest has only the paper's title, so the specific results, dilution levels and operating conditions cannot be reported here.

Cell-voltage consistency matters in operation. A wide spread in cell voltages points to uneven conditions within the stack and can drive local degradation. Hydrogen dilution and inlet stoichiometry are both variables that an operator or system designer controls, so a systematic account of their effects at 130 kW scale speaks directly to fuel cell system design and day-to-day operation.
