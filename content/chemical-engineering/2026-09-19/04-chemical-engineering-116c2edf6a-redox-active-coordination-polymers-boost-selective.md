Researchers report redox-active coordination polymers that remove Sr²⁺ from water by capacitive deionization, addressing the usual trade-off between ion uptake capacity and selectivity.

Conventional capacitive deionization electrodes tend to favour competing ions such as Na⁺ and Ca²⁺ over Sr²⁺, limiting use in nuclear wastewater and contaminated groundwater treatment.

The coordination polymers use faradaic redox reactions to bind Sr²⁺ selectively while still delivering high removal capacity, a combination hard to achieve with purely electrical double-layer electrodes.

The work suggests a route to electrode materials that combine adsorption kinetics with electrochemical regeneration, relevant to selective radionuclide recovery and hardness ion control.
