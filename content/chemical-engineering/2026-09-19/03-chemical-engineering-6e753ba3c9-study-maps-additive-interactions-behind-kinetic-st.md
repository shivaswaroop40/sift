Researchers report that electrolyte additives in aqueous zinc batteries interact in process-dependent ways, producing a trade-off between reaction kinetics and long-term stability. The work points to the need to match additive combinations with the specific electrode process they are meant to serve.

The result matters for designers of grid-scale storage, where aqueous zinc systems are attractive for their safety and low cost but are limited by zinc dendrite growth, hydrogen evolution and poor cycling life. Additive cocktails are the usual fix, yet their interactions had not been systematically linked to particular electrochemical processes.

The authors frame the finding as a kinetic–stability trade-off: an additive that speeds plating or stripping often undermines interfacial stability, and vice versa. Engineering choices therefore need to target one performance axis and accept the consequence on the other, rather than chasing both with a single additive package.
