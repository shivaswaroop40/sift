A Perspective in Nature Chemical Engineering argues that membrane-based carbon dioxide separations are expanding into a wider range of applications, and that new materials and separation mechanisms need evaluation methods beyond the traditional permeability-selectivity trade-off.

The authors recommend incorporating pressure-dependent saturation effects, thickness-limited permeance, and application-specific requirements to guide material selection and design for next-generation CO2 separation membranes.

As CO2 capture requirements grow more varied, from post-combustion flue gas to direct air capture, the established trade-off curve may not capture how a membrane performs under realistic operating pressures and thin-film conditions.
