A triad kinetics framework has been developed to describe copolymer sequence changes during the hydrodechlorination of poly(vinyl chloride). It follows how chlorine atoms are removed across monomer triads rather than treating the chain as uniform, capturing sequence-dependent reactivity.

For chemical engineers, this matters because hydrodechlorination is a route to upcycle PVC waste into shorter, more tractable olefinic polymers and chemical feedstocks. Predicting how the chain rearranges, not just how much chlorine leaves, is needed to design reactors and control product quality.

The model treats sequence modification as a kinetic process, so it can be coupled to reactor models to estimate composition drift along a plug flow or batch system. The trade-off is that triad kinetics add parameter estimation effort compared with lumped conversion models.
