INEOS Energy and partners Harbour Energy and Nordsøfonden have begun commercial operation of the Greensand CO2 storage facility at Esbjerg, Denmark, the EU's first full-scale permanent storage site. CO2 from Danish biomethane plants is liquefied, trucked to a terminal, shipped on the purpose-built carrier Carbon Destroyer 1, and injected into the Nini West depleted oil field 250 km offshore at 1,800 m depth.

The first phase can store up to 400,000 tonnes of CO2 per year, with design potential to scale to 4-8 million tonnes annually. The site is part of the EU's stated targets of 50 million tonnes per year by 2030 and 250-280 million tonnes by 2040, a level the bloc remains far from reaching.

For practising engineers, Greensand demonstrates a working CCS value chain that links capture, transport, and offshore injection, and it is positioned to accept CO2 from industrial emitters across Europe as capacity expands. The model offers a route for hard-to-abate sectors such as cement, steel, and chemicals to cut emissions without closing plants.

The opening is framed by INEOS and the European Commissioner for Energy as a move from demonstration to deployment, but the scale of the first phase is small relative to EU targets, and the announcement contains no cost or capture-rate data.
