A study in Chemical Engineering Science examines how interfacial polarisation affects Ni/SiC catalysts during microwave-assisted dry reforming of methane. The work looks at how the metal-support interface responds to electromagnetic heating compared with conventional methods.

Dry reforming of methane converts CO2 and CH4 into syngas, and microwave heating could improve energy efficiency and selectivity if the catalyst can couple effectively with the field. The findings matter for engineers designing microwave-driven reforming reactors at pilot or industrial scale.

The paper identifies how polarisation at the Ni-SiC interface influences catalytic performance under microwave irradiation. Details on conversion rates and selectivity are not given in the available abstract, so the practical scale of improvement is unclear.
