Researchers report a hierarchical ZSM-5 zeolite-supported Pd–Co bimetallic catalyst for selective phenol hydrogenation to cyclohexanone, achieving 93.1% yield with a turnover frequency of 268.9 h⁻¹ at a phenol-to-Pd molar ratio of 1132.

The design relies on two complementary functions. Pd–Co alloy nanoparticles enhance H₂ adsorption and activation, accelerating phenol conversion, while Co–O–Si bonds at zeolite defect sites tilt phenol adsorption and suppress over-hydrogenation of cyclohexanone to cyclohexanol.

The work targets a longstanding trade-off in this route, where sluggish kinetics and poor selectivity have limited industrial use of phenol hydrogenation. The dual-function approach offers a design principle for phenolic hydrogenation catalysts beyond this specific system.

Only abstract-level claims are reported; long-term stability, regeneration behaviour, and performance at industrially relevant pressures or with real feedstocks are not addressed in the available text.
