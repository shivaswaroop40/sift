Researchers used in situ Raman microscopy to observe how supercritical or pressurised CO2 dissolves into alkane–aromatic mixtures and extracts aromatic components, imaging the process at the microscale rather than relying on bulk measurements.

For separation design, the work offers direct evidence of where mass transfer occurs in CO2-based extraction, which is relevant to aromatics recovery, supercritical fluid processing and carbon capture solvent screening.

The source provided is only the paper title; details on operating conditions, composition ranges, quantitative dissolution rates or extraction efficiencies are not available, so specific numbers cannot be reported.

The method itself, combining Raman mapping with a pressurised CO2 feed, could be useful for engineers validating mass transfer models or screening solvents without building full extraction rigs.
