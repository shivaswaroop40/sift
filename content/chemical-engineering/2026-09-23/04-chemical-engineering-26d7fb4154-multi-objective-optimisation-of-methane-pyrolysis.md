This study applies multi-objective optimisation to turquoise hydrogen production via methane pyrolysis in liquid metal bubble reactors, balancing conversion and selectivity against energy and economic factors.

For process engineers, the work offers a structured framework for tuning liquid metal bubble reactor operating conditions to maximise hydrogen yield while managing energy input and cost trade-offs relevant to low-emission hydrogen routes.

The paper treats methane pyrolysis as a carbon-neutral pathway when the solid carbon product is sequestered, positioning liquid metal bubble reactors as a means to separate hydrogen from carbon without CO2 emissions.
