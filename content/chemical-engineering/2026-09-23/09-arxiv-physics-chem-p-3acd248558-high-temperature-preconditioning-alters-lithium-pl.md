Researchers cycled commercial LG Chem 18650 HG2 lithium-ion cells at minus 20 °C, comparing three pretreatment histories: no preconditioning, two weeks of galvanostatic cycling at 30 °C, and storage at 30 °C. They observed distinct differences in lithium metal deposition on the anode between the three cases during sub-zero operation.

The study combines X-ray computed tomography of the electrode microstructure with three-dimensional, spatially resolved electrochemical modelling. This pairing aims to link plating morphology to local current density and transport effects inside the porous electrode at low temperature.

For practitioners, the work suggests that warm pre-conditioning protocols can change plating behaviour when cells are subsequently charged below 0 °C, which has direct implications for cold-soak start-up procedures in spacecraft and other fielded battery systems.

The source is an abstract; quantitative results on plating extent, capacity loss and the relative ranking of the three protocols are not reported here.
