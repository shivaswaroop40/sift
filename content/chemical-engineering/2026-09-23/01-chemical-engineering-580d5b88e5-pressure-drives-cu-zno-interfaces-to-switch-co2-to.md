Researchers report that raising pressure steers CO2 hydrogenation to methanol over Cu–ZnOx catalysts through a formate-mediated pathway rather than the reverse water-gas shift route.

Pressure stabilises Cu–ZnOx interfacial sites, raising methanol selectivity at the expense of CO formation, which is the usual competing product in CO2 reduction.

For process engineers, the result points to pressure as a practical handle to lift methanol yield when designing captured-carbon utilisation units, though reported rates and long-term stability data are not given in the abstract.
