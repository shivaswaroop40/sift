Researchers at ITQB NOVA characterised PsGalOx, the first galactose oxidase found in bacteria. Unlike fungal versions, it has two sugar-binding modules alongside its catalytic core. Using AlphaFold predictions and directed evolution, they developed variants up to 1800 times more efficient at processing sugars and alcohols.

Galactose oxidases perform alcohol oxidation under mild conditions using oxygen, avoiding harsh chemical oxidants and toxic waste. This matters for pharmaceutical synthesis, food ingredients, and flavour production, where greener routes reduce operational hazard and environmental burden.

The team found that the two nearly identical binding modules, despite 80 per cent sequence similarity, evolved distinct roles: one strengthens sugar binding, the other stabilises the protein. Directed evolution unexpectedly merged these modules into hybrids that increased protein solubility and expression.
