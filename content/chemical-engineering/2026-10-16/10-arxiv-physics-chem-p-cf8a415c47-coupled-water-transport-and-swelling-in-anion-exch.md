Researchers built a finite element model of water transport and mechanical deformation in Fumasep FAA-3-50 anion exchange membranes under water-activity gradients. The model couples concentration-dependent diffusivity, hygroscopic swelling, and viscoelastic polymer response. Mechanical feedback alters transport predictions, though diffusion remains dominant.

For CO2 separations using anion exchange membranes, understanding how water gradients cause uneven swelling and stress matters. Membranes operate under nonuniform hydration in practice. The coupled model predicts spatially and temporally varying deformation that simpler models miss.

The model validates against steady-state water-transport measurements and shows strongest agreement at intermediate hydration levels. This framework lets engineers evaluate membrane behaviour in moisture-facilitated transport applications and refine designs for continuous separation processes.
