Researchers studied anion exchange membrane electrolysis of alkaline saline water and identified how hypochlorite ions migrate and concentrate locally during the process. This transport mechanism drives hypochlorite formation at specific sites rather than as a uniform product.

For process design, this finding matters because local hypochlorite buildup affects selectivity, electrode performance, and product distribution. Understanding where and how hypochlorite evolves lets engineers optimise operating windows and membrane selection to improve yields or avoid unwanted side reactions.
