Researchers integrated four imaging and analysis techniques to map the full pore structure of an industrial oxidative coupling of methane catalyst, from nanometre to micrometre scale. They found a multimodal pore distribution with porosity and pore size both decreasing from particle centre to edge.

For process engineers designing and scaling OCM reactors, knowing the actual pore gradient matters because it changes how reactants diffuse through the catalyst. A single-particle CFD model built from these measurements predicted an effectiveness factor of 0.639, showing moderate internal diffusion limitation.

The result confirms that micro-CT imaging can quantify 3D pore structure in industrial catalysts and that neglecting the radial gradient would give wrong performance predictions in reactor models.
