Researchers used pore-scale imaging to track how asphaltenes evolve when crude oil contacts CO2 repeatedly in a confined space. Interfacial mass transfer and precipitation patterns changed with each cycle, revealing complex interactions between the gas and liquid phases.

For process design in enhanced oil recovery or CO2 storage operations, understanding asphaltene stability during cycling matters. Precipitation or mobilisation alters permeability, affects equipment fouling, and changes fluid properties in ways that single-contact assumptions miss.

The pore-scale view shows mechanisms that bulk experiments cannot capture. Practitioners designing cyclic injection schemes need this detail to predict flow behaviour and maintenance demands.
