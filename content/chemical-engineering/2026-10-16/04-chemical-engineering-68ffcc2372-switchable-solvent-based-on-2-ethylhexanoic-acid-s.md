Researchers mapped the chemical and phase equilibria for a four-component system combining 2-ethylhexanoic acid, its sodium salt, water, and sodium hydroxide. The system can switch between hydrophilic and hydrophobic states by adjusting pH through NaOH addition.

Switchable hydrophilicity solvents reduce solvent waste in separation processes by reversibly changing polarity without adding or removing liquid. This matters for extraction and recycling applications where multiple solvent swaps currently drive costs and environmental load.

The work establishes the equilibrium data needed to design processes around this acid-salt pair. Without such phase diagrams, engineers cannot predict composition changes or set optimal operating points.
