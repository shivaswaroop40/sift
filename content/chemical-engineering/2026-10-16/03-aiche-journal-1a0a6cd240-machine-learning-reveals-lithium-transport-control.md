Researchers used a physics-informed machine-learning model to understand how polyamide nanofiltration membranes separate lithium from magnesium in salt-lake brines. The model combined empirical membrane data with steric and Donnan partitioning factors from established pore theory.

For practitioners, this matters because lithium extraction from magnesium-rich brines is economically important, but membrane design has been hampered by conflicting principles. The study shows that lithium selectivity depends primarily on controlling lithium transport accessibility, not on maximising magnesium rejection alone.

The framework identified a narrow operating window: Donnan factors for lithium between 2 and 2.35, and steric factors below 0.08. Effective separation requires balancing lithium permeability against magnesium rejection rather than optimising either property independently.
