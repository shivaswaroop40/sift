A study in Nature Chemical Engineering argues that conventional battery safety management can misjudge risk. These approaches rely on fixed thresholds and capacity-based metrics. The authors report that cells showing similar capacity fade can sit in very different safety states.

The practical point is that capacity alone is a poor proxy for how safe an aged cell is. Two cells that look equally degraded by capacity could carry different levels of hazard. A threshold set once for a fresh cell may therefore be wrong later in life.

The authors use this to motivate adaptive, usage-aware safety approaches that change across battery lifetime. The title refers to mapping safety zones as cells degrade. The source text is a short abstract, so it gives no detail on the cell chemistries, ageing conditions, test methods or quantitative results.
