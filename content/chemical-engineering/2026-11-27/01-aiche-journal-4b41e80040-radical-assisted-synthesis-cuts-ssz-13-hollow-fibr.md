Researchers added sodium persulfate to the synthesis, generating hydroxyl radicals that speed SSZ-13 zeolite crystallisation. Synthesis time fell by 75% to 24 hours. On α-Al2O3 hollow fibre supports with 250 nm pores, a matching index of 0.82 gave the best CO2 permeance and CO2/CH4 selectivity.

The best membrane reached a CO2 permeance of 3.1 × 10^-7 mol m^-2 s^-1 Pa^-1, about 926 GPU, with a CO2/CH4 selectivity of about 160. The method was scaled to 40 cm long fibres, and the authors report good batch-to-batch reproducibility.

Process modelling for biomethane production indicates the zeolite membranes need more than 12 times less membrane area than commercial polymeric membranes. Long synthesis times and poor scalability have held back zeolite membranes, so a one-day route on hollow fibres addresses two of the main barriers.

The abstract gives no cost data, no mixed-gas results under realistic wet or contaminated feeds, and no long-term stability figures. The area saving comes from modelling, not from a pilot unit.
