This study applies an integrated machine-learning workflow to sulfur dioxide oxidation catalysis, using curated experimental records from multiple literature sources. Four preprocessing scenarios were compared to assess their effect on surrogate-model performance. Multi-objective optimisation then explored catalyst and operating-condition space against SO2 conversion, productivity, and catalyst cost, with a multi-criteria decision step ranking candidates.

The authors show that preprocessing choices can substantially shift surrogate-model behaviour and the resulting optimised regions, so transparent data treatment is critical when mixing heterogeneous literature datasets. For practising engineers, the work offers a practical template for combining ML, optimisation, and decision analysis when screening catalysts from scattered published data, alongside a caution that dataset curation drives the answer as much as the model does.

The study is framed as lessons from a real case rather than a benchmark, so reported gains reflect the workflow on this specific SO2 system rather than a general claim about ML in catalysis.
