An experimental campaign has characterised axial impellers by measuring power input, flow fields, shear rate distributions and overall mixing performance across multiple configurations. The work combines torque-based power measurements with detailed velocity and shear mapping to give a joined-up view of how geometry drives hydrodynamics. Shear rate distributions are reported alongside the flow field rather than as an isolated metric, allowing direct links between local hydrodynamics and bulk mixing outcomes.

For design and scale-up, the data set offers a reference point that links power number, flow pattern and shear distribution in one consistent study, which is useful when selecting an impeller for a given duty. Practitioners working on mixing-sensitive operations such as crystallisation, fermentation or liquid-liquid dispersion can use the relative shear and flow characterisation to judge whether a given geometry will meet their process needs without resorting to multiple separate correlations.
