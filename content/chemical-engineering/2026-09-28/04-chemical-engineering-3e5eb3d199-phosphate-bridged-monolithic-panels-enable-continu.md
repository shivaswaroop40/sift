Researchers have built phosphate-bridged monolithic photocatalytic panels that produce hydrogen peroxide under continuous flow rather than in batch reactors. The design binds the photocatalyst within a single solid structure, removing the need to suspend particles and separate them after reaction.

For chemical engineers, the relevance is the shift from slurry photoreactors, which suffer from light scattering, catalyst recovery and limited scalability, to a structured panel geometry that can be stacked and illuminated more uniformly. Continuous operation also changes the way productivity and energy yield are reported.

The paper claims this configuration supports sustained H2O2 photosynthesis with the catalyst held in place, though the source text does not give concentrations, rates or operating pressures. Readers should check the full article for the quantitative performance and stability data before judging it against existing photochemical processes.
