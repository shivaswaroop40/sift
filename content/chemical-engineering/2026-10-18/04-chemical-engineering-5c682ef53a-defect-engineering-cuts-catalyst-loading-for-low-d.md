Researchers modified BEA zeolites by introducing structural defects to enhance acid site density and strength. The defect-engineered catalyst achieved LDPE conversion at ultra-low catalyst loadings, reducing the amount of catalyst needed per unit of polymer.

This matters because high catalyst loading adds cost and complicates product separation in polyethylene recycling. Lower loadings improve process economics and simplify downstream recovery of both the catalyst and the upcycled products.

The mechanism involves increased Brønsted acidity from defect sites, which improves the catalytic activity per unit mass and allows the same conversion at much lower weight percentages of catalyst in the reaction mixture.
