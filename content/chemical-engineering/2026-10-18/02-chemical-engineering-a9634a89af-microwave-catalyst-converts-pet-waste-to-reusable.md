A Zn-Zr/ZnO catalyst under microwave heating converts polyethylene terephthalate waste to a plastic-like polymer with near-quantitative yield. The process uses microwave assistance rather than conventional heating.

For process engineers, this matters because PET recycling typically degrades material or gives low conversion. A near-total recovery route that produces usable polymer cuts waste and reduces reliance on virgin feedstock.

The catalyst system combines zinc and zirconium oxides, suggesting potential for tuning selectivity through metal oxide chemistry.
