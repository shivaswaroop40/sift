Nickel-based catalysts are improving oxygen evolution and hybrid anodic reactions in alkaline seawater electrolysis. The approach avoids freshwater use and enables direct hydrogen generation from seawater. Recent work focuses on catalyst design and electrolyzer system integration.

For process engineers, this matters because seawater is abundant and cheap, while freshwater electrolysers face resource constraints. Alkaline systems suit nickel catalysts well. Direct seawater splitting cuts desalination steps and capex, though salt tolerance and chloride handling remain engineering challenges.
