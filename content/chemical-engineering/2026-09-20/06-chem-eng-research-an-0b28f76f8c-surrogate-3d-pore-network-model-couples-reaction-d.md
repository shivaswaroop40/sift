The paper presents a surrogate three-dimensional pore-network model that simulates coupled intraparticle reaction, diffusion and heat transfer inside spherical porous catalyst particles. The authors replace the full pore structure with a reduced network that preserves transport behaviour while cutting computational cost.

The approach matters for reactor engineers screening catalysts or operating conditions where internal temperature and concentration gradients control selectivity, hotspot risk and overall rate. A faster surrogate enables parameter sweeps and coupling with reactor-scale models.

The paper reports validation against detailed three-dimensional simulations, with the surrogate reproducing key profiles and thermal run-away tendencies at a fraction of the CPU time. Concrete numbers are not given in the source text provided.

Only the abstract framing is available, so claims of speed-up and accuracy are taken at face value.
