Researchers converted cellulose-rich textile waste into aromatics via ZSM-5-catalysed hydropyrolysis, achieving aromatic yields of 41.0% to 56.0% with over 95% selectivity across four real feedstocks. The Aspen Plus-based life-cycle and techno-economic assessment gave a cradle-to-gate global warming potential of 0.73 kg CO2-eq per kg aromatics with grey hydrogen and 0.59 kg CO2-eq with green hydrogen, both well below the fossil benchmark.

The grey-hydrogen route produced a minimum selling price of US$0.68 per kg aromatics, competitive with current mixed-aromatic prices, while the carbon benefit of green hydrogen remained locked in the upstream supply. The authors frame hydrogen provenance as the lever for carbon footprint and hydrogen price as the lever for production cost.

For practitioners, the work points to low-cost, low-carbon hydrogen as the single decisive variable for scaling textile-waste-to-aromatics. The claim rests on a process model rather than a built and operated plant, so capital and operating risks remain unproven.

The study is one of the first to couple hydropyrolysis performance on real textile waste with full LCA and TEA, giving a concrete carbon-cost map for decision-makers.
