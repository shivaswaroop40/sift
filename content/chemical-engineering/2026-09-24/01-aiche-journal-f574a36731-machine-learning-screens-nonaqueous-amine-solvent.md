Researchers used a machine learning workflow trained on a small experimental dataset to screen nonaqueous absorbents for CO2 capture, linking molecular structure to desorption performance.

The top candidate, a blend of 2-(butylamino)ethanol (BEA) and tetramethylethylenediamine (TMEDA), gave a desorption capacity of 0.668 mol CO2 per mol amine and a maximum desorption rate of 0.0513 mol CO2 per mol amine per minute. Regeneration energy was 1.38 GJ per tonne CO2, 62.3% below aqueous monoethanolamine.

The solvent also showed good cyclic stability, low temperature operation, and low corrosivity, addressing common drawbacks of conventional aqueous alkanolamine systems. NMR and DFT analysis indicate BEA captures CO2 while TMEDA acts as a proton acceptor, aiding proton transfer and lowering the heat of reaction.

The approach combines data-driven solvent screening with mechanistic interpretation, offering a framework for designing energy efficient nonaqueous absorbents without exhaustive experimental campaigns.
