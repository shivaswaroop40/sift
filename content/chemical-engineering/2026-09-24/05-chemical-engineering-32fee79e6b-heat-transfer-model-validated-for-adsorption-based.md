The paper validates a one-dimensional heat transfer model for adsorption-based direct air capture (DAC) systems and applies it to a configuration with an embedded heat exchanger using a geometric heat transfer distribution approach.

Modelling heat transfer accurately matters because DAC adsorption beds cycle between adsorption and desorption, and the energy required for temperature swings is a major operating cost. A reliable model supports sizing of adsorbent, bed geometry and heat exchange hardware.

The work claims to extend a standard 1D framework with a geometric treatment of heat distribution, then demonstrates simulation of an embedded exchanger, though the abstract provides limited quantitative detail on accuracy, scale or conditions tested.

The claim is based on the abstract alone; full validation ranges, error metrics and cycle performance numbers are not stated here.
