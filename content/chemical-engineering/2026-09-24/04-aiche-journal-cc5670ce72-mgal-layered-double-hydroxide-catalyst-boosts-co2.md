A composite MgAl-layered double hydroxide on activated carbon (LDH/AC) has been reported as a solid base catalyst that accelerates CO2 absorption in N-methyldiethanolamine solution. At just 0.1 wt% loading with a 1:1 LDH to AC ratio, the catalyst raised the CO2 absorption rate by 256% compared with uncatalysed MDEA, and held that performance through 10 absorption cycles.

The result matters because tertiary amines such as MDEA are widely used in gas treating for their low regeneration energy, but their slow reaction kinetics have limited throughput and equipment size. A heterogeneous solid catalyst that works at very low loading, and that can be filtered out of solution, offers a route to higher capacity without the solvent degradation concerns that come with some homogeneous activators.

Online FT-IR data pointed to a base-catalysed mechanism involving the hydroxide surface and oxygen vacancies. DFT calculations indicated a 47% drop in the energy barrier of the rate-limiting step, giving molecular-scale support for the rate gain observed in the absorption experiments.

The work is laboratory-scale and the claims rest on a single solvent system. Long-term stability beyond 10 cycles, behaviour in the presence of flue gas impurities, and catalyst attrition in real contactors are not addressed in the abstract.
