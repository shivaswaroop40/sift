The paper presents an Eulerian CFD model for gas-liquid flow in foam-packed rotating beds, treating the foam as a porous medium. The authors compare their model results with experimental data from rotating bed studies.

For process engineers working on reactive separations or intensified contactors, this modelling approach offers a way to predict two-phase flow behaviour in rotating packed beds without full resolved-pore CFD. That can guide design and scale-up of foam-structured contactors used in gas-liquid operations.

The work also provides a framework for evaluating different foam morphologies as the porous structure in these rotating devices, linking pore-level characteristics to bed-scale flow predictions.
