Researchers prepared bimetallic GaPt catalysts on HZSM-5 zeolite for ethane dehydroaromatization. The optimal 0.5Ga0.8Pt/HZSM-5 achieved 52% initial ethane conversion, 5.4 mmol aromatics per gram catalyst per hour, and a deactivation rate of only 0.007 h⁻¹ over 10 hours. Coke resistance was markedly better than monometallic equivalents.

The result matters for natural gas upgrading, since ethane is the second-most abundant component but underutilised. Converting it directly to aromatics rather than methane-centric routes improves economics and selectivity. The low deactivation rate is critical for commercial viability in a reaction prone to rapid fouling.

Spectroscopic studies showed that the GaPt alloy works synergistically with the zeolite's acid sites. The metal phase enhances dehydrogenation and aromatization, while suppressing the coke formation that normally limits stability. The Ga/Pt ratio is key to this synergy.
