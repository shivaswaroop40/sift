Researchers at Seoul National University identified local pH variation near electrodes as a critical factor in electrochemical reactions. By controlling acidity in the immediate electrode environment, they increased selectivity for desired products in Baeyer-Villiger oxidation from 16% to 97%, producing lactones and epoxides without added peroxide oxidants.

For process engineers, this finding means overlooked microscopic conditions may limit performance across many electrochemical syntheses. The team surveyed over 600 papers and found 89% involved reactions where protons transfer, suggesting local pH effects are widespread. Near cathodes, pH can reach 11; near anodes, pH 2.5, even in nominally neutral solutions.

The work directly addresses electrochemistry and process design trade-offs: replacing hazardous external oxidants with oxygen and electrical energy while managing the chemical environment at reaction sites. Reactor geometry and liquid flow control these local conditions, making the approach practical for manufacturing plastics, medicines, and adhesives.
