Researchers modelled CO2 hydrogenation to aromatics over tandem In2O3-HZSM-5 catalysts in a fixed-bed reactor. Positioning In2O3 upstream in a layered configuration increased aromatic yield by 98 percent versus random packing, because methanol builds up at high concentration where it meets HZSM-5 downstream.

For process design, this shows catalyst placement order drives performance in cascade reactions. A 1:1 In2O3 to HZSM-5 ratio coordinates both steps. Diffusion limits matter: small pellets, large pores, and Raschig ring shapes cut CO2 consumption loss and suppress unwanted water-gas-shift.

The model is particle-resolved CFD, meaning it captures intraparticle transport alongside bulk flow. This level of detail reveals how local methanol concentration shapes selectivity.
