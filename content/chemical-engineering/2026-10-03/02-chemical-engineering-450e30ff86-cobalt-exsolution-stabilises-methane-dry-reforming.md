Researchers used cobalt-doped composite oxides where cobalt ions exsolve from the matrix to form dual active sites on the catalyst surface. The exsolved cobalt particles stabilise the catalyst during methane dry reforming, reducing carbon deposition and improving conversion of both methane and carbon dioxide.

Methane dry reforming converts both CH₄ and CO₂ into synthesis gas, but catalysts typically suffer from rapid deactivation through carbon laydown and sintering. A stable, selective catalyst is valuable for industrial syngas production and for utilising waste CO₂. Exsolution offers a route to anchor active metal sites within the matrix rather than as separate particles.
