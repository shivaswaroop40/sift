Researchers at National Taiwan University developed a fluorescence-based screening method to test thousands of variants of BsPPS, an enzyme that phosphorylates phenolic plant compounds. The assay uses umbelliferone as an indicator, which loses fluorescence when phosphorylated, allowing rapid measurement via a standard microplate reader instead of labour-intensive HPLC.

The method accelerates enzyme engineering for water-soluble derivatives of bioactive plant compounds, relevant to nutraceutical development and formulation. A heat-stable variant, G145D, was identified that retained more activity after thermal stress while maintaining catalytic performance.
