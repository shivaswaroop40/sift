Researchers at EPFL modified platinum co-catalysts on organic semiconductor nanoparticles by changing the halide ligand in the precursor. Chloroplatinate left Pt–Cl species that poisoned active sites. Bromo- and iodoplatinate precursors reduced more readily, with iodide modification reaching 17% apparent quantum yield at 700 nm.

For process engineers, this addresses a known bottleneck in photocatalytic hydrogen generation. Organic semiconductors are attractive because they use earth-abundant elements and work under visible light, but their low photopotential has limited performance. The co-catalyst surface chemistry was poorly understood until now.

Platinum photodeposition is essential but the resulting surface state directly controls hydrogen evolution rates. This work identifies halide selection as a critical design parameter that has been overlooked in optimising these systems.
