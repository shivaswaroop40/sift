Researchers grafted zwitterionic and fluorinated polymer brushes onto RO membranes using photoinitiated polymerization. The surface adapts to operating conditions through polymer segregation, creating alternating hydrophilic and low-energy domains. Water permeance reached 3.4 L m−2 h−1 bar−1 with 99% NaCl rejection.

Fouling is the primary limit on RO lifetime and throughput in desalination and wastewater treatment. The low-energy domains repel both organic and inorganic foulants, reducing adhesion and speeding their release. Flux recovery after fouling was 91% for oil emulsions and 99.4% for petrochemical wastewater, versus lower values for unmodified commercial membranes.
