Researchers at Karlsruhe Institute of Technology have developed millimeter-sized beads made entirely from enzymes held together by complementary molecular binding modules. The beads are created by mixing enzyme building blocks in liquid droplets, freezing them in liquid nitrogen, and then freeze-drying to produce mechanically robust, porous particles that can be stored and rehydrated for use.

For process engineers, this offers a way to deploy biocatalysts continuously in chemical production without inert support materials, improving reaction efficiency. The method is modular: different enzymes can be assembled into customized networks for specific reactions, and the beads remain stable during storage and long continuous-flow operation.

The team also demonstrated hybrid beads combining enzymes with living Escherichia coli cells, which can provide chemical energy or intermediates. These hybrid beads retained catalytic activity after four weeks of dry storage at room temperature and viable cells could be recovered after five months, retaining genetic function.
