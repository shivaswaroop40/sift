Researchers built a one-dimensional model of a direct reduced iron shaft furnace capable of predicting performance when fed with syngas, hydrogen, or ammonia instead of traditional reducing gases. The model tracks temperature, composition, and reduction progression down the furnace shaft.

For steelmaking under carbon constraints, this work matters because it quantifies how different reducing agents perform in existing DRI hardware. Switching fuels changes reaction kinetics and heat transfer, so accurate prediction guides process redesign and feedstock choice.

The model integrates reduction chemistry with thermal behaviour in a single column simulation. This lets operators evaluate low-carbon pathways before capital investment in alternative DRI routes.
