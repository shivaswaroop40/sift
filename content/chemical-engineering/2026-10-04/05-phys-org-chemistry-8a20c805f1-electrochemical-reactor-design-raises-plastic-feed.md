Researchers at Seoul National University developed an electrochemical reactor that synthesizes lactones and epoxides—key feedstocks for plastics, adhesives and coatings—using oxygen and electricity. By controlling electrode spacing and electrolyte flow to manage local pH near the electrodes, they raised selectivity for the target product from approximately 16% to 97% without adding peroxide oxidants.

Local pH near electrodes can differ drastically from bulk solution pH, reaching pH 11 at the cathode and pH 2.5 at the anode even when the bulk is neutral. This extreme microenvironment alters reactive oxygen species supply, starting material chemistry, and product degradation rates. Previous work focused on bulk conditions and overlooked this effect.

Analysis of over 600 electrochemical synthesis papers since 2010 found that 89% involved proton transfer alongside electron transfer at the electrodes, suggesting local pH control may benefit a broad range of organic electrosynthesis reactions, not just specialised cases.
