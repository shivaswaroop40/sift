Cyclic regeneration of catalysts during polyethylene pyrolysis maintained product selectivity over repeated runs. The work tested different contact modes between feedstock and catalyst, varied catalyst loading, and tracked deactivation patterns through regeneration cycles.

For process engineers scaling up waste plastics conversion, understanding deactivation mechanisms and regeneration efficacy is critical to economic viability. Catalyst life and selectivity directly affect yields of target products and operating costs.

The study examined how contact mode and loading influence both initial performance and how catalyst responds to thermal regeneration, providing data for reactor design and catalyst cycling strategies.
