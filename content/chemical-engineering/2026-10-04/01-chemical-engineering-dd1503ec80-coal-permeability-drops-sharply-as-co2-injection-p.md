Large-scale experiments on coal cores show permeability declines by up to 80% during CO2-enhanced coalbed methane recovery. The loss occurs as CO2 adsorbs onto the coal matrix, causing swelling that blocks pore pathways. This effect varies across the seam, creating zones of high and low injectivity.

For process design, this deterioration means CO2 injectivity will decline over time in any producing field. Engineers must account for spatial variation in permeability loss when planning injection patterns and predicting long-term recovery rates. The modelling approaches used here help predict where blockage will be most severe.
