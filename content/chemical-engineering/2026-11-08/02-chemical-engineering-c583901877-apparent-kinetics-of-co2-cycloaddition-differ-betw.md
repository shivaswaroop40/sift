A study in Chemical Engineering Science examines CO2 cycloaddition over supported imidazole-based ionic liquid catalysts in two reactor configurations. It compares stirred-slurry operation with trickle-bed operation. The reported finding is that the apparent kinetics are reactor-dependent, so the two configurations do not yield the same apparent rate behaviour for the same catalyst.

This matters wherever laboratory kinetic data feed reactor design. Slurry and trickle-bed reactors impose different contacting between gas, liquid and catalyst. If apparent kinetics shift between them, a rate expression fitted in a stirred slurry may not transfer directly to trickle-bed scale-up, or the reverse. The paper addresses that transfer problem for supported ionic liquid catalysts in CO2 conversion.

Only the article title is available in this source. No rate constants, conversions or proposed mechanisms are given, so the quantitative basis for the reactor dependence cannot be reported here. Treat the claim as directional until the full paper is read.
