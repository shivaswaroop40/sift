A paper in Chemical Engineering Research and Design asks whether the pores of ultrafiltration membranes effectively capture per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances, PFAS. Only the title is available in the source text. There is no abstract, so the findings, the membranes tested and the compounds covered cannot be reported here.

The question is practical rather than academic. Ultrafiltration is already widespread in water and process trains, and PFAS discharge limits keep tightening. If pores retain these compounds, an existing ultrafiltration step may earn some removal credit. If they do not, a tighter membrane or an adsorption step is needed. The answer also bears on how rejection changes as membranes foul and are cleaned.
