Researchers modelled how microwaves create localised hot-spots in CO2-loaded monoethanolamine through dielectric swing effects, then validated the mechanism experimentally. Techno-economic simulation showed microwave-assisted desorption reduces process energy consumption by 31.5 per cent compared to conventional heating.

This matters to capture plant operators and designers because lower regeneration energy directly cuts operating costs and carbon footprint. The study quantifies what has been suspected: selective microwave heating is more efficient than bulk heating of the solvent.

The work moves from mechanism understanding to cost impact: CO2 capture cost fell from $60.8 to $42.8 per tonne. However, the analysis is model-based and validated only for MEA, leaving questions about scalability and applicability to other solvents or solid sorbents.
