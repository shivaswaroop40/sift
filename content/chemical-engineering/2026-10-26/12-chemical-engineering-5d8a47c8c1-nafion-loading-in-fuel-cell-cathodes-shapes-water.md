Researchers tested how much Nafion ionomer in proton exchange membrane fuel cell cathode layers affects water movement and buildup. Nafion content directly influenced where water accumulated and how it moved through the porous catalyst layer structure.

Water management is critical to PEMFC performance. Too much water blocks reactant access; too little dries the membrane. The cathode is where oxygen reduction produces water, making it the accumulation hotspot. Catalyst layer design controls this balance.

The finding suggests cathode composition tuning offers a lever for controlling water distribution without major architectural changes. This matters for improving cell efficiency and durability through optimised hydration at the reaction site.
