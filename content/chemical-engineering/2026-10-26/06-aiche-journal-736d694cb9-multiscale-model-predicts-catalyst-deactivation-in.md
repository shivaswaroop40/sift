Researchers built a spatiotemporal model linking particle-level chemistry and heat transfer to reactor performance for mercury-free acetylene hydrochlorination catalysts. The model predicts deactivation from lab to pilot scale by resolving non-spherical catalyst geometry and uneven active site loss within particles.

Thermal management is critical. Hotspots form where internal heat generation outpaces external cooling, driving catalyst deactivation. Understanding this mismatch helps engineers design reactors with better temperature control for exothermic processes.

Non-uniform deactivation within particles actually slows hotspot movement through the bed and extends catalyst life. The modular framework lets engineers test detailed sub-models and optimise fixed-bed reactor design.
