Researchers used CO2 radical anion to attack per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS). The mechanism transfers one electron at a time to break C-F bonds, releasing fluoride. Rate constants and transformation kinetics varied with PFAS structure.

PFAS compounds resist breakdown in soil and water, bioaccumulate, and persist for decades. A working defluorination route matters for remediation and treatment. Single electron pathways offer an alternative to current methods that often require extreme conditions.

The study maps how structure affects reactivity, suggesting selectivity in targeting specific PFAS classes over others. Understanding these kinetics helps design more efficient or selective treatment processes.
