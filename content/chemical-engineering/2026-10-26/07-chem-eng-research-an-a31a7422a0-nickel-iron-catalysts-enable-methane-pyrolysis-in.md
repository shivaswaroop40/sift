Researchers tested nickel-iron catalysts for methane pyrolysis in a bubbling fluidized bed reactor, combining experiments with hydrodynamic and reaction modeling. The work explores how to break methane into hydrogen and solid carbon without combustion, using a reactor type common in industrial practice.

For process design, fluidized beds offer good heat transfer and scalability. A validated model linking fluid flow with reaction kinetics helps predict performance at larger scales. Ni-Fe catalysts are cheaper than noble metals, important for economic viability of hydrogen production via pyrolysis rather than steam reforming.
