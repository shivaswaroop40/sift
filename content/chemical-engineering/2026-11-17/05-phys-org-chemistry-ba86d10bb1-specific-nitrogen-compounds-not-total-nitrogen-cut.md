Researchers at KRICT and KOREATECH added 13 nitrogen-, sulfur-, chlorine- and oxygen-containing impurities one at a time to real waste plastic pyrolysis oil. They then measured light-olefin yields over the zeolite catalyst from their 2024 circulating fluidized-bed process. Sulfur, chlorine and oxygen compounds had relatively minor effects. Certain nitrogen compounds cut performance significantly. 2-Methylpyridine had a pronounced effect, while benzonitrile had a small one.

The result suggests that total nitrogen content is a poor guide to feedstock quality. The chemical form matters. A machine-learning potential was used to compare adsorption of nitrogen compounds and hydrocarbons on the active sites. Some nitrogen species occupy the sites first, leaving fewer for cracking. Molecular size and the spatial environment inside the catalyst also played a part.

Higher nitrogen concentrations caused greater losses, and a higher reaction temperature reduced the effect. Catalysts that had lost activity recovered their initial performance after regeneration to remove coke. The authors say this supports continuously regenerated fluidized-bed operation. The source gives no yield figures, and the work used one catalyst and one base oil, so transfer to other feedstocks is not shown.
