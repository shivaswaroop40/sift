A paper in Chemical Engineering Journal examines support-amine matching in resin-supported solid amine adsorbents for CO2 capture. It centres on two features of the resin, its pore structure and its skeleton, and the roles each plays in whether a support and an amine pair well. The title frames the work as deciphering those matching rules.

Solid amine adsorbents are a common route to CO2 capture, and performance hinges on the pairing of amine and support. A poorly matched pair can block pores or leave amine sites inaccessible to the gas, and can lose capacity over adsorption cycles. Rules for matching amines to resin supports would let designers choose supports rationally rather than by trial and error.
