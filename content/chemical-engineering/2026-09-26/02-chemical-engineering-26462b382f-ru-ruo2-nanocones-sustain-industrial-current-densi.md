Researchers report Ru-RuO2 nanocone catalysts that couple a tip-enhanced local electric field with surface oxygen vacancies to drive the hydrogen evolution reaction at industrial current densities. The cone geometry concentrates the field at the apex, while the vacancies tune adsorption energetics on the active sites. Stability tests under high-rate operating conditions form part of the reported evaluation.

For process engineers, the claim is a non-platinum group route to heavy-duty water electrolysis catalysts. Industrial-current-density performance, typically above 200 mA cm-2, is the benchmark for sizing electrolyser stacks. A ruthenium-based formulation is cheaper than iridium or platinum but must still survive prolonged operation, so the durability data points are the key item to check.

The paper attributes the activity to a synergy between geometric field enhancement and vacancy-induced electronic modification, rather than to one effect alone. Quantitative rates, overpotentials, and hours of operation are not given in the supplied text and would need to be checked against the full article before design use.

The source provided is essentially the title and topic only, so specific numbers and operating conditions are not available here.
