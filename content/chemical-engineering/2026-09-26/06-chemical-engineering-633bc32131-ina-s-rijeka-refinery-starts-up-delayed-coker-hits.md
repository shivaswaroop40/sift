INA has started up the new delayed coker unit (DCU) at its Rijeka refinery in Croatia, feeding in feedstock on 1 September 2026 and reaching about 70% of design capacity within weeks. All key products have been made at the required quality, and the unit is operating continuously.

For a process engineer, the relevant detail is the residue-to-diesel uplift: the same crude slate is expected to yield up to 30% more diesel, and the refinery can now take heavier crudes. The DCU also removes the need to import vacuum gas oil, which simplifies the feed envelope and adds sourcing flexibility.

The DCU is the centrepiece of a Rijeka Refinery Upgrade Project costing close to EUR 700 million, with INA and MOL Group together spending EUR 1.3 billion over 12 years on refining and logistics. Stable operation is targeted by the end of 2026, followed by final performance testing.

Other upgrades in flight at the site include a green hydrogen production plant under construction and a completed steam back-pressure turbine installation, indicating broader energy-transition work tied to the refinery complex.
