The paper reports an acid-free route to valorise alkaline coal fly ash using hydrogen peroxide, which simultaneously solubilises molybdenum and upgrades the residual sodium carbonate. Peroxo species reshape the ionic environment so that Mo is selectively recovered while sodium carbonate gains value through the process.

This matters because conventional fly ash treatment uses strong acids, generating wastewater and loss of alkali. A peroxide route sidesteps acid use and keeps the loop closed, lowering reagent cost and effluent load for operators handling alkaline ashes from coal or waste combustion.

The authors describe a synergistic effect between Mo recovery and sodium carbonate enhancement, although the paper is an abstract and quantitative yields, purity figures, and scale-up data are not visible in the supplied text.
