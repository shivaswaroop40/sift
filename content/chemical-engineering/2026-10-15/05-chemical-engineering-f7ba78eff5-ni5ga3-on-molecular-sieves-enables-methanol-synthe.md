Researchers tested Ni₅Ga₃ intermetallic catalysts on either molecular sieve or activated carbon supports for methanol synthesis from biomass-derived syngas at atmospheric pressure. The molecular sieve support gave superior performance compared to activated carbon.

For process design, this matters because ambient-pressure methanol synthesis reduces capital and operating costs versus high-pressure routes. Better catalytic activity at milder conditions improves economics of converting biomass syngas to fuel or chemical feedstock.

The study signals that support choice—not just active phase composition—controls performance in this reaction. Molecular sieves likely offer different pore structure, surface chemistry, or metal-support interaction than carbon.
