Membrane reactors that remove water in-situ during CO2 hydrogenation to methanol improve conversion and selectivity. The study assesses how water removal affects equilibrium, kinetics, and reactor performance across different operating conditions and membrane types.

For process engineers designing methanol synthesis from CO2, this work quantifies the trade-offs between water removal rate, product yield, and energy demand. Understanding these effects helps optimise reactor design when shifting from fossil fuels to captured carbon feedstocks.
