Researchers found that in 316L stainless steel welds exposed to chloride, the location of pitting corrosion shifts with temperature. At lower temperatures, pitting initiates in the heat-affected zone; at higher temperatures, it shifts to the weld metal itself. This matters for biomanufacturing systems where both thermal cycles and chloride exposure occur.

The shift in preferential pitting site affects maintenance and design strategies. Engineers must account for different failure modes across the temperature range their equipment experiences. Knowing where corrosion will initiate—and how this changes with operating temperature—allows better material selection and protective measures tailored to specific process conditions.
