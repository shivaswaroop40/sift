Researchers optimized methanol synthesis systems that pair renewable hydrogen with direct air capture (DAC) of CO2. The study examined trade-offs between system size, hydrogen source costs, and the energy needed to extract CO2 from ambient air. Modular plant designs were evaluated across multiple performance criteria rather than a single objective.

For process engineers, this matters because methanol synthesis depends critically on both hydrogen feedstock and CO2 supply. The work maps how to configure these systems at different scales, helping design decisions when renewable hydrogen is available but DAC remains costly. The approach helps identify which operating points minimise total cost or emissions.
