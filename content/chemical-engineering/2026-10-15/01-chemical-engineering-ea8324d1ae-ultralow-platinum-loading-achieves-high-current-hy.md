Researchers developed a catalyst using electronic modulation and surface confinement to reduce platinum loading while maintaining performance in hydrogen evolution from seawater under alkaline conditions at high current density. The work addresses platinum scarcity by achieving competitive hydrogen production rates with minimal noble metal content.

For process engineers designing electrochemical water splitting systems, platinum cost and availability are major constraints. Lower loadings without performance loss translate directly to reduced capital and operating costs in deployed electrolysers, particularly for seawater splitting where conventional freshwater sources are limited.

The approach combines electronic modulation of the platinum surface with confinement strategies to enhance catalytic activity. This combination compensates for reduced metal loading by improving intrinsic activity per atom.
