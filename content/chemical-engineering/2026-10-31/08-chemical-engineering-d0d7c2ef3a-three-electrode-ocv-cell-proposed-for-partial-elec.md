A paper in Chemical Engineering Journal reports a three-electrode open-circuit-voltage cell for sensing state of charge in flow batteries. The authors describe it as accurate and cost-effective, and say it works on a partial sample of the electrolyte rather than the full tank volume. The title is the only text available, so the sensing mechanism and any performance figures are not reported here.

SOC measurement is a standing problem in flow battery operation. Operators use it to balance electrolytes, avoid over-charge and over-discharge, and set pump rates. A device that reads SOC from a partial electrolyte sample at low cost would cut the instrumentation burden on commercial systems. Whether this design delivers that depends on details the title does not give.

Only the title of the paper is available. It gives no accuracy figures, no cost comparison, no response time, and no indication of which electrolyte chemistry was tested. The claim of accuracy and low cost is the authors' own, and the supporting evidence sits behind the paywall.
