A paper in Chemical Engineering Science sets out population-balance-based models for electrostatic crude oil dewatering. It develops both semi-empirical and machine learning forms of the model. The paper addresses asymptotic reduction of the population balance, identifiability of its parameters, and robustness across scales.

Electrostatic dewatering is a standard step in crude oil processing. Population balances are the natural framework for droplet coalescence in an electric field, but they are heavy to solve and hard to fit. Identifiability and scale transfer are the usual sticking points. A paper that reduces the model, examines parameter identifiability, and checks robustness across scales addresses those problems directly.
