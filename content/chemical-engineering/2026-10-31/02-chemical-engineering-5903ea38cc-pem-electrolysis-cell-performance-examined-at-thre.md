A paper in Chemical Engineering Journal examines polymer electrolyte membrane electrolysis cell performance at three levels of iridium loading. The work combines experiment with modelling. Iridium is the conventional anode catalyst for the oxygen evolution reaction in PEM water electrolysers, so loading level is a key design variable for these cells.

Iridium supply is limited and the metal is expensive, which makes anode loading a major cost driver for PEM electrolysers. Data linking cell performance to loading level, backed by a model, would help designers set catalyst budgets and predict how thin coatings behave. That trade-off sits at the centre of electrolyser scale-up for hydrogen production.
