A paper in Chemical Engineering Science addresses the apparent high-shear rheology of shear-thinning fluids, with turbulent pipe flow data as the source. The available text is the title alone. No abstract, method or results accompany it, so the specific approach and any validation cannot be reported here.

The topic matters in practice. Turbulent pipe flow exposes a shear-thinning fluid to shear rates that bench rheometers often struggle to reach, and those rates govern friction losses and scale-up. A route that recovers high-shear behaviour from pipe data itself would let engineers characterise fluids at the conditions that matter, rather than extrapolating a fitted model far beyond its measured range.
