Researchers made Pd catalysts by aerosol spray-pyrolysis with tunable dispersions of nanoparticles, clusters and single atoms, set by the Ti/Ce ratio in the support. Pd clusters on Ti-doped CeO2 hydrogenated cinnamaldehyde to phenylpropanal with 97.9% selectivity at 70°C and 1 MPa H2, with complete conversion in 0.5 h. Nanoparticles gave 56.1% selectivity, and single-atom-dominant catalysts favoured acetalization.

The finding matters for anyone running selective hydrogenation. C=C and C=O bonds in these aldehydes compete for activation, so steering the reaction to the saturated aldehyde is difficult. The catalyst held its performance over 10 recycling cycles, and the same cluster strategy worked for acrolein and crotonaldehyde. The authors also argue the spray-pyrolysis route is potentially scalable.

The mechanism work explains the selectivity pattern. Synchrotron XAFS, AC-STEM and DFT show that Pd clusters cleave H2 homolytically and adsorb the C=C bond strongly at -1.39 eV. Single-atom sites instead split H2 heterolytically, which promotes acid-catalysed acetalization. Metal dispersion, not just metal identity, therefore sets the product distribution.
