Researchers have developed a mass transfer model for CO2 absorption into phase-change solvents in packed columns. It rests on two-film theory and proposes a new overall mass-transfer coefficient, KGav, for absorbents that separate into two phases during absorption. Experiments examined mass transfer and phase separation across a range of operational parameters, and the model was validated against them. It cuts average absolute deviation from 10.6% to 8.3% versus existing models.

Phase-change absorbents appeal for their energy-saving potential, but they behave differently from traditional absorbents. CO2 absorption triggers phase separation, which changes viscosity and mass transfer behaviour. Existing mass transfer models lose accuracy on these systems. Anyone rating or sizing packed absorbers for such solvents needs a coefficient that accounts for the phase change, and this one also indicates when separation will occur.

The experiments showed that operational parameters significantly affect both mass transfer and phase separation. The abstract does not name the solvent, the packing, or the parameters varied, so the 8.3% figure cannot be tied to specific conditions from this text alone.
