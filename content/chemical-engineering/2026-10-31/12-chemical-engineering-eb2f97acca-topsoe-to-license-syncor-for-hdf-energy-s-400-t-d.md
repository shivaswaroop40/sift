Topsoe will supply the licence, process design, basic engineering, proprietary equipment and catalysts for HDF Energy's BaseBlue project in Texas. The plant uses Topsoe's SynCOR autothermal reforming to convert Permian Basin natural gas into about 400 metric tons of blue hydrogen per day. It is expected to capture more than 95% of the CO2 generated, around 1.1 million metric tons a year. HDF plans roughly 250 MW of power for data centers.

The deal marks a first for Topsoe. SynCOR has not previously been used to make blue hydrogen for a data-centre power project. The scheme pairs natural gas reforming and carbon capture with HDF's fuel-cell systems to deliver firm, low-carbon electricity onsite, behind the meter. HDF argues data centers need power that is both firm and low-carbon, and that most solutions offer only one.

The 95% capture rate and 1.1 million tons per year are project expectations, not measured results. The announcement gives no schedule or capital cost. HDF says it chose SynCOR because the technology integrates carbon capture in a proven and bankable way at the scale a power plant demands.
