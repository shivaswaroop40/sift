Rio Tinto has officially opened its Fénix 1B expansion and the new Sal de Vida lithium carbonate operation in Catamarca, Argentina, following a combined investment of about $1.4 billion. Both projects reached first production in the second quarter, ahead of plan, and are now ramping up. Together they will add 25,000 metric tons of annual lithium carbonate equivalent capacity once fully ramped.

The split matters for anyone tracking lithium supply. Fénix 1B adds 10,000 metric tons per year to a site that began in 1997 as Argentina's first commercial lithium operation, lifting its total capacity target to roughly 40,000 metric tons. Sal de Vida, about 20 kilometres northeast of Fénix, targets 15,000 metric tons per year. Both sit within Rio Tinto's Argentine portfolio across Catamarca, Jujuy and Salta, which also includes the Rincon project.

The openings advance Rio Tinto's stated target of 200,000 metric tons of annual lithium carbonate equivalent capacity by 2028. Around 70% of the roughly $4 billion in growth capital planned across its Argentine lithium operations is expected to be spent with local suppliers, which the company says is strengthening the country's supplier network.
