Researchers at Battelle have developed an immobilized-protein system that selectively binds and purifies rare-earth elements from complex mixtures containing impurities and radioactive contaminants. Calcium-binding peptides and proteins exploit the chemical similarity between rare-earth elements and calcium to achieve selectivity. The team identified six candidate proteins and demonstrated separation of difficult pairs like praseodymium/neodymium and samarium/europium.

This matters because conventional rare-earth separation relies on solvent extraction or chelating agents, generating hazardous waste with poor selectivity. The protein-based approach eliminates solvents, allows column reuse across many cycles in harsh conditions, and reduces operating costs and waste. A liter-scale column has produced gram quantities.

The work is being qualified with real-world mining residues through partnerships with Mountain Pass Materials and Rio Tinto, moving the technology closer to industrial deployment.
