Researchers embedded nickel into calcium oxide and added cerium oxide to create a single material that captures CO2 and converts it to syngas in one step. The material achieved 12.6 mmol g−1 CO2 capture and 58 mmol g−1 syngas yield, a 2.9-fold improvement over conventional materials used separately.

Integrated capture and conversion reduces the energy cost of CO2 utilization by eliminating intermediate transport steps. The material works isothermally at 650°C during calcium-looping dry reforming of methane, shortening the path carbonates must travel from capture site to catalytic site.

Spectroscopy and modelling show captured carbonates convert via a COOH-mediated pathway at the nickel–cerium–calcium interfaces, rather than migrating through the bulk. This mechanism lowers activation energy and resists sintering and coking, improving long-term stability.
