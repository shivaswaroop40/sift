Researchers have demonstrated an electrochemical method that converts waste polyethylene into graphite by dissolving the polymer in molten salts and applying electric current. The process operates at elevated temperature in a molten salt electrolyte, transforming the carbon backbone of the plastic into ordered graphite structure.

This matters to process engineers working on waste valorisation and circular economy pathways. Graphite is a valuable commodity used in batteries, refractories and other applications. Converting abundant plastic waste into a useful carbon product offers both a disposal route and a potential revenue stream.

The approach sidesteps traditional thermal or chemical degradation routes. Direct electrochemical conversion in molten salts allows selective carbon transformation whilst keeping the process contained and energy-efficient compared to conventional pyrolysis or gasification.
