Researchers built a data-driven model that corrects errors from low-resolution CFD simulations of mechanical flotation cells. The approach trains on high-fidelity data to predict accurate flow fields from coarse mesh results, cutting computation time while preserving accuracy.

Flotation separations require precise understanding of mixing and bubble dispersion. Engineers usually run expensive fine-mesh CFD to get this, or accept poor predictions from fast coarse models. This framework bridges the gap for process design.

The method trades higher training cost upfront for faster prediction on new geometries or operating conditions. Practitioners can run multiple design scenarios rapidly once the model is built.
