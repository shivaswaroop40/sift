Molecular simulation shows that CO2 displaces adsorbed methane from shale nanopores with variable efficiency depending on pore width, interfacial chemistry, and water presence. Displacement becomes less effective in narrower pores where CO2 adsorbs strongly, blocking methane release rather than promoting it.

This matters to enhanced gas recovery and CO2 storage in shale. Understanding when CO2 injection fails to mobilise trapped methane affects the economics and effectiveness of both methane extraction and permanent CO2 sequestration in tight formations.

Water at pore surfaces adds another complication: it can block adsorption sites, changing whether CO2 or methane dominates the pore space and reducing displacement efficiency further.
