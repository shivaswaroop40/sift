Cellulose-rich textile waste was converted to aromatic hydrocarbons via ZSM-5-catalyzed hydropyrolysis. Four real textile wastes yielded 41–56% aromatics with selectivity above 95%. Combined LCA and TEA showed 0.73 kg CO₂-eq per kg aromatics using gray hydrogen, 37% lower than fossil routes, and 0.59 kg CO₂-eq per kg with green hydrogen.

The process economics hinge on hydrogen. Gray hydrogen gave a minimum selling price of $0.68 per kg, matching commercial mixed aromatics. The study found that hydrogen source drives carbon footprint whilst hydrogen price drives production cost, making low-cost low-carbon hydrogen the critical variable for scaling textile-waste conversion.
