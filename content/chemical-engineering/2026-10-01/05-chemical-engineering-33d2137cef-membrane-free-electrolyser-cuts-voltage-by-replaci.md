Keele University researchers have demonstrated a laboratory-scale alkaline electrolyser that eliminates the membrane separator by replacing the conventional anode reaction. Instead of oxidising water to oxygen, the system oxidises 5-hydroxymethylfurfural to 2,5-furandicarboxylic acid, a plastic precursor.

This substitution reduces the electrical voltage required because FDCA formation demands less energy than oxygen evolution. The approach removes the explosion risk of hydrogen-oxygen mixtures, simplifying the cell design and lowering capital cost.

Nickel-based catalysts are used, which are abundant. The researchers propose future adaptations to process biomass-derived or waste-derived feedstocks alongside hydrogen generation.
