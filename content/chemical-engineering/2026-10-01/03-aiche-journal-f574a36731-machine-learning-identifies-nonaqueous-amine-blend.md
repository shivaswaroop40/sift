Researchers used machine learning to screen nonaqueous absorbent mixtures and identified a 2-(butylamino)ethanol and tetramethylethylenediamine blend. This system achieved 0.668 mol CO2 per mol amine desorption capacity with regeneration energy of 1.38 GJ per tonne CO2, substantially lower than aqueous monoethanolamine.

For process design, this matters because regeneration energy dominates CO2 capture operating costs. The nonaqueous system also showed low corrosivity and good cyclic stability, reducing materials compatibility issues and maintenance downtime that plague conventional aqueous amine plants.

The mechanism involves butylamino ethanol capturing CO2 whilst tetramethylethylenediamine accepts protons and destabilises intermediates, lowering the reaction heat. This structural understanding enables rational design of further solvent blends rather than empirical trial.
