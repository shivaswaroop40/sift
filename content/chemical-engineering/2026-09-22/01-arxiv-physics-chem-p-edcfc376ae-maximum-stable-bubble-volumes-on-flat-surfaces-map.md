The paper calculates the maximum stable volume of bubbles on circular cavities and flat surfaces as a function of contact radius, contact angle, and capillary length. Equilibrium bubble shapes are obtained by solving the Young-Laplace equation with a shooting method, and the authors identify three distinct detachment pathways: necking at the cavity, sideways instability, and spreading followed by necking on the surrounding surface.

The predicted detachment volumes are tested against experimental data from electrolytic, boiling, and carbonated bubble systems, as well as pendant drops, and show good agreement. By symmetry the same framework also applies to pendant drops, extending prior work that handled these cases separately.

For practitioners designing bubble-generating equipment, the result gives a single predictive tool covering electrolysis cells, boilers, and carbonation hardware, with the contact angle and contact radius of the nucleation site as the main inputs. The three-mode classification matters because the active mechanism changes which geometric parameter most strongly controls detachment size.
