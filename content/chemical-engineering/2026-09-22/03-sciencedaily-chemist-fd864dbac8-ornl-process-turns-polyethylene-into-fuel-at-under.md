Researchers at Oak Ridge National Laboratory have converted polyethylene into gasoline- and diesel-like fuels using molten aluminium chloride salts at temperatures below 200 degrees Celsius, achieving around 60% gasoline yield.

The salt acts as both reaction medium and catalyst, removing the requirement for an external hydrogen supply, organic solvents, noble-metal catalysts or a chemical initiator. Conventional pyrolysis routes typically operate between 450 and 500 degrees Celsius.

Mechanistic work using soft X-ray spectroscopy, neutron scattering, isotopic labelling and NMR showed that charged aluminium atoms form acidic sites that cleave the polymer chains. Simpler chains favoured gasoline-range products, while more complex chains gave diesel-range hydrocarbons.

A patent has been filed and the work is published in the Journal of the American Chemical Society, but the result remains at laboratory scale. Scale-up feasibility, salt recovery, feedstock variability and energy input have not yet been quantified.
