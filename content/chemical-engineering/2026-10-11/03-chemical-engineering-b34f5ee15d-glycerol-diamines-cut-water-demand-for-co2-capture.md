Researchers synthesised tetraether diamines from glycerol and tested them as CO2 capture solvents that work with minimal water. The compounds showed structure-property relationships that govern CO2 absorption and rich-phase viscosity, the key constraint in solvent regeneration.

Water-lean solvents reduce the energy cost of removing absorbed CO2 by stripping, since less water must be evaporated. Viscosity in the CO2-loaded state directly affects regeneration efficiency and reboiler duty. For process design and solvent selection, understanding how molecular structure tunes both absorption and viscosity is essential.

The study connects chemical structure to measurable performance metrics needed for process simulation and scale-up. Glycerol-derived solvents offer a bio-based alternative to conventional polyamines, with tunable properties for different capture conditions.
