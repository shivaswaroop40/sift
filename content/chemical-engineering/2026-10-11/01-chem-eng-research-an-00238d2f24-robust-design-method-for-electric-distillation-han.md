Researchers developed a sizing method for electrified distillation columns that accounts for uncertain electricity prices and feed composition. The approach uses Wasserstein distributionally robust optimisation to hedge against worst-case scenarios within a bounded uncertainty set. The method produces equipment dimensions that perform well across plausible cost and feedstock variations without requiring precise probability distributions.

For process engineers designing separation systems with variable power costs, this matters because it reduces the risk of oversizing or undersizing equipment when electricity prices and feed properties cannot be forecast accurately. The method avoids the need to guess a single probabilistic distribution, which is often hard to justify in practice. It trades computational complexity against decision confidence when uncertainties are substantial.
