Princeton physicists have identified a more efficient path to fusion ignition by reversing the traditional sequence: heating plasma before increasing its density rather than the reverse. Their refined calculations map a route through the Cordey saddle, a low-energy passage in the ignition landscape, that could substantially reduce the external energy required to reach self-sustaining fusion.

This matters because fusion reactors today follow approaches that require climbing over the energy requirements mountain directly. The new sequence sidesteps the highest peaks. The work extends the 70-year-old Lawson criterion by incorporating four additional factors: helium ash buildup, plasma contamination, synchrotron radiation losses, and temperature-dependent heat escape.

The findings apply to both tokamaks and stellarators. In idealised plasma, the Cordey saddle sits at Q equals 5, but real reactors shift this point higher due to impurities and magnetic field effects. Accounting for these details together, rather than separately, yields a more practical roadmap for commercial fusion pilot plants.
