Researchers fractionated corn stover using benzyl alcohol and 5-sulfosalicylic acid as solvent and catalyst. The method suppressed unwanted lignin condensation reactions that typically occur during biomass processing, allowing better recovery of usable lignin and carbohydrate streams.

For process engineers working on biomass valorization, this matters because lignin condensation during fractionation usually degrades lignin quality and reduces downstream product yields. Controlling condensation improves both the economics and product range of biorefinery operations.

The work demonstrates that solvent selection and acid strength together govern depolymerisation rates and side-reaction pathways. This informs solvent choice for other reactive fractionation schemes in lignocellulose processing.
