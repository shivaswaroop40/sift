Researchers tested sorption-enhanced reverse water gas shift (SErWGS) with potassium-doped zeolites at high pressure. They compared LTA and FAU zeolite types and found potassium additives reduced unwanted methanol formation during the reaction, improving selectivity toward the desired CO conversion.

The reverse water gas shift converts CO₂ and H₂ into CO and water, a key step in syngas production for chemicals and fuels. Methanol is a problematic byproduct that reduces yield and complicates downstream separation. Zeolite sorbents with dopants offer a route to suppress this side reaction while capturing CO₂.
