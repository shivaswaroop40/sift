Researchers at Tokyo Institute of Science developed a high-entropy oxide catalyst combining lanthanum, samarium, europium, gadolinium and dysprosium. At 525°C it initiated C2 hydrocarbon formation, and at 600°C achieved 12.3% C2 yield while maintaining performance unchanged over 240 hours. The deactivation rate was 1.0 mmol C2 per gram per hour squared, 25 times lower than single-element oxide catalysts.

Oxidative coupling of methane (OCM) typically requires 800°C and suffers rapid deactivation, limiting industrial use. The lower operating temperature reduces energy input. Sustained stability over 240 hours without loss of active sites addresses a critical barrier to commercialisation.

The team linked low-temperature activity to moderate surface basicity tuned by the average ionic radius of the lanthanoid elements (0.957 Ångströms for HEO-2). Excessively strong basic sites promote unwanted overoxidation of ethane and ethylene products, so moderate basicity proves more selective.
