A paper in the Chemical Engineering Journal examines CO2 flowback and residual trapping at the pore scale in subsurface CO2 energy storage systems. The available text is limited to the title, so no methods, results or quantitative findings can be reported here. The subject is the behaviour of CO2 as it is injected into, and withdrawn from, porous subsurface formations used for energy storage.

Residual trapping determines how much injected CO2 stays immobilised in the rock rather than returning to the surface during withdrawal cycles. Flowback affects the recoverable fraction and the composition of the produced stream. For engineers designing subsurface CO2 storage or energy storage schemes, pore-scale mechanisms set limits on cycle efficiency, storage security and the sizing of surface separation equipment.
