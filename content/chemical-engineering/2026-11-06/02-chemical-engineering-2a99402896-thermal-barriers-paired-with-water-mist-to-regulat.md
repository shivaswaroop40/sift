A paper in Chemical Engineering Journal examines thermal runaway propagation in lithium-ion batteries. It studies how several hazards evolve as runaway spreads from cell to cell, and looks at controlling them with two measures used together. Thermal barriers sit between cells. Water mist supplies cooling. The authors frame the coupled approach as a way to regulate the multi-hazard chain during propagation.

Propagation between cells is the central process safety problem in battery packs and storage systems. Barriers are meant to slow heat transfer from a failed cell to its neighbours. Water mist cools and can suppress combustion of vented gases. Acting on different hazard modes at once is the rationale for coupling them.
