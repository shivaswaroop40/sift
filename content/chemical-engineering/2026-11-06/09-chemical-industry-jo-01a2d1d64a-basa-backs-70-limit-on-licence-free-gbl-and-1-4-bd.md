BASA, the trade body for the £1.7 billion UK and Irish adhesives and sealants sector, has backed Option 1 in the Home Office consultation on gamma-butyrolactone (GBL) and 1,4-butanediol (1,4-BD). Option 1 sets the licence-free limit at 70%. Products containing no more than 70% of either substance, mixed with other organic substances, would stay outside licensing. High-purity material would need a Home Office controlled drugs licence. Today anyone can trade both chemicals without a licence if they are not meant for human consumption.

The controls respond to misuse. Both substances convert to GHB in the body and have been used in drug-facilitated sexual assault, robbery and murder. High-purity product has been sold online as cleaning products. For industry, 1,4-BD builds polyurethane adhesives and sealants, and GBL is a solvent in some formulations and removers. Finished products typically contain far less than 70%, so they would stay outside licensing.

BASA welcomed the decision not to remove the exemption altogether, which would have controlled everyday products. It asked the government to clarify that "organic solvent" includes the polyols, resins and other organic ingredients in reactive formulations, and to confirm that unreacted traces of 1,4-BD in cured products are not caught. It also sought a proportionate licensing route for buyers of high-purity raw material, and a transition of at least 12 to 18 months with guidance ready from the start. The government response is expected by 8 February 2027.
