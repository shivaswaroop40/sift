A study in Chemical Engineering Research and Design applies hydrophobic eutectic solvents to rare earth metal extraction using two-phase systems, with the experimental design guided by Central Composite Design methodology. The work develops the formulation and operating window for these solvent systems.

For separations engineers, the approach offers an alternative to conventional aqueous extractants for rare earths, which are energy intensive and produce large waste streams. Hydrophobic eutectic solvents are tunable and can reduce aqueous effluent if the extraction performance holds up at scale.

The paper reports design-of-experiments guidance rather than a full process flowsheet, so the practical extraction efficiencies, phase ratios, and metal selectivities would need to be checked before any process commitment.
