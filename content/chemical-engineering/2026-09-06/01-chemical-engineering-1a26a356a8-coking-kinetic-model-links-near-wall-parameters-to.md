The paper presents a coking kinetic model for supercritical hydrocarbon fuel pyrolysis in regenerative cooling channels, using near-wall parameters. Pyrolysis under supercritical conditions generates coke deposits on channel walls, degrading cooling performance.

For engineers designing regenerative cooling systems, coking rates determine allowable residence times, channel geometry, and fuel mass flow. A near-wall resolved model provides more realistic deposition predictions than bulk-fluid kinetics, supporting safer and more efficient thermal management of high heat-flux propulsion and process equipment.
