A paper in Chemical Engineering Research and Design presents an integrated environmental and technoeconomic assessment of chemical heat pumps based on isopropanol. The systems are evaluated for output temperatures between 150°C and 255°C.

The source text gives only the title. It does not report results, costs, emissions figures, efficiencies or the process configuration, so the paper's conclusions cannot be stated here.

The temperature range covers much of the industrial process heat demand that is hard to serve with conventional heat pumps. An assessment that combines cost and environmental impact is the kind of evidence needed to judge whether the concept merits pilot work. Whether it does depends on figures in the full paper.
