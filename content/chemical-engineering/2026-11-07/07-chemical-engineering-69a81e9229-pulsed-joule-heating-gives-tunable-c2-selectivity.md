A study in Chemical Engineering Journal reports tunable C2 selectivity in the direct coupling of methane and CO2 under non-equilibrium pulsed Joule heating. The title indicates that selectivity towards two-carbon products can be adjusted, and that the reaction runs in a pulsed, non-equilibrium thermal regime rather than under steady isothermal conditions.

This sits at the intersection of electrified process heat and one-step natural gas upgrading. Direct CH4/CO2 coupling to C2 species would convert two greenhouse-gas feeds into higher hydrocarbons without a syngas loop. Selectivity is the hard part in methane activation, because conditions hot enough to activate C-H bonds also drive over-oxidation and coking. A pulsed heating route that tunes C2 output speaks directly to that problem.

The available text is the title alone. No selectivity values, conversion data, catalyst identity, pulse duration or duty cycle, or energy efficiency figures appear in it. The size and origin of the claimed selectivity tuning cannot be judged from this source, so treat the claim as unquantified until the full paper is read.
