A techno-economic study has assessed adding wet carbonation of recycled concrete fines to an electrified-calciner cement plant at industrial scale. Benchmarks cover a stand-alone electrified calciner, direct use of non-carbonated fines in the blend, and MEA capture. The carbonation step needs 3.35 MJ of electricity per kg of CO2. Under the European energy mix the plant avoids 61.7% of CO2, slightly above electrification alone, at an avoidance cost of 200.7 €/tCO2 versus 217.4 €/tCO2 for MEA capture.

This matters for anyone choosing between cement decarbonisation routes. Most of the avoidance comes from clinker substitution rather than CO2 uptake during carbonation, so the carbonation reaction is not the main driver. The cost edge over MEA capture comes from higher cement production and lower capital spend, not from cheaper CO2 removal.

The 61.7% avoidance rate is computed for the European energy mix. Both the calciner and the carbonation step run on electricity, so the balance between these routes will differ on other grids, and power prices will move the cost ranking.
