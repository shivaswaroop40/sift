Researchers developed a charge-compensated electrochemical system using LiMn₂O₄ for lithium capture and reduced graphene oxide with bismuth oxybromine for bromide capture. In produced water from Zigong field, lithium and bromide adsorption reached 21.3 and 20.9 mg/g respectively, yielding 0.5 mmol of lithium bromide per cycle.

Oilfield produced water contains dilute lithium and bromide mixed with interfering ions, making conventional recovery uneconomical. This dual-electrode approach sidesteps single-ion separation limits by capturing both species simultaneously while enriching them as lithium bromide, a higher-value product.

The bismuth oxybromine nanostructure, stabilised by reduced graphene oxide, improves bromide accessibility and electron transport. This engineering avoids the bottleneck of sequential separation steps typical in current practices.
