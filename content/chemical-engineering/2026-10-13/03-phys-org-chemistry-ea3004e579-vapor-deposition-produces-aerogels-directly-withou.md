Researchers at National Taiwan University have developed a solvent-free method for making aerogels using vapor sublimation and deposition polymerization. Ice in frozen templates sublimates under low pressure while polymer molecules deposit to form interconnected porous networks. The resulting materials achieve densities as low as 0.004 g/cm³, porosities of 90–99 per cent and surface areas exceeding 1,300 m²/g.

This eliminates the complex drying steps and environmental burden of conventional sol-gel aerogel manufacturing. For process engineers, the method offers tighter control over pore structure and density without solvent recovery challenges. The platform also avoids harmful byproducts from liquid-based routes.

The technique successfully incorporates functional components including metal-organic frameworks, living cells and gases within the same aerogel structure. Living stem cells remained biologically active during fabrication, and aerogels demonstrated controlled gas storage and release, suggesting applications in tissue engineering, gas delivery and sensing.
