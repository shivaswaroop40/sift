Between October 2025 and September 2026, malicious emails mentioning RFQ increased 12-fold, according to Fortra researcher Cadence Riddle. Nearly 73% led to credential-theft pages. Attackers send short, credible messages to sales and procurement staff asking for quotations, then follow up with links to fake login portals after the target replies.

Sales and procurement teams cannot avoid opening links from unknown senders as part of their work. A compromised business email account exposes customer data, invoices, payment instructions and project details. The attacker can then impersonate the legitimate sender to phish coworkers, customers and suppliers.

The 664 different sender domains used mean blocklists alone cannot stop these attacks. Riddle notes that organisations should treat RFQ processes as a targeted abuse vector with clear controls and owners to prevent routine business inquiries from becoming account-compromise incidents.
