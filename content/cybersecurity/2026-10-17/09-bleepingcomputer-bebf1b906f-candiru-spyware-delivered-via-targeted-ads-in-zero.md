Candiru spyware infected at least 45 devices at Al Jazeera Media Network through malicious JavaScript embedded in targeted online advertisements between 2024 and 2025. The attack, called AdFection, required no user interaction. Israeli firm Discover-Tech purchased ad placements across mainstream networks and used a NOAS server to identify and target eligible devices, delivering spyware payload only to selected targets.

This demonstrates a new distribution channel for mercenary spyware that bypasses traditional delivery methods. Attackers can now weaponise the mainstream advertising ecosystem at scale, with Discover-Tech winning 442 million ad auctions and reaching hundreds of thousands to millions of users between March and June 2026. The technique leaves minimal forensic traces compared to exploit-based delivery.

Citizen Lab estimates ads with attacker code appeared across 75,373 mobile apps and 120,768 websites, though actual infection numbers remain unclear. Major ad platforms including Google, IAB Europe, Beeswax, and Vungle subsequently removed offending accounts and blocked associated domains after receiving Citizen Lab's report.
