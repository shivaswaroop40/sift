An advisory from the FBI, US Defense Department and agencies in Japan, Australia and Germany details a North Korean operation called WaterPlum that infected at least 30,000 devices in 100 countries between December 2025 and July 2026, stealing credentials or funds from roughly 7,000 cryptocurrency wallets and netting more than $10.5 million.

The attackers posed as AI or blockchain recruiters on social media and freelance portals, targeting web designers, engineers and crypto specialists with malicious interview files. Japanese police found BeaverTail, InvisibleFerret, OtterCookie, OtterCandy and StoatWaffle malware on victims, alongside infostealers and remote management tools for persistence.

The campaign feeds directly into North Korea's IT worker scheme. Stolen identities and maintained device access let operatives apply for jobs at foreign tech firms, sometimes using AI face-swapping and text-to-speech tools during interviews. Japanese officials disrupted one laptop farm run by a Japanese national and traced hundreds of millions of yen sent abroad.

Past incidents caused real downstream damage: one blockchain firm has previously confirmed a major crypto theft traced to this tactic, and other North Korean IT hires have extorted employers or defaced company sites after being hired.
