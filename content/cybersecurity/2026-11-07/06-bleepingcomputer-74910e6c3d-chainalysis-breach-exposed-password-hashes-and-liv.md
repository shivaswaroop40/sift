Chainalysis says an attack exposed names, usernames, password hashes and live tokens for all of its customers. The company said the credential exposure happened in an on-path attack, more commonly known as a man-in-the-middle attack, and that the attackers used AI tools with what it described as offensive agentic techniques. Its initial disclosure last Friday said the impact was limited to non-sensitive identity metadata. Later updates widened the scope.

The company found no evidence that attackers accessed investigative graph data or manipulated its blockchain attribution repositories. It has nonetheless taken the login portal offline, along with Reactor, Data Solutions, the KYT user interface and API, and address screening products, after further abnormal activity. Chainalysis customers include law enforcement, government agencies, financial institutions and crypto businesses, so credential exposure carries significant risk beyond a typical vendor breach.

Recovery requires customers to send trusted IP addresses for an allowlist before the login portal returns. Once access is granted, every user must reset passwords and rotate API keys before registering transactions. The disclosure follows other crypto incidents, including Binance's revelation that North Korean hackers stole roughly $6.34 billion after infecting an employee's laptop during a fake job interview.
