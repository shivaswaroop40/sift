Google Project Zero is replacing its "90+30" disclosure policy with shorter fixed deadlines. The 30-day extension was removed on 13 November. The deadline drops to 60 days on 2 February 2027 and to 30 days on 3 January 2028. Once it expires, details will be published even if no fix exists.

Project Zero says AI has sped up vulnerability discovery and exploitation. It also says attackers can reverse-engineer patches as soon as they ship and build working exploits more easily. The policy puts more pressure on vendors to triage, fix and deliver patches faster.

Vendors had warned that going straight to 30 days could expose users to unfixed flaws while remediation processes improve. They also feared maintainers would prioritise Project Zero reports over other, possibly more severe, issues. Google chose a phased approach in response.

Exceptions exist. Earlier disclosure applies if a flaw is exploited in the wild, if others have published details, or if the vendor asks. Google may also withhold details for longer, citing Spectre and Meltdown, which stayed undisclosed beyond 120 days.
