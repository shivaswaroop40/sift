A botnet of more than 1.3 million Android devices, mostly TV set-top boxes, lost control of its servers in late October in a takedown led by Google. Google says it is highly confident that Videostrong, the device maker, intentionally put a backdoor in the boxes and sold access to criminals. Those criminals used the boxes for ad fraud and to pass other people's internet traffic through owners' homes.

The origin matters for defenders. On Google's account the backdoor shipped with the device rather than arriving through later infection, so patching, malware scans and user behaviour offer no protection. Network monitoring of what such devices send out becomes the main control. The relay use also means strangers' traffic exits from the owner's IP address, consuming their bandwidth and tying activity they never initiated to their connection.

Google's attribution is hedged. The company says it is highly confident rather than certain, so treat the claim against Videostrong as a strong allegation. The report also leaves open what happens to the affected boxes. A backdoor shipped by the vendor may leave replacement as the only fix.
