The European Commission confirmed that a cyberattack, first detected on 22 October, involved a publicly available AI tool. A spokesperson called it an "AI-generated cyber campaign" but did not name the tool or the attackers' motive. POLITICO reported that CERT-EU identified ARTEX, an open-source AI hacking platform.

The attack reportedly disrupted personnel systems for more than 10 days, including payroll, leave requests, medical reimbursements and translation services. The Commission took several services offline as a protective measure and notified affected individuals. It found no evidence of a broad staff data breach and knows of no ransom demand. The investigation remains open.

The FBI warned last week that criminals and state-sponsored groups were using ARTEX widely, with some intrusions going from initial targeting to privileged access in under three hours. The framework was released in July and uses commercial AI models to automate vulnerability discovery and exploitation.

SentinelOne's Shaun Williams said the point is that such tools let attackers exploit internet-facing devices faster, more cheaply and with less skill. This is the Commission's third disclosed incident this year, after Ivanti and cloud infrastructure breaches. Officials have not linked them.
