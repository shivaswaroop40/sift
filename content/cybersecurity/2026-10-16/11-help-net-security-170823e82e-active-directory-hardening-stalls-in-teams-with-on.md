A cohort of 27 municipalities hardened Windows Server and Active Directory together over 12 weeks, completing tasks that individual teams had deferred for years. The group met weekly, completed shared homework, tested changes in a lab environment, and measured progress from start to finish. The demand exceeded expectations: the programme planned for 10 to 15 participants.

Understaffed teams do not lack awareness of Active Directory weaknesses. They lack structured time and collective motivation to execute known fixes. A single administrator managing dozens of competing tasks cannot prioritise hardening without external pressure and shared goals. The cohort model provided both.

The cohort addressed high-risk changes such as SMB signing, LDAP signing and Kerberos hardening by first establishing logging and auditing across all sites, then reviewing results before proceeding. Each municipality tested changes in a lab before production rollout and planned rollbacks individually based on their infrastructure.
