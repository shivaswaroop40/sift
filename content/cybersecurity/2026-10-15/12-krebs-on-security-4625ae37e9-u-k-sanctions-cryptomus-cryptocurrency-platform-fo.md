The U.K. has issued economic sanctions against Cryptomus, a cryptocurrency payment processor that repeatedly changed its name to evade restrictions. Since 2022, the platform processed over $7 billion in transactions tied to ransomware, drugs, fraud, and cybercrime, helping Russia bypass financial sanctions. It relaunched as Heleket after Canada imposed a CAD $176 million fine in 2024.

For defenders, Cryptomus represents a critical gap in financial infrastructure controls. The platform processed payments for over 900 illicit counterparties in a single month and accepted transactions linked to terrorism, trafficking, and child exploitation. A Canadian fine proved unenforceable because fines do not stop cryptocurrency processors; sanctions do.

The platform derived legitimacy from fake regulatory bodies using disputed territory in Western Sahara as their jurisdiction. Cryptomus's parent company was nominally owned by a 25-year-old Ukrainian woman who also headed the supposed regulator, exposing how payment processors obscure true control and ownership.
