Ledger discovered that devices sold by Southeast Asian reseller CryptoBilis contained hardware implants designed to intercept and transmit the 24-word recovery phrases needed to access cryptocurrency wallets. The implant uses a provisioned eSIM to exfiltrate seed phrases over LTE to an unauthorised party, bypassing Ledger's integrity checks because it operates independently of the Secure Element.

Supply-chain compromise of hardware wallets creates direct financial loss for users without detection by existing security mechanisms. A defender storing keys in these devices cannot prevent theft at the manufacturing or distribution stage. Ledger's investigation began after multiple users reported cryptocurrency losses, and modified devices have now been found at other retailers including Metapod and MediaMarkt across Asia and Europe.

Ledger has ordered all distributors in Southeast Asia and Hong Kong to halt sales and remove products from shelves pending investigation. The company states its direct sales channel and infrastructure remain safe. Competitor Trezor has preemptively announced plans to reduce its reseller network and tighten supply-chain controls, ending partnerships with existing resellers by January 1, 2027.
