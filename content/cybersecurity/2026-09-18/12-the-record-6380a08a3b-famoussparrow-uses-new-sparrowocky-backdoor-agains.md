ESET says a Chinese government-backed group tracked as FamousSparrow has been running an espionage campaign against government agencies in Guatemala, Honduras, Puerto Rico, Panama, Venezuela, Peru and Argentina since at least August 2025. The attacks deliver a previously undocumented Windows backdoor named SparroWocky, which incorporates open-source code and enables file exfiltration, screenshot capture and collection of host details such as IP address and usernames.

ESET researcher Alexandre Côté Cyr called the regional focus rare, noting that long-running Chinese operations usually spread across multiple regions. The firm linked the targeting to US pressure on Chinese investments in Latin America, suggesting the campaign is intended to help Beijing anticipate local government reactions. In Panama, one victim is involved in a port-operations dispute that has drawn direct US attention.

The malware embeds the opening stanza of Lewis Carroll's poem Jabberwocky in early samples, and is built with anti-analysis features that suggest deep familiarity with internal Windows behaviour. FamousSparrow has been publicly tied to Salt Typhoon, the group US authorities say breached the Treasury Department, major US telecoms and a congressional email platform.
