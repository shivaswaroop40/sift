The US government has disrupted FaceStealer, a North Korean social engineering campaign that stole more than $400 million in cryptocurrency. It ran for 18 months before the US seized the cloud infrastructure behind it. Targets were people in crypto and Web3, plus open-source developers. Authorities found more than 900 webcam videos secretly recorded from tech workers.

Attackers hijacked Telegram, LinkedIn, Slack and Discord accounts and used them to invite victims to video meetings. The calls showed recorded footage of people the target knew, taken from earlier victims. A fake technical error then pushed the target to run a supposed fix. That fix installed malware taking crypto data, meeting platform credentials, contacts, messaging apps and webcam video.

The FBI says attackers sometimes waited months, or more than a year, before acting on a compromised person, likely to let their holdings grow. ArticWolf attributed the campaign to BlueNoroff and counted 677 targets in 58 countries, mostly founders and senior executives. Other lures included fake PDF viewer updates, identity verification prompts and security audits.

The FBI described an attempt on the maintainer of a popular JavaScript library with tens of millions of weekly downloads, using a fake Teams meeting. Quick detection stopped it. The FBI confirmed further attempts against npm and Rust crate developers. Its advice is to join meetings through official apps, verify unexpected invites independently, keep webcams off and never run scripts offered as fixes.
