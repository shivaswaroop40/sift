Attackers stole an estimated $133 million from Crédit Mutuel Arkéa by using the bank's own account to wire money to other banks. The French cooperative group, which holds €211.5 billion in assets and serves more than 5.8 million customers, disclosed the attack on 4 November. It said the attackers reached the bank directly through the platform of an unnamed service provider, not through customer accounts.

The case shows how a foothold at one service provider can reach a bank's own payment account and initiate wires. It also shows how quickly the freeze window closes once funds enter crypto. ZachXBT traced withdrawals of USDC and USDT from centralized exchanges on 1 November, swapped into ether, moved through instant exchanges, and partly converted to Monero. He said delayed disclosure let over $30 million be laundered that EU law enforcement could have frozen.

Several details remain disputed. The bank declined to confirm the €118 million figure and would only validate the order of magnitude. It denies that entry came through Fortuneo, its online bank. Journalist Grégory Raymond reported that fraudsters set beneficiary names themselves and used exchange accounts opened with falsified identities, so each transfer passed sender-to-owner name checks. ZachXBT suspects losses exceed the on-chain total once OTC activity is counted. Part of the funds has been blocked.
