Unauthorised parties accessed names, addresses, and personal identification numbers for 8.8 million people in Denmark's Central Person Register on 5 October. They exploited a private company's legitimate access credentials to the national population database.

For platform engineers running identity systems, this shows how lawful access credentials create persistent risk. A compromised account with read-only permissions to a national registry exposed data at scale without triggering immediate detection.

The breach affected both living and deceased individuals, expanding the surface for identity fraud and enabling targeting of specific population segments through enriched data.
