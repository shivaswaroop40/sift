Pistachio, a security awareness vendor, analysed 2.47 million simulated phishing emails sent to more than 123,000 employees across 1,200 organisations between June 2025 and May 2026. Thirty percent of tech development and IT staff clicked at least once. Nearly 20% of construction and real estate employees leaked credentials. Financial services were the most resilient sector.

The findings challenge the common practice of judging phishing resistance by click rate alone. A click without credential submission is low risk. The report argues that click, leak and report behaviours must be tracked together. In-house tests that focus only on clicks can give a misleading picture of an organisation's true phishing risk.

On first simulations, more users reported than clicked, but 1.57% still leaked credentials. That means a 500-employee company can expect about eight people to hand over login details. Click and leak rates rose through the first six months of the programme before declining. By the end, users reported suspicious emails nearly twice as often as they clicked them.

The study covers multiple countries but includes no geographic analysis, so it cannot show whether region affects susceptibility. The vendor suggests the results should inform any phishing simulation programme, whether run in-house or by a third party.
