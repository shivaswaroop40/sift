Anthropic said it disrupted a campaign by a Russian state-sponsored group, tracked as GTG-20006, that used Claude to rebuild malware after detection. The group developed an AI-assisted workflow to get ahead of the detection curve. Anthropic announced the finding on Thursday. The company attributes the operation to a cyber espionage group it calls GTG-20006, where GTG stands for Generative Threat Group.

The abuse matters because Claude let the group iterate on malware quickly after defenders detected it. AI-assisted development lowers the cost of adapting tools, so signature-based defences become less reliable. Engineers should expect state actors to use commercial AI models for malware maintenance and plan detection around behaviour, not just file hashes.

Anthropic's account gives few technical details about the workflow or the malware involved. The claim rests on the company's own investigation, and the original report is truncated. Treat the attribution as credible but incomplete. No indicators of compromise or tool names were included in the available text.
