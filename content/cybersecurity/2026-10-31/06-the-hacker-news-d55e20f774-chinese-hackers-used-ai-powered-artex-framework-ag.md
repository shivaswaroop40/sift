CrowdStrike has attributed attacks using a Go-based, AI-powered penetration testing framework called ARTEX to a China-based targeted intrusion adversary it tracks as Vapor Panda. The actor was previously tracked as VexatiousTemper. The framework was used against high-value targets simultaneously, and the campaign is reported to have targeted more than 200 organizations worldwide.

The use of an AI-powered penetration testing framework by a state-sponsored group shows offensive automation has reached targeted intrusion campaigns. ARTEX was aimed at high-value targets simultaneously, which suggests coordinated and parallel operations across victims. Defenders tracking Chinese intrusion sets should add the Vapor Panda and ARTEX names to their watchlists and hunt for Go-based tooling.

The available reporting is thin on mechanics. The excerpt does not cover how the AI components of ARTEX work or what initial access vectors were used. It also omits which sectors the victims span and when the activity was first identified. Treat the attribution and the tool name as CrowdStrike claims pending fuller technical detail.
