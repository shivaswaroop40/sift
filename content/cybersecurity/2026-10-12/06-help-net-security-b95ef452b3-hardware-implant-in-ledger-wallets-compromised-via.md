Ledger confirmed that devices sold through CryptoBilis, a reseller in Southeast Asia, contained unauthorized hardware implants. The implant monitors the wallet's screen display to capture the seed phrase, then transmits it to attackers. On-chain researchers estimate losses exceed $93 million from coordinated drains on October 9, 2026.

The implant design allows attackers to recover wallets on any device without accessing the original hardware. Attackers can collect multiple seed phrases quietly and execute mass theft simultaneously. The circuit board is difficult to detect because it sits under the display padding and the device passes Ledger's security checks.

Ledger's own systems and direct sales channels were not compromised, but the incident demonstrates that authorised resellers present supply chain risk. Bitquery analysis suggests compromised devices may extend back to June, beyond Ledger's initial 90-day guidance. Users are advised to move assets to new devices with fresh seed phrases and purchase only from Ledger directly.
