Hackers posed as a government agency and sent fraudulent legal requests to Revolut Bank UAB for roughly five months, extracting personal and financial data on around 680 high-profile customers, many of them cryptocurrency holders. The attackers first compromised a government employee email through an infostealer infection and used it to issue the requests, which Revolut processed without questioning their legitimacy.

The same group also claims to have stolen 147GB of data from an Italian law enforcement agency via a compromised pec.interno.it mailbox. A threat actor using the moniker 'IAmNotAVillain' has publicly demanded a $3 million ransom, though Revolut says it has not received any direct contact. The Italian police have opened an investigation.

Hudson Rock reports over 300 compromised credentials associated with pec.interno.it and assesses that the attacker likely bought existing infostealer logs rather than infecting employees directly. The case shows how social engineering combined with stolen credentials can bypass legal request workflows at financial institutions.
