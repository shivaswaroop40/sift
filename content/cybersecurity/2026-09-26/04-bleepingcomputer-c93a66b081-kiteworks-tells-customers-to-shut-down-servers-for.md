Kiteworks has told customers worldwide to take their secure file-transfer servers offline for a six-hour window on 26 September, citing credible threat intelligence from federal authorities about an imminent attack. In Central Europe the window runs from 04:00 to 10:00, with equivalent local slots from AEST to PDT. The company told BleepingComputer the advisory is precautionary and that no compromise of its systems has been confirmed.

Heise reports that Kiteworks support described the shutdown as protection against potential zero-day attacks. The company itself has not confirmed any unpatched vulnerability and says all known flaws are fixed in version 9.5.1. The warning was originally circulated by email from CISO Frank Balonis.

The product is used by governments, financial institutions and enterprises to move sensitive documents, making it a high-value target. BleepingComputer notes that Clop has previously hit file-transfer platforms including Accellion FTA, GoAnywhere MFT, SolarWinds Serv-U, Cleo and MOVEit Transfer. The US State Department is offering a $10 million reward for information linking Clop to a foreign government.
