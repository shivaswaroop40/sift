French cooperative bank Crédit Mutuel Arkéa says a cyberattack let an intruder use the bank's own account to transact with other banking institutions and convert funds to cryptocurrency. Blockchain investigator ZachXBT estimated the loss at up to 118 million euros, about $133 million, withdrawn as USDC and USDT stablecoins. The bank suspended certain systems and disclosed the incident on 4 November, saying customer assets were unaffected.

The access vector is the detail practitioners should note. The attacker reached the bank's network directly through a service provider's platform, though Arkéa ruled out Fortuneo. Disclosure timing also had a measurable cost. ZachXBT said the lack of public disclosure let the attacker launder $30 million by 3 November, funds that EU law enforcement would otherwise have frozen.

The bank has not confirmed the exact figure. It said it could only validate the order of magnitude of the 118 million euro estimate, and that some funds were blocked. It ruled out human error and called the incident a sophisticated attack. Judicial authorities and police are investigating the origin and method. Arkéa also warned customers of increased phishing risk.
